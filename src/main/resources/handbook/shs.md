## THE FOUNDER

### SAINT HANNIBAL MARY DI FRANCIA

St. Hannibal was born to a knight and a noblewoman in Messina, Italy on July 5, 1851. He was orphaned at a tender age. As a young man, he had been given the intuition of the necessity of Prayer for Vocations. When he became a priest (March 16, 1878), he committed himself to the moral and spiritual redemption of the poorest area in his city, giving up all his inherited wealth to the service of God through his manifold works of charity among the poor children. He is known as the "Apostle of Prayer for Vocations" because he focused all his works on the promotion of the ROGATE (Latin for "pray"), following the evangelical call of our Lord as told by two evangelists: Matthew (9:37-38) and Luke (10:2) — "THE HARVEST IS GREAT, BUT THE WORKERS ARE FEW, PRAY THEREFORE THE LORD OF THE HARVEST TO SEND GOOD WORKERS INTO HIS HARVEST." Pondering upon the meaning and message of the sacred words, he saw in them the ineffable solution for the salvation of the many poor children whom he learned to love with tender and divine affection. Fr. Hannibal founded two religious congregations, the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and the Daughters of Divine Zeal, to help him carry out and propagate the prayer for vocations.

Fr. Hannibal passed to eternity on June 1, 1927, but his spirit remains alive in the hearts and minds of all his spiritual sons and daughters. He was beatified on October 7, 1990 during the Synod of Bishops and was canonized on May 16, 2004 in Rome, Italy by His Holiness, Pope John Paul II.

### FOREWORD

A wholesome educational formation is possible only through cooperative efforts extended by both the home and the school. Consistency of purpose and manner in realizing this formation is best achieved through policies and regulations.

This handbook is intended for the purpose of this realization. It aims to summarize in an orderly and readable manner all the salient information and the policies and regulations of Rogationist College. Through this, it is expected that:

1. Students will acquaint themselves with the objectives, policies, procedures and regulations of Rogationist College;
2. Administrators and teachers will acquire working knowledge in guiding the education community to realize the vision-mission and goals of the school as they promote the observance of its policies and regulations; and
3. Parents will understand their role in promoting and realizing the educational and institutional objectives of the school.

The policies, rules and regulations in this handbook are intended to promote an atmosphere that is conducive to the students' personal growth founded on Christ and His Gospel to pursue God's call, in obedience to the Rogate, and in accordance with the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

## Part I — HISTORY

Like any family or community, Rogationist College (RC) has a rich historical heritage which every RC student should be proud of. To appreciate more deeply the roots of an RC community, an overview of the history of the congregation of the Rogationists, and more particularly that of RC, is presented here.

### THE ROGATIONIST CONGREGATION

The Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus (RCJ) is a religious congregation of priests and brothers founded by St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia in 1887 in Messina, Italy. Both the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and its sister congregation, the Daughters of Divine Zeal, are devoted to prayer and promotion of vocations in the Church, and to the education of young people, particularly the poor and disadvantaged. The legacy of St. Hannibal is kept alive by these congregations present today in Europe (Italy, Spain, Albania and Poland); in South America (Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay); in North America (USA and Mexico); in Africa (Rwanda and Cameroun); in Oceania (Papua New Guinea); and in Asia (the Philippines, India, Korea, Vietnam, and Indonesia).

### THE ROGATIONIST PHILIPPINE DELEGATION

The Rogationists established their first missionary activities in the Philippines in November 1976 to promote a culture of vocation and to assist the poor especially the children. Their long term strategy was to prepare young Filipinos to be agents of social growth and transformation driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

Milestones in the development of the Rogationist Philippine Delegation:

- **October 1977** — opening of the first Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park (Parañaque);
- **November 1979** — construction of a parish church in Multinational Village (Parañaque);
- **June 1980** — profession of the religious vows of the first three Filipino Rogationist novices;
- **December 1981** — opening of a second and larger Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park;
- **June 1987** — opening of St. Anthony's Boys Village and Rogationist Academy (High School) in Silang, Cavite;
- **April 1990** — opening of the Rogationist Formation Center for the Visayan Region in Cebu City;
- **January 1999** — start of the new mission in Toril, Davao City with Fr. Hannibal Children's Home, a charitable institution for children;
- **December 1999** — inauguration of the Fr. Di Francia Center of Studies for Religious Formation in Theology in Parañaque City;
- **January 2002** — opening of the Oasis of Prayer and Retreat House in Silang, Cavite;

The Official RC logo represents man's total human and Christian formation, youth **DEUS ET PROXIMUS**. The Latin words *Deus et Proximus* (God and neighbor) stand for the direction of Rogationist College education.

## Part II — LIFE PURPOSE, GUIDING PHILOSOPHY, VISION AND MISSION

### THE LIFE PURPOSE

Rogationist College exists primarily to enable the youth, especially the poor and underprivileged assisted by the St. Anthony's Boys Village Foundation, to discover in their life the project of God by becoming good Christians and responsible members of society through Secondary, Technical and Tertiary education anchored on the formative philosophy of the Rogationists.

### GUIDING PHILOSOPHY

Rogationist College (RC) in St. Anthony's Boys Village in Silang, Cavite is a Catholic co-educational community committed to the formation of the whole human person.

RC, in its mission to help the youth, bases its educative system on a philosophy of education which is attentive to the instances of human life. It is both personalistic and vocational in nature. RC forms its students to the proper understanding and dynamics of self. The process brings them to a serene enjoyment of life according to the dictum of responsible acting and sharing.

The vocational nature of the RC system facilitates its students in the acquisition of a realistic life perspective. It is a view which is sensitive to life's origin and destiny. RC believes and professes that every person is called not only to live but also to fulfill life as God's gift.

RC is convinced that real education is not just academic education. It espouses a style of forming that opens avenues to the proper exercise of love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence.

The educative formation of RC is always considered an encounter between the teachers and their students. In doing the personalistic and vocational approach to education, RC individuates three interrelated moments of the meeting, which ends in the mature choices for the life orientation of its students.

The first moment is **ORGANIZATION**. Here, the organizing student is met by a teacher who is a guide. In this moment, the students are initiated to find and form the most important instances of their life and then cherish them.

The second moment is **IDENTIFICATION**. Here teachers are models. St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia, founder of the Rogationist, wants all institutions of the Rogationists to educate through good examples. RC believes that for students to grow in the right direction, the edifying life of teachers is very important.

The third moment is **DECISION**. Self-fulfillment is also self-determination. At the end of the formative process, students of RC should have already acquired a certain degree of independence. Knowing better to distinguish right from wrong, they should be capable of pursuing not just terrestrial but also personal and possibly celestial good.

RC's philosophy of education, following the teachings of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia and inspired by the Gospel of the Christ of the Rogate, goes therefore to the roots of the human person.

### VISION

Rogationist College is a prime Catholic educational community dedicated to the formation of culturally competent and socially responsible persons driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

### MISSION

Rogationist College commits itself to:

1. Form its members toward Love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence;
2. Offer a balanced and technically-oriented curriculum, as well as excellent instruction, learning environment and facilities;
3. Prepare well-rounded graduates who are agents of social growth and transformation; and
4. Promote a culture of vocation and good workers who, like St. Hannibal, will be espousing the cause of the poor especially the children.

## Part III — GENERAL POLICIES AND GUIDELINES

*"The home shall fully support the school in the implementation of the total school program — curricular and co-curricular — toward the proper physical, social, intellectual and moral development of the child." — Article 76, The Child and Youth Welfare Code (Presidential Decree No. 603, s. 1974)*

### GENERAL POLICIES

1. The rules and regulations of RC as stated in this Handbook are intended to enhance the life in school of an RC student as he relates and interacts with the members of the school community. As such, it is understood that they are mutual good role models of each other.
2. It is the duty and responsibility of the parents and/or guardians to keep track of the progress of their children/wards in school. The parents and/or guardians are encouraged to confer with the school authorities at their own volition or whenever calls for conference are issued to them. This is to enhance the harmonious working relationship between the school and the home for the welfare of the student.
3. The enrollment of a student in RC is taken as an expressed willingness on his/her part, and consequently of his/her parents and/or guardians, to abide by all rules and regulations of the school. Thus, the student and his/her parents and/or guardians are committed to the strict observance of the same.
4. RC reserves the right to admit, readmit, refuse admission or exclude a student or drop the name of an erring student from the school rolls on the basis of his/her academic performance, conduct or attendance.
5. RC acknowledges its obligations to exercise reasonable supervision over the conduct of the student and to cultivate the best potentialities of the heart and mind of the student. Its role in "loco parentis" is the exercise of substitute parental authority over students in the school and in school-related activities (Civil Code, Art. 349).
6. RC, as a Catholic school, strongly respects and adheres to the practice of holy sacrament (matrimony) and does not tolerate sexual indiscretions. Violation of this policy would merit the student appropriate disciplinary action, including but not limited to, requiring the student to go on leave or being excluded or dropped from the school rolls.
7. RC expects its students to uphold the standards of responsibility and dignity. When a student of RC does not measure up to these standards, he/she will be excluded or dropped from the school rolls after due process.
8. Students, either as individuals or as a group, must not use the name and seal of the school without official authorization.
9. Students who are using social media in their respective homes are not to make inappropriate comments (including in private messages) about the school personnel or other students.
10. Parents or guardians may consult or confer with teachers and instructors provided they secure appointments with the Department Head (Principal, Dean or TED Head) two days before the actual date of appointment. Concerns and/or complaints must be written and directly addressed to proper school authority rather than posting them on social media — whether on their own pages, in closed groups (e.g. groups set up for school parents to communicate with each other) or on the school's pages. Complaints posted on social media will not be entertained by the school.
11. Parents, guardians or visitors are not allowed to go to the classrooms. Messages and allowance for the students must be coursed through the Guidance Office.
12. Students may be called out of the classrooms provided an approved written permission/call slip is issued by either of the following:
    - Rector/Vice-Rector
    - Director for Academic Affairs (DAA)
    - Director for Student Services (DSS)
    - Director for Spiritual Affairs (DSA)
    - Principal/Dean/Technical Education Department Head (TED Head)
    - Vice Principal/Vice Dean
    - Guidance Counselor/Facilitator
    - Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer
    - Health Services Coordinator/Nurse
13. As a Catholic School, with Rogationist Formation as the core of the curriculum, RC requires all students to attend and pass all the requirements of Christian Living Education or Religious Education subjects. Students also need to attend masses and/or similar activities and complete spiritual recollections and retreats scheduled by the institution. In addition, parents of High School students are required to follow the agreement (to attend/participate in parents' formation programs, PTA assemblies, etc.) signed during the enrolment of their children.
14. The "No ID, no entry" policy is strictly implemented. The school ID is required for the admittance of students and personnel to the school premises. For parents or other visitors, a visitor's ID is issued by the guard in exchange for a valid identification card, which is returned after a legitimate business transaction or visit inside the campus. Visitors will be charged for lost visitor's ID.
15. High School students will stay inside the campus from the moment of their arrival in the morning until their dismissal time in the afternoon. They cannot leave the school premises unless they have written permission from their parents and/or they have secured a gate pass from the Discipline Office duly noted by the Homeroom Adviser and approved by the Principal. In the absence of both, the Prefect of Discipline signs the gate pass. In case of sickness/medical concern, the School Nurse will issue the gate pass.
16. The privacy of certain areas in the campus should be respected by the students. The areas where students are not allowed to stay or loiter are:
    16.1. Religious community's residence and interns' dormitories
    16.2. Refectory, kitchen, lavatory, and carpentry shop
    16.3. All laboratories except during scheduled classes and/or when permitted by the Principal/Dean/TED Head
    16.4. Fr. Vitale Administration Building, except for official business
    16.5. Faculty rooms
    16.6. High School classrooms and corridors during recess, lunch and after dismissal time, unless under the supervision of a teacher or with specific permission from the Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer.
17. Everyone is enjoined to keep the campus clean at all times. Thus, littering is strictly prohibited. There are waste bins around the campus for proper waste disposal.
18. Everybody should respect, maintain and safeguard the school physical plant. Any damages must be reported immediately to the Office of the General Services.
19. Students are obliged to use English as a medium of communication.
20. Everyone is expected to be courteous and respectful at all times especially to visitors and treat others in a positive and considerate manner.
21. Students must exhibit proper behavior at all times in all places. When the behavior of a student adversely affects the integrity and objectives of the school, he will be subjected to disciplinary action.
22. Using the school website, official Facebook page or RC ID System, announcements are made from time to time to keep the students abreast of school concerns and procedures. Students should relay to their parents all announcements which concern them. Likewise, the school gives out regular and/or timely communications to parents. The parents are earnestly requested to acknowledge every communication by filling-up the reply/return slips/parental consents and returning them to the school through their children.
23. Parents/Students are discouraged from posting photos or videos that include other children at the school on their personal social media accounts.
24. In case of misunderstanding between or among the students, parents of the concerned students should not do any drastic action or in any way harass the other concerned students. Such occurrences will be settled according to the policies and guidelines stipulated in this handbook. In addition to this, cooperation of all stakeholders is needed to maintain and promote trust, respect, and dignity of all students, parents/guardians, teachers, and administrators of the school.
25. An activity permit signed by the teacher-in-charge, noted by the Student Affairs Coordinator, and approved by the Principal/Dean/TED Head must be secured by students at least two days before any school activity/practice during non-class days. This form, obtainable from the Student Affairs Coordinator, should be signed by their parents and returned to the teacher-in-charge a day before the approved activity/practice.
26. Students who wish to participate in any outside activity as representatives of the school should secure approval from the school administration.
27. In cases of emergency, or for valid reasons, high school students may bring cellular phones or other gadgets provided there is a letter of permission from the parents. These gadgets will be surrendered to the subject teacher, adviser or discipline officer right after the morning assembly and will be retrieved after classes in the afternoon. College students, however, may bring their cellular phones but should refrain from using these inside the classrooms, library or during school hours and liturgical celebrations.
28. High School students permitted to bring cellphone, laptop, netbook, camera or any gadget/device must deposit the gadget/device to the Office for Student Discipline or to the subject teacher during the first period. The gadget/device must be used only during the class activity and only by its owner. Unauthorized/improper use of a gadget/device may result in disciplinary action and confiscation of the gadget/device. A confiscated item shall be released only to the student's parent/legal guardian.
29. Taking into consideration the educational system of RC and the promotion of an environment that is conducive to learning, the Discipline Officer, together with the subject teacher or adviser, may conduct inspection as the need arises.
30. Parents or students who wish to request school forms or documents such as ESC Certificates, Form 137/SF10, Certificate of Enrolment, Certificate of Grades, Certification of Good Moral Character and/or Rank, and Recommendation Letter, must apply for these at least a week before actual claim. Formal request letters must be submitted to the concerned offices with proof of requirements attached. Online (e-mail) or phone call requests may be accommodated provided proof of requirement is presented upon claiming. A minimal amount will be charged.
31. In order to be allowed to enter the school premises, students' vehicles/school service vehicles must have the official school sticker which may be secured at the Accounting Office.
32. The Office for Student Discipline will inform parents/guardians in writing about a student's case and may require the parents to come personally for proper disposition of the said case.
33. Parents visiting the school are required to wear modest/proper clothes.
34. No students or visitors, including parents or guardians, shall be allowed inside the school building and the premises after the last class period has ended. (DepEd Order No. 11 s. 2011)
35. A student shall be responsible for informing his parents/guardians of the following:
    35.1. School policies and guidelines stated in this handbook.
    35.2. Student's academic standing and the possible consequences of his excessive absences, dropping, failures or gross misbehavior.

### SUSPENSION OF CLASSES

Classes are suspended in the following situations:

1. Typhoon warning signal is raised by PAGASA in the area of Cavite. Depending on signal numbers declared at 10:00 pm and 4:30 am of the following day, classes in appropriate levels for the whole day are deemed automatically cancelled/suspended: signal no. 3 automatically suspends classes in the tertiary level; signal no. 2 automatically suspends classes in the secondary level. (DepEd Order No. 43 series 2012)
2. Natural calamities or disturbances compromising the safety of the students;
3. Rector's announcement;
4. Cancellation of classes by DepEd, TESDA or CHED;
5. National, Provincial, and Municipal proclamations.

*N.B. Afternoon classes in the appropriate levels in areas with signal numbers declared at 11:00 am that day will be cancelled or suspended at 2:00 pm to ensure the safety of the students and to provide ample time for the school authorities to contact school service providers and/or the parents. In addition, during heavy rains affecting the health or safety of their children, parents have the discretion not to send their children to school.*

### FEES AND PAYMENTS

1. Tuition fees and other charges are prescribed by the school authorities. These charges are posted in the Bulletin of Information.
2. Parents and students enter into agreement with the school regarding the mode of payment of tuition and other fees.
3. It is the obligation of the parents and students to update themselves with regard to the status of their accounts by consulting the Finance and Administration Department.
4. Examination permit for the JHS & SHS [is signed by the Registrar] and by the Cashier for the College/TED students. This should be signed by the Cashier two weeks before each major examination. It is recommended that accounts be settled at least two weeks before the scheduled examination period to avoid inconvenience and delay.
5. Tuition fees and other charges for transferring or withdrawing students shall be governed by the following provisions:

   When a student registers in a school, it is understood that he/she is enrolling for the entire school year for secondary courses (DepEd Order No. 88 series 2010: Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, Section 119, Rules for Registration). Thus, a student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 20% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.

   For College students (Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education, Section 100, Tuition Charges): A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged 25% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 50% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes.

The Senior High School Department is an excellent provider of integrated human formation leading towards learners' tertiary, work and entrepreneurial preparedness, aligning its thrust to Catholic education and the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

### GUIDELINES AND CONDITIONS ON MONITORING/PROBATION STATUS

RC education is for all students who can maintain its standards of academic excellence and exemplary conduct. It requires a high degree of achievement from its students. A student who does not meet the standards set by the school is put on a probationary status — a period designed to ascertain the ability of a student to abide by the academic and conduct standards of the school.

**I. Academic Probation Status**

Academic Probation status is a formal warning that carries with it a condition that improvement in academic achievement is necessary for readmission and/or promotion.

1.1. Given to a student who has shown below average academic performance in the previous semester/school year; imposed on a student whose final general average is below 82%.
1.2. Student on Academic Probation must maintain good grades (no failing marks) and a final general average of at least 82% to change status to regular student. Failure to improve or abide by academic standards within the school year means non-readmission for the following school year.
1.3. A student placed on Academic Probation for an incoming school year shall not be eligible for any higher elective and/or appointive position in any varsity team, club or organization, subject to discretion of the Rector.

**II. Conduct Monitoring Status**

1. A student is placed on Conduct Monitoring after incurring a series of minor offenses during a semester/school year.
2. If a student commits other minor offenses or a major offense during the monitoring period, status is elevated to conduct probation for the current semester/school year.
3. Conduct Monitoring status may be lifted the following semester/school year if the student shows satisfactory improvement.

**III. Conduct Probation Status**

1. A student is placed on Conduct Probation after committing a major offense.
2. A student placed on Conduct Probation for an incoming school year shall not be eligible for any elective and/or appointive position in any varsity team, club or organization, subject to discretion of the Rector.
3. If a student on Conduct Probation commits a series of minor offenses or a major offense, he/she may be dismissed the following school year or dismissed immediately.
4. Conduct Probation status may be lifted after a semester/school year with remarkable improvement in behavior.

**II. Academic Policies**

1. **Classroom Assessment** — Integral part of curriculum implementation, tracking and measuring learners' progress and informing learners/parents/guardians of progress. RC Senior High School Department uses two kinds of assessment:
   1.1. **Formative Assessment (Assessment as Learning)** — conducted during/after every lesson to check attainment of instructional objectives; results recorded but not part of the grade except when satisfactory; used to improve teaching strategies.
   1.2. **Summative Assessment (Assessment of Learning)** — conducted at the end of every lesson phase to judge proficiency level attained. Components:
      1.2.1. **Written Work** — long quizzes, unit/long tests, essays, written reports, and other written output.
      1.2.2. **Performance Task** — skills demonstration, group presentations, oral work, multimedia presentations, research projects.
      1.2.3. **Quarterly Assessment** — measures student learning at the end of the quarter (objective tests, performance-based assessment, or combination).

   1.3. **Weight of the Components for SHS**
      1.3.1. Core Subjects: Written Works 25%, Performance Tasks 50%, Quarterly Assessment 25%
      1.3.2. Applied and Specialized Subjects (except those in 1.3.3): Written Works 25%, Performance Tasks 45%, Quarterly Assessment 30%
      1.3.3. Work Immersion/Business Enterprise Simulation/Capstone Project/Culminating Activity: Written Works 35%, Performance Tasks 40%, Quarterly Assessment 25%
      1.3.4. Elective Subjects: Written Works 20%, Performance Tasks 60%, Quarterly Assessment 20%

2. **Grading System** — RC uses a standards- and competency-based grading system per curriculum guides/maps mandated in the K to 12 Basic Education Program. A student's grade is based on the weighted raw score of summative assessments. Minimum passing raw score is 60, transmuted to 75 in the report card. Lowest mark that can appear is 60 for Quarterly Grades and Final Grades. At the end of each semester, grades are computed by averaging the midterm grade and final grade, considered the subject grade.

   | Descriptor | Grading Scale | Remarks |
   |---|---|---|
   | Outstanding | 90-100 | Passed |
   | Very Satisfactory | 85-89 | Passed |
   | Satisfactory | 80-84 | Passed |
   | Fairly Satisfactory | 75-79 | Passed |
   | Did Not Meet Expectations | Below 75 | Failed |

3. **Benefits of Students Who Join Academic Competitions outside the School**
   3.1. For the Related Subject: 3.1.1. exempted from all activities (seat works, quizzes, assignments, laboratory activities) except performance task and summative examinations during trainings/competition. 3.1.2. Student's grade for the affected term must not be lower than 82%.
   3.2. For Other Subjects: 3.2.1. Exempted from seat works and quizzes; must take missed activities on schedule set by the subject teacher with ample time to comply. 3.2.2. Grade for the affected quarter must not be lower than the previous grade, provided requirements are complied with.

### DISCIPLINARY MEASURES

Disciplinary actions are corrective and formative measures given to students who fail to comply with the policies and rules of the school. The primary objective is to guide and accompany erring students to the right behavior, considering the guidelines on proper student decorum.

Disciplinary actions range from verbal warning to exclusion depending on the gravity of the offense. The Discipline Officer directly monitors students' conduct; all offenses are reported to Discipline Officers for verification and processing.

Prompt reporting of misbehavior is encouraged, covering incidents from the first day of classes of the current year until final report card distribution the following year, to maximize intervention, reformation and/or imposition of appropriate disciplinary action.

**I. Disciplinary Action**

**1. For Minor Offenses**

| Frequency | Corresponding Disciplinary Action |
|---|---|
| 1st Minor offense | Verbal warning |
| 2nd Minor offense | Written warning |
| 3rd Minor offense | Community Service (2-hour service/work specified by the Discipline Officer in agreement with student/parent/guardian) |
| 4th Minor offense & succeeding | Working Suspension (constitutes a major offense; done in agreement with parent/guardian) |

*N.B. In some cases, erring students may not be allowed to attend/participate in a major school activity such as Acquaintance Party, Celestial Night, Christmas Party, Sports Festival, etc.*

**2. For Major Offenses**

2.1. **Working Suspension** — suspended student reports to work in school offices or canteen for one day or more depending on gravity/nature of offense. Corresponding conduct grade applies (70%-79%).
2.2. **Preventive Suspension** — A student under investigation of a serious/grave case may be preventively suspended if evidence of guilt is strong and continued stay poses a distraction or risk; also protects the student from acts of vindictiveness.
2.3. **Exclusion** — The school reserves the right to exclude/dismiss or drop a student who fails to give positive evidence of earnestness of purpose and active cooperation, or whose conduct makes the student a liability/nuisance.

*N.B. In some major disciplinary offenses, erring students may not be allowed to attend/participate in a major school activity such as Acquaintance Party, Celestial Night, Christmas Party, Educational Tour, Sports Festival, Completion Rite, etc.*

**II. Monitoring and Probation**

1. **Conduct Monitoring** — Period during which a student who has incurred a series of minor offenses during a semester/school year is closely monitored by Guidance Counselors/Facilitators and Discipline Officers for one semester/school year. May be lifted the following period with satisfactory improvement; elevated to Conduct Probation for other minor offenses or a major offense during monitoring. An erring student may also be placed under Conduct Monitoring for a special case (neither minor nor major).

2. **Conduct Probation** — A student who has committed a major offense; closely followed up by the Adviser, Guidance Counselor and Discipline Officers for one school year. Series of minor offenses or a major offense may lead to exclusion the following year or immediate exclusion. May be lifted the following year if no offense is made and behavior improves.

**III. Conference with Subject Teachers**

Done if a student commits an academic-related offense. After a series of conferences, a third academic-related offense (inside or outside classroom) may be referred to the Office for Student Discipline.

**IV. Due Process**

The school recognizes students' right to due process through conference and dialogue before implementing disciplinary action, with due inquiry/verification and a chance for parties to present their sides.

The Discipline Officer is authorized to call students involved, who write a narrative account of the incident. Parents/legal guardians (as students are of minor age) are invited to be informed and answer charges. In major offenses, the Director for Student Services may convene a discipline committee (DSS, Director for Spiritual Affairs, Principal, Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer, and Guidance Counselor) to assess the case. A final conference is then facilitated with the student and parents. A student may also be referred to the Guidance Office if counseling is deemed more appropriate.

## APPENDIX A — MANUAL OF REGULATIONS FOR PRIVATE SCHOOLS

**Section 66. Tuition Charges**

A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged ten (10%) percent of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the first week of classes, or twenty percent if within the second week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the second week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to a justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.

The foregoing tuition fee schedules are based on the premise that a student who enrolls in a school stays enrolled for the entire school year or semester, regardless of subsequent transfer or withdrawal.

Section 66, MRPS and Section 104, TVET Manual protect schools which accept tuition fee payments on installment (down payment upon enrollment, balance paid before examinations) by requiring students who transfer or withdraw to first settle their accounts before the school issues transfer credentials. Per Section 62(e) of the MRPS and Section 124(e) of the TVET Manual, a school can legally collect outstanding tuition fees from a student who has transferred or withdrawn only if he was officially enrolled — i.e., made an initial payment accepted by the school and was authorized to attend classes. Without these circumstances, there is no legal basis for collection since no contractual relationship was established.

A student who has paid in full or for a length longer than one month and transfers/withdraws in the first week after classes begin shall be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term (one semester for collegiate/TVET, one year for elementary/secondary); 20% if in the second week; may be fully charged for all fees applicable for the entire term if any time after the second week. These charges hold whether or not the student actually participated, because what matters is that the school authorized attendance, not actual attendance.

The date of the student's written notice of withdrawal is the date of withdrawal for determination purposes. Verbal notice is not enough.

If tuition, registration, library and laboratory fees already paid equal the amount still to be paid for the first month (or less) in a monthly installment scheme, no refund shall be given upon withdrawal after the registration period, unless due to justifiable cause, in which case the student is charged only up to the last month of attendance.

Any promissory note signed by a student below 18 years old is null and void under existing DepEd ruling — it does not constitute "payment of some kind" to establish enrollment unless a responsible relative or legal guardian acts as surety, since minors are not legally competent to contract such obligations. Even a student of majority age but dependent on parents/guardian for support would not be considered competent, unless a responsible relative/guardian appears as surety. Students are entitled to a full refund of fees for any course/subject discontinued by the school or not credited by DepEd, CHED or TESDA.

## APPENDIX F — CONSTITUTION AND BY-LAWS OF THE STUDENT GOVERNMENT OF ROGATIONIST COLLEGE — SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL DEPARTMENT

**PREAMBLE**

We, the students of Rogationist College Senior High School Department, with the aid of Almighty God and impelled by the desire to establish a democratic student organization that shall unite the whole student body, protect and preserve their rights and interests, promote their welfare and the academic standards of the Alma Mater, and encourage involvement in school and in society guided by the Rogate Core Values: Excellence, Love of God and Neighbor, Patriotism and Integrity, do hereby establish and promulgate this Constitution and By-Laws.

### Article I — General Provisions

Section 1. This Constitution and By-Laws shall be known as the Constitution and By-Laws of the Student Government of Rogationist College – Senior High School Department. SG refers to the Student Government of Rogationist College – Senior High School Department.

### Article II — Name and Domicile

Section 1. The name of the organization shall be known as the Rogationist College – Senior High School Department Student Government, referred to as the SG.
Section 2. The office of the SG shall be located inside the perimeter of the school.

### Article III — Declaration of Principles and Objectives

**Principles**

Section 1. The SG shall have the power which emanates from the student body. It shall be an autonomous, unified, and highest democratic representative of the studentry.
Section 2. The SG shall uphold and adhere to the Vision of Rogationist College "to form culturally competent and socially responsible persons driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia." The Vision and Mission of Rogationist College shall serve as the guide of the SG.

**Objectives**

Section 3. The SG of Rogationist College – Senior High School Department shall be committed to promoting mutual understanding through social, civic, intellectual, spiritual, recreational and scientific programs and activities.

Section 4. The SG shall have the following objectives:
4.1. To help students develop love of God and country, moral character, personal discipline, leadership and competencies that will enable them to realize their full potential;
4.2. To aid students develop self-confidence, critical thinking, problem-solving and decision-making skills and utilize these in contributing towards nation building;
4.3. To empower students to strive for excellence in academics, leadership, and social responsibility;
4.4. To encourage students to be proactive members of society;
4.5. To train members for effective and efficient leadership;
4.6. To promote unity among leaders and the student body;
4.7. To maintain school aspirations to promote quality education and academic excellence;
4.8. To serve and protect students' rights and welfare;
4.9. To represent the student body in the policy-making body of the school concerning students' welfare.

### Article IV — Membership

Section 1. All bonafide students of Rogationist College are members of the student body to whom the SG shall be committed to serve and to be accountable at all times.

### Article V — Rights of Students

Section 1. Every student has the right to enjoy responsible freedom of speech and expression.
Section 2. Every student has the right to avail of all the services offered by the SG.
Section 3. Every student has the right to conduct and participate in all school activities.
Section 4. Every student has the right to be informed about all issues and matters concerning them.

Section 1. Every student has the responsibility to observe the laws of the Republic of the Philippines and the rules and regulations of the school at all times.
Section 2. Every student must pay on time all SG fees as approved by the School Principal or the Parents-Teachers Association.
Section 3. Every student must support and promote the thrusts and objectives of the SG and the School.
Section 4. Every student must abide by the SG constitution and by-laws.
Section 5. Every student must participate actively in all school activities and strive for academic excellence.
Section 6. Every student must exercise his/her rights and do his/her responsibilities as an SG member.
Section 7. Every student must illustrate and perform social equity.

## APPENDIX G — RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING SEXUAL HARASSMENT

**KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:**

Whereas, Rogationist College is aware of the mandate of the 1987 Philippine Constitution as to the role of educational institutions to inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, respect for human rights, appreciation of the roles of national heroes, teach the rights and duties of citizenship, strengthen ethical and spiritual values, develop moral character and personal discipline, encourage critical and creative thinking, broaden scientific and technical knowledge, and promote vocational efficiency (Article XIV, Section 3(2));

Whereas, the State, to protect the rights of individuals against sexual harassment especially in educational institutions, promulgated Republic Act 7877 ("the Sexual Harassment Act of 1995");

Whereas, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012 ("DepEd Child Protection Policy");

Whereas, Rogationist College, in order to comply with these laws, rules and regulations, formulated its own rules of procedure in addressing complaints on sexual harassment; thus, the foregoing rule is hereby adopted.

**Section 1.** This shall be known as "RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING SEXUAL HARASSMENT."

**Section 2. State Policy** — Reiterates the 1987 Constitution mandate for educational institutions (Article XIV, Section 3(2)), RA 7877, and DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012, which Rogationist College hereby adopts in toto.

**Section 3. School Policy** — RC aims to provide good and quality education while inculcating the values and teachings of Fr. Hannibal de Francia regarding morals, proper decorum and good behavior. RC's environment shall be free from all acts of sexual harassment. Proceedings shall be treated with strict confidentiality, with due process protecting both complainant and respondent, without prejudice to reportorial requirements under DO 40 s.2012 and separate criminal cases.

**Section 4. Definition of Terms**
- **Child** — any person below eighteen (18) years of age, or those over but unable to fully take care of themselves due to physical/mental disability, including all students above 18 enrolled in RC.
- **School personnel** — b.1 school head; b.2 other school officials in supervisory/policy positions; b.3 academic personnel (teaching/research staff, registrars, librarians, guidance counselors, researchers); b.4 other non-academic personnel.
- **Sexual Harassment (per R.A. 7877)** — committed by an employer, employee, manager, supervisor, agent, teacher, instructor, professor, coach, trainor, or person with authority/influence/moral ascendancy who demands, requests or requires sexual favor.
  - c.1. In a work-related environment: (1) sexual favor as a condition of employment/re-employment/continued employment/compensation/promotion; (2) refusal resulting in limiting/discriminating employment opportunities; (3) impairing employee rights/privileges; or resulting in an intimidating/hostile/offensive environment.
  - c.2. In an education or training environment: (1) against one under offender's care/custody/supervision; (2) against one whose education/training is entrusted to offender; (3) sexual favor as condition of passing grade/honors/scholarships/stipend/benefits; (4) sexual advances resulting in intimidating/hostile/offensive environment. Any person who directs, induces, or cooperates in the commission is also held liable.
- **Sexual Violence** — d.1 rape, acts of lasciviousness, demeaning/sexually suggestive remarks, physically attacking sexual parts; d.2 forcing the child to watch obscene publications/indecent shows; d.3 forcing indecent sexual acts/involvement in creation or distribution of such films/materials; d.4 acts causing/attempting to cause sexual activity by force/threat/harm/coercion/inducements/gifts/favor; other sexually prejudicial acts.

**Section 5. Procedure — Procedures for Filing a Complaint with the Discipline and Grievance Committee**

1. A written complaint shall be filed: a. by the complainant himself to the Office of the Rector, if personnel of the school or above 18; b. if the complainant/aggrieved party is a child, a written incident report by any student, personnel, parent, guardian or guidance counselor with knowledge of the incident, submitted to the Office of the Rector; c. by the minor complainant, with conformity of parent, guardian, teacher or guidance counselor.

2. After receipt, the Office of the Rector may call parties for possible amicable settlement; failing that, convenes the grievance committee: Chairman — Director of Student Services; Members — Director of Spiritual Affairs, Dean/TED Head/Principal, Coordinator for Guidance & Counseling Office, HRD (if employee involved), Prefect of Discipline (if student involved).

3. The complaint/incident report is endorsed to the chairman of the grievance committee.

4. The chairman sends a copy of the complaint to the respondent, who has five (5) working days to answer in writing, with right to counsel. Failure to answer within five days is treated as waiver, and the Committee proceeds ex-parte.

5. If justifiable reasons prevent timely reply, a non-extendable 3-day extension is granted.

6. The Chair presides during the hearing, commencing by reading the complaint/s and reply/ies.

7. Parties present evidence and witnesses; witnesses subject to cross-examination by the Committee, not the other party.

8. New witness statements during investigation give the respondent five (5) days to rebut, forwarded to the complainant.

9. Complainant presents first, followed by the respondent.

10. The Committee submits findings/recommendations to the Rector, who submits to the House Council for final decision.

11. Decisions of the House Council are final, executed by the Rector.

**Section 6. Penalty** — reprimand, suspension or termination imposed based on gravity of the charge.

**Section 7. Confidentiality clause** — All proceedings/records treated with utmost confidentiality, especially the victim's identity if a minor.

**Section 8. Reportorial Requirement of Department of Education** — HRD prepares reports for cases involving school personnel; Director for Student and Personnel Services prepares reports to DepEd for cases involving students.

## APPENDIX H — RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES OF BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE

**KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:**

Whereas, Rogationist College as a Catholic School acknowledges its obligation to exercise reasonable supervision over student conduct and cultivate the best potentialities of heart and mind, committing to formation towards love of God and neighbor and promotion of proper decorum, good behavior and positive values;

Whereas, DepEd issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012 ("DepEd Child Protection Policy");

Whereas, REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10627, or the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013," addresses growing concerns of bullying inside and outside school, and RC promotes zero-tolerance on bullying;

Whereas, RC, to comply with these laws, formulated its own rules of procedure on bullying; thus, the foregoing rule is hereby adopted.

**Section 1. Short Title** — "RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE."

**Section 2. State Policy** — Reiterates 1987 Constitution mandate (Article XIV, Section 3(2)), DepEd Order No. 40 s.2012, and RA 10627, which RC adopts in toto.

**Section 3. School Policy** — RC aims to provide quality education while inculcating St. Hannibal Mary di Francia's values regarding morals, decorum and behavior; free of bullying; proceedings treated with strict confidentiality; due process protects both complainant and accused, without prejudice to DO 40 s.2012 reportorial requirements and criminal cases.

**Section 4. Definition of Terms**
- **"Bullying"** — severe or repeated use by one or more students of written, verbal, electronic expression, physical act or gesture, or combination, directed at another student causing/placing fear of physical/emotional harm or property damage; creating a hostile environment; infringing rights; or materially disrupting the education process.
- **"Bullied" or "Victim"** — any student experiencing bullying or retaliation.
- **"Bystander"** — any person witnessing or having personal knowledge of bullying/retaliation.
- **"Learning center"** — resources/facilities for out-of-school youth/adult learning programs (DepEd Order No. 43 s. 2013).
- **"Service provider"** — non-teacher/personnel working in the school (security guards, canteen personnel, utility workers, transportation service personnel).
- **"Student"** — a person attending classes at any basic education level, per DepEd Order No. 40 s. 2012.
- **"Prompt Report"** — prompt reporting from the first day of classes until final report card distribution of the same school year, to maximize intervention/reformation/sanction.

**Section 5. Forms of Bullying** (not limited to):
1. Unwanted physical contact (punching, pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, school pranks, teasing, fighting, use of objects/weapons);
2. Acts damaging victim's psyche/emotional well-being;
3. Slanderous statements causing undue emotional distress (foul language, name-calling, tormenting, negative comments on looks/clothes/body);
4. Cyber bullying (texting, email, instant messaging, chatting, internet, social media, online games, other tech per DO 40 s.2012), including bullying at non-school locations via technology not owned/used by the school;
5. Social bullying (deliberate, repetitive, aggressive social behavior to hurt/belittle);
6. Gender-based bullying (based on perceived/actual sexual orientation and gender identity/SOGI);
7. Retaliation against a reporter, information provider, or witness to bullying.

**Section 6. Place of Commission** — a. school grounds; b. property adjacent to school grounds; c. school-sponsored activities/functions/programs on or off grounds; d. school bus stops; e. school buses/vehicles owned/leased/used by the school; f. privately-owned but school-accredited buses/services.

**Section 7. Procedure**

**A. Immediate Responses**
1. Victim, bystander, or school personnel who receives information reports to the Prefect of Discipline or Guidance Counselor.
2. School personnel notified shall intervene: i. Stop the bullying/retaliation immediately; ii. Separate students involved; iii. Remove the victim or offending student from the site; iv. Ensure victim's safety by determining/addressing immediate safety needs, and ensuring medical attention/securing a medical certificate if physical injury occurred.

**B. Reporting the Bullying Incident or Retaliation**
Bringing the bully to the Guidance Office or Office for Student Discipline. Written complaint filed by: 1. the complainant/victim if personnel or above 18; 2. a written incident report if aggrieved party is a child, submitted by any student/personnel/parent/guardian/counselor with knowledge; 3. by the minor complainant with consent of teacher/parent/guardian/counselor.

The Prefect of Discipline receiving the report asks the victim/bystander/witness to fill up the Intake Sheet per DepEd Order No. 40 s. 2012, and informs the Principal and Rector of all reports and actions taken.

**C. Fact-Finding and Documentation**
The Prefect of Discipline shall:
1. Separately interview in private the offending student/s, victim/s and others with vital information, in the presence of homeroom adviser/guidance facilitator/counselor; students write their account of the incident.
2. Determine levels of threat and develop intervention strategies; if urgent, action taken within twenty-four (24) hours.
3. Inform parents/legal guardians of both parties and personally confer to resolve issues and restore relationships as far as practicable.
4. Study the case thoroughly to determine appropriate interventions/disciplinary measures.
5. Submit to the Director of Student Services a comprehensive report on interviews, conferences, and recommended interventions/measures.
6. Make appropriate referral to the Child Protection Committee if unresolved or for further fact-finding.

**D. Due Process**
1. The Director for Student Services convenes the Child Protection Committee: Director for Student Services (Chairperson), Guidance Counselor/Teacher (Vice Chairperson), Representative of the Teachers, President of the Parents Teacher Association, President of the Student Body Organization.
2. Offending student/s and parent/s or legal guardian/s informed of the complaint in writing.
3. Students given opportunity to answer in writing within the school ground in the presence of the Committee, with assistance of parents/guardian.
4. Reply period not extendable except for justifiable reasons (max 3 calendar days extension).
5. In case of contradicting statements, parties may be called for clarificatory questioning by Committee members only.
6. Committee submits findings/recommendations to the Office of the Rector for decision/sanction.
7. Parents may appeal to the School Board within 10 calendar days of the decision; intervention program (not disciplinary action) commences pending appeal.
8. The decision of the School Board is final.

**E. Referral**
The Rector or Child Protection Committee may refer victim/s and bully/ies to trained professionals (social workers, psychologists, child protection specialists) for further assessment/intervention. The School Head or designee shall notify the Women and Children's Protection Desk (WCPD) of the local PNP if criminal charges may be pursued.

**Section 8. False Accusation of Bullying** — A student found to have knowingly made a false accusation is subject to disciplinary actions or appropriate interventions.

**Section 9. Incident Involving Students from More Than One School** — The school first informed shall promptly notify the other school's administrator/head so both may take appropriate action.

**Section 10. Anonymous Reporting** — Entertained and protected from retaliation; however, no disciplinary action shall be based solely on an anonymous report without other evidence.

**Section 11. Role/Duty of Child Protection Committee** — In addition to DepEd Order No. 40 s. 2012 duties: a. conduct awareness-raising programs; b. ensure the anti-bullying policy is implemented; c. monitor all bullying cases/incidents reported/referred; d. make necessary referrals to appropriate agencies/offices/persons.

**Section 12. Penalty** — Written reprimand, community service, suspension, exclusion or expulsion may be imposed per existing school rules (or DepEd rules for public schools), with due process. In addition, the bully undergoes an intervention program administered/supervised by the Child Protection Committee; parents encouraged to join.

**Section 13. Jurisdiction** — Complaints of bullying/abuse under DO 40 s.2012 fall within RC's exclusive jurisdiction, not for Barangay amicable settlement, subject to existing laws. Complaints against persons outside RC's jurisdiction referred to appropriate authorities.

**Section 14. Confidentiality** — Identity/information reasonably identifying the student (victim or offender) withheld from the public.

**Section 15. Criminal and Civil Liability** — Separate and distinct from administrative case; serious physical injury or death dealt with per RA 9344 (Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act) and other applicable laws.

**Section 16. Effectivity** — Effective fifteen (15) days after publication by posting in the school bulletin board or authorized publication.

**Section 17.** Signed: Silang, Cavite, March 1, 2014. RC Rector.

## APPENDIX I — ANTI-HAZING LAW OF THE REPUBLIC OF THE PHILIPPINES (Republic Act No. 8049)

*An Act Regulating Hazing and Other Forms of Initiation Rites in Fraternities, Sororities and Organizations and Providing Penalties Therefor.*

**Section 1.** Hazing is an initiation rite or practice as a prerequisite for membership in a fraternity, sorority or organization, placing the recruit/neophyte/applicant in embarrassing or humiliating situations (menial, silly, foolish tasks) or subjecting to physical/psychological suffering or injury. "Organization" includes any club, the AFP, PNP, PMA, or CMT/CAT officer/cadet corps. Physical/mental/psychological testing/training procedures to determine/enhance fitness of prospective AFP/PNP members, approved by the Secretary of National Defense and National Police Commission, are not considered hazing.

**Section 2.** No hazing/initiation shall be allowed without prior written notice to school authorities/organization head seven (7) days before the initiation, indicating the period (not exceeding 3 days), names of those subjected, and an undertaking of no physical violence.

**Section 3.** The head of the school/organization or representatives must assign at least two (2) representatives to be present during initiation and ensure no physical harm is inflicted.

**Section 4.** If the person subjected suffers physical injury or dies, officers/members who actually participated shall be liable as principals, with penalties:
1. Reclusion perpetua (life imprisonment) if death, rape, sodomy or mutilation results.
2. Reclusion temporal maximum (17y4m1d–20y) if victim becomes insane, imbecile, impotent or blind.
3. Reclusion temporal medium (14y8m1d–17y4m) if victim loses speech/hearing/smell, an eye, hand, foot, arm or leg, or becomes incapacitated for habitual work/activity.
4. Reclusion temporal minimum (12y1d–14y8m) if victim becomes deformed or loses use of a body part, or is incapacitated for more than 90 days.
5. Prison mayor maximum (10y1d–12y) if incapacitated for more than 30 days.
6. Prison mayor medium (8y1d–10y) if incapacitated for 10+ days or injury requires medical assistance for the same period.
7. Prison mayor minimum (6y1d–8y) if incapacitated for 1-9 days or injury requires equivalent medical assistance.
8. Prison correccional maximum (4y2m1d–6y) if physical injuries do not prevent habitual activity/work nor require medical attendance.

School/police/military/citizen's army training officials may impose administrative sanctions even before conviction. Maximum penalty applies when: (a) recruitment is accompanied by force/violence/threat/intimidation/deceit against a refusing recruit; (b) a recruit who initially consented is prevented from quitting upon learning of hazing; (c) a recruit who underwent hazing is prevented from reporting through force/violence/threat/intimidation; (d) hazing is committed outside the school/institution; (e) the victim is below twelve (12) years of age.

The owner of the place where hazing is conducted is liable as an accomplice if aware but failed to prevent it. If hazing occurs in an officer's/member's home, parents are liable as principals if aware but failed to prevent it. School authorities/faculty who consent or have actual knowledge but fail to act are punished as accomplices. Officers, former officers or alumni who planned the hazing (even if absent) are liable as principals. A fraternity/sorority adviser present during the acts who fails to prevent them is liable as principal. Presence during hazing is prima facie evidence of participation as principal unless the person prevented the commission of the acts. No mitigating circumstance of "no intention to commit so grave a wrong" applies. This section applies to responsible officers of a corporation engaged in hazing as an employment requirement.

**Section 5.** Separability clause.

**Section 6.** Repealing clause.

**Section 7.** Effective fifteen (15) calendar days after publication in at least two (2) national newspapers.

Approved: June 07, 1995. (Signed) Fidel V. Ramos, President of the Philippines.

## APPENDIX J — DANGEROUS DRUGS BOARD REGULATION NO. 6, Series of 2003

**SUBJECT: General Guidelines For The Conduct Of Random Drug Testing For Secondary And Tertiary Students**

Pursuant to Section 36(c), Article III of Republic Act No. 9165, these guidelines apply to random drug testing of students in public and private secondary, tertiary/higher education institutions and post-secondary technical vocational schools, outlining purposes, procedures and consequences of positive results after confirmation. Procedures must account for fairness and rehabilitation, not isolation, and must not violate constitutional rights to due process, equal protection and self-incrimination.

**A. Guiding Principles**
1. Government recognizes the primary responsibility of the family/parents for education and awareness about dangerous drugs.
2. Parental involvement maximized in drug education, testing, treatment and rehabilitation.
3. Schools, with LGU/agency assistance, must provide a healthy, drug-free environment.
4. Emphasis on guidance/counseling, treatment and rehabilitation, respecting students' right to continue/complete studies.
5. Academic freedom of higher institutions respected.
6. Drug abuse prevention/education programs intensified.
7. Random drug testing is a collaborative undertaking of government, schools, students and parents, not for harassment.
8. Implemented primarily for prevention and rehabilitation.
9. Guarantees personal privacy and dignity.
10. Results treated with utmost confidentiality.
11. Results not used in criminal proceedings.

**B. Purposes** — determine prevalence of drug users; assess prevention program effectivity; deter illegal drug use; facilitate rehabilitation; strengthen collaboration against drug use.

**C. Definition of Terms** — Drug Counselor; Drug Testing Coordinator (school principal/administrator); Parents (includes court-appointed guardians); Random selection; Rehabilitation; Schools; Selection Board (Drug Testing Coordinator as chair, one representative each from students, faculty, parents); Supervising Agency (DepEd, CHED, or TESDA).

**D. Procedures**
1. **Notification** — Supervising Agency informs schools; schools explain provisions/procedures to the community, included in the handbook. Students/parents notified in writing of the process; failure to return acknowledgment receipt does not bar testing.
2. **Samples** — Supervising Agency informs randomly selected schools; whole student population included in random sampling; sample size yields 95% statistical confidence level.
3. **Selection of Samples** — Drug Testing Coordinator convenes Selection Board within five days of notice; random selection and testing done the same day, ensuring confidentiality/integrity; selected students disclose prescription medicines/vitamins/supplements taken within past 5 days; laboratory follows DOH guidelines; monitor assigned is of the same sex as the student; testing done in school by a duly accredited laboratory.
4. **Treatment of Random Drug Test Results** — Results strictly confidential, not published/posted. If positive, a confirmatory test is scheduled with parent conference; results transmitted in sealed envelope; first-time positive confirmatory result is not grounds for expulsion/disciplinary action; student referred to DOH-accredited facility/physician for dependency-level determination; 3-month observation/counseling period follows; if no improvement or second positive result, referral to DOH-accredited facility per level of dependency, or per Section 61, R.A. 9165, without prejudice to Section 73.

**E. Reportorial Requirements** — Names/results not revealed except to student/parents; aggregate results (without identities) submitted by School Head to DepEd Division Superintendent, CHED Regional Director, or TESDA Training Institution Administrator.

**F. Training of Guidance Counselors** — Conducted by DepEd, CHED, TESDA, PDEA and DDB.

**G. Expenses of the Program** — DOH designates testing laboratories; payment of fees done by government through DOH.

**H. Enforcement of Compliance** — Refusal to undergo testing dealt with per school rules; refusal alone does not presume drug use/dependency, but school may impose sanctions for refusal itself. Schools refusing to implement the program are liable under Section 32 of RA 9165.

**I. Miscellaneous Provisions** — Separability clause; effectivity immediately upon DDB approval.

ADOPTED and APPROVED this 1st day of August, 2003 at Camp Crame, Quezon City.

### DISCIPLINARY MEASURES (Blended Learning Addendum)

Disciplinary actions are corrective and formative measures for students who fail to comply with school policies. Actions range from verbal warning to dismissal/exclusion depending on gravity of the offense. The teacher in-charge directly monitors student conduct; offenses are reported to Discipline Officers for verification and processing.

Prompt reporting is encouraged, covering incidents from the first day of classes until the final report card distribution of the following year.

**Conference with Subject Teachers** — done for academic-related offenses (non-submission of mini task, unnecessary remarks during online class, etc.). After a third such offense, the case may be referred to the Office for Student Discipline.

**Due Process** — Students' right to due process through conference and dialogue is recognized. The Discipline Officer calls students involved; parents/legal guardians are invited to be informed and answer charges. Major offenses may be assessed by a discipline committee convened by the Prefect of Discipline (composed of the DSS, Principal, Discipline Officer, and Guidance Counselor); a final conference is facilitated with student and parents. Students may be referred to the Guidance Office if counseling is deemed more appropriate.

### PARENT(S)/GUARDIAN(S) AS COLLABORATORS IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF RC DIGIPLINE

Parent(s)/guardian(s) are key collaborators in ensuring continuity of learning and discipline at home. They are encouraged to:

1. Promote and implement proper netiquette under their supervision.
2. Conscientiously guide their children/wards in online activities, especially during online classes.
3. Monitor children during online classes to help teachers prevent dishonesty in schoolwork/requirements, promoting academic integrity (core values of Excellence and Integrity).
4. Encourage practice of time management in day-to-day activities.
5. Let children know by heart what digital literacy and digital citizenship mean:
   - *"Digital literacy"* is the ability to use ICT to find, evaluate, create and communicate information, requiring cognitive and technical skills.
   - *"Digital citizenship"* refers to norms of appropriate, responsible behavior regarding technology use.
   (p.3 of DOJ Office of Cybercrime's Public Advisory on Conduct of Online Classes Using Video Conferencing Services, September 22, 2020)
6. Instill exercise of due diligence in children's online dealings and activities.
7. Teach discernment of online information and provide guidance on safety measures against cybercrime victimization.
8. Not take any drastic action or in any way harass another party in case of misunderstanding between students.
