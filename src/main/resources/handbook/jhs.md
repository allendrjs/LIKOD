## THE FOUNDER — SAINT HANNIBAL MARY DI FRANCIA

St. Hannibal was born to a knight and a noblewoman in Messina, Italy on July 5, 1851. He was orphaned at a tender age. As a young man, he had been given the intuition of the necessity of Prayer for Vocations. When he became a priest (March 16, 1878), he committed himself to the moral and spiritual redemption of the poorest area in his city, giving up all his inherited wealth to the service of God through his manifold works of charity among the poor children. He is known as the "Apostle of Prayer for Vocations" because he focused all his works on the promotion of the ROGATE (Latin for "pray"), following the evangelical call of our Lord as told by two evangelists: Matthew (9:37-38) and Luke (10:2) — "THE HARVEST IS GREAT, BUT THE WORKERS ARE FEW, PRAY THEREFORE THE LORD OF THE HARVEST TO SEND GOOD WORKERS INTO HIS HARVEST." Pondering upon the meaning and message of the sacred words, he saw in them the ineffable solution for the salvation of the many poor children whom he learned to love with tender and divine affection. Fr. Hannibal founded two religious congregations, the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and the Daughters of Divine Zeal, to help him carry out and propagate the prayer for vocations.

Fr. Hannibal passed to eternity on June 1, 1927, but his spirit remains alive in the hearts and minds of all his spiritual sons and daughters. He was beatified on October 7, 1990 during the Synod of Bishops and was canonized on May 16, 2004 in Rome, Italy by His Holiness, Pope John Paul II.

## FOREWORD

A wholesome educational formation is possible only through cooperative efforts extended by both the home and the school. Consistency of purpose and manner in realizing this formation is best achieved through policies and regulations.

This handbook is intended for the purpose of this realization. It aims to summarize in an orderly and readable manner all the salient information and the policies and regulations of Rogationist College. Through this, it is expected that:

1. Students will acquaint themselves with the objectives, policies, procedures and regulations of Rogationist College;
2. Administrators and teachers will acquire working knowledge in guiding the education community to realize the vision-mission and goals of the school as they promote the observance of its policies and regulations; and
3. Parents will understand their role in promoting and realizing the educational and institutional objectives of the school.

The policies, rules and regulations in this handbook are intended to promote an atmosphere that is conducive to the students' personal growth founded on Christ and His Gospel to pursue God's call, in obedience to the Rogate, and in accordance with the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

# Part I — HISTORY

Like any family or community, Rogationist College (RC) has a rich historical heritage which every RC student should be proud of. To appreciate more deeply the roots of an RC community, an overview of the history of the congregation of the Rogationists, and more particularly that of RC, is presented here.

## THE ROGATIONIST CONGREGATION

The Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus (RCJ) is a religious congregation of priests and brothers founded by St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia in 1887 in Messina, Italy. Both the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and its sister congregation, the Daughters of Divine Zeal, are devoted to prayer and promotion of vocations in the Church, and to the education of young people, particularly the poor and disadvantaged. The legacy of St. Hannibal is kept alive by these congregations present today in Europe (Italy, Spain, Albania and Poland); in South America (Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay); in North America (USA and Mexico); in Africa (Rwanda and Cameroun); in Oceania (Papua New Guinea); and in Asia (the Philippines, India, Korea, Vietnam, and Indonesia).

## THE ROGATIONIST PHILIPPINE DELEGATION

The Rogationists established their first missionary activities in the Philippines in November 1976 to promote a culture of vocation and to assist the poor especially the children. Their long term strategy was to prepare young Filipinos to be agents of social growth and transformation driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

Milestones in the development of the Rogationist Philippine Delegation:

- **October 1977** — opening of the first Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park (Parañaque);
- **November 1979** — construction of a parish church in Multinational Village (Parañaque);
- **June 1980** — profession of the religious vows of the first three Filipino Rogationist novices;
- **December 1981** — opening of a second and larger Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park;
- **June 1987** — opening of St. Anthony's Boys Village and Rogationist Academy (High School) in Silang, Cavite;
- **April 1990** — opening of the Rogationist Formation Center for the Visayan Region in Cebu City;

## THE OFFICIAL RC LOGO

**DEUS ET PROXIMUS**

The Official RC logo represents man's total human and Christian formation.

The three Latin terms *Fides*, *Sapientia* and *Virtus* stand for Faith, Wisdom and Virtue, which are indicative of the three pillars (Religion, Reason, and Love) upon which the Rogationist College System of Education rests.

The open book represents the Word of God — The HOLY BIBLE — the source of inspiration, knowledge and strength of all those who are part of the Institution.

The Latin words *Deus et Proximus* (God and neighbor) stand for the direction of Rogationist College education.

# Part II — LIFE PURPOSE, GUIDING PHILOSOPHY, VISION AND MISSION

## THE LIFE PURPOSE

Rogationist College exists primarily to enable the youth, especially the poor and underprivileged assisted by the St. Anthony's Boys Village Foundation, to discover in their life the project of God by becoming good Christians and responsible members of society through Secondary, Technical and Tertiary education anchored on the formative philosophy of the Rogationists.

## GUIDING PHILOSOPHY

Rogationist College (RC) in St. Anthony's Boys Village in Silang, Cavite is a Catholic co-educational community committed to the formation of the whole human person.

RC, in its mission to help the youth, bases its educative system on a philosophy of education which is attentive to the instances of human life. It is both personalistic and vocational in nature. RC forms its students to the proper understanding and dynamics of self. The process brings them to a serene enjoyment of life according to the dictum of responsible acting and sharing.

The vocational nature of the RC system facilitates its students in the acquisition of a realistic life perspective. It is a view which is sensitive to life's origin and destiny. RC believes and professes that every person is called not only to live but also to fulfill life as God's gift.

RC is convinced that real education is not just academic education. It espouses a style of forming that opens avenues to the proper exercise of love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence.

The educative formation of RC is always considered an encounter between the teachers and their students. In doing the personalistic and vocational approach to education, RC individuates three interrelated moments of the meeting, which ends in the mature choices for the life orientation of its students.

The first moment is **ORGANIZATION**. Here, the organizing student is met by a teacher who is a guide. In this moment, the students are initiated to find and form the most important instances of their life and then cherish them.

The second moment is **IDENTIFICATION**. Here teachers are models. St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia, founder of the Rogationists, wants all institutions of the Rogationists to educate through good examples. RC believes that for students to grow in the right direction, the edifying life of teachers is very important.

The third moment is **DECISION**. Self-fulfillment is also self-determination. At the end of the formative process, students of RC should have already acquired a certain degree of independence. Knowing better to distinguish right from wrong, they should be capable of pursuing not just terrestrial but also personal and possibly celestial good.

RC's philosophy of education, following the teachings of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia and inspired by the Gospel of the Christ of the Rogate, goes therefore to the roots of the human person.

## VISION

Rogationist College is a prime Catholic educational community dedicated to the formation of culturally competent and socially responsible persons driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

## MISSION

Rogationist College commits itself to:

1. Form its members toward Love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence;
2. Offer a balanced and technically-oriented curriculum, as well as excellent instruction, learning environment and facilities;
3. Prepare well-rounded graduates who are agents of social growth and transformation; and
4. Promote a culture of vocation and good workers who, like St. Hannibal, will be espousing the cause of the poor especially the children.

# Part III — GENERAL POLICIES AND GUIDELINES

> "The home shall fully support the school in the implementation of the total school program — curricular and co-curricular — toward the proper physical, social, intellectual and moral development of the child."
> — Article 76, The Child and Youth Welfare Code (Presidential Decree No. 603, s. 1974)

## GENERAL POLICIES

1. The rules and regulations of RC as stated in this Handbook are intended to enhance the life in school of an RC student as he relates and interacts with the members of the school community. As such, it is understood that they are mutual good role models of each other.
2. It is the duty and responsibility of the parents and/or guardians to keep track of the progress of their children/wards in school. The parents and/or guardians are encouraged to confer with the school authorities at their own volition or whenever calls for conference are issued to them. This is to enhance the harmonious working relationship between the school and the home for the welfare of the student.
3. The enrollment of a student in RC is taken as an expressed willingness on his/her part, and consequently of his/her parents and/or guardians, to abide by all rules and regulations of the school. Thus, the student and his/her parents and/or guardians are committed to the strict observance of the same.
4. RC reserves the right to admit, readmit, refuse admission or exclude a student or drop the name of an erring student from the school rolls on the basis of his/her academic performance, conduct or attendance.
5. RC acknowledges its obligations to exercise reasonable supervision over the conduct of the student and to cultivate the best potentialities of the heart and mind of the student. Its role in "loco parentis" is the exercise of substitute parental authority over students in the school and in school-related activities (Civil Code, Art. 349).
6. RC, as a Catholic school, strongly respects and adheres to the practice of the holy sacrament (matrimony) and does not tolerate sexual indiscretions. Violation of this policy would merit the student appropriate disciplinary action, including but not limited to, requiring the student to go on leave or will be excluded or dropped from the school rolls.
7. RC expects its students to uphold the standards of responsibility and dignity. When a student of RC does not measure up to these standards, he/she will be excluded or dropped from the school rolls after due process.
8. Students, either as individuals or as a group, must not use the name and seal of the school without official authorization.
9. Students who are using social media in their respective homes are not to make inappropriate comments (including in private messages) about the school personnel or other students. Concerns and/or complaints must be written and directly addressed to the proper school authority rather than posting them on social media — whether on their own pages, in closed groups (e.g. groups set up for school parents to communicate with each other) or on the school's pages. Complaints posted on social media will not be entertained by the school.
10. Parents or guardians may consult or confer with teachers and instructors provided they secure appointments with the Department Head (Principal, Dean or TED Head) two days before the actual date of appointment.
11. Parents, guardians or visitors are not allowed to go to the classrooms. Messages and allowance for the students must be coursed through the Guidance Office.
12. Students may be called out of the classrooms provided an approved written permission/call slip is issued by either of the following:
    12.1. Rector/Vice-Rector
    12.2. Director for Academic Affairs (DAA)
    12.3. Director for Student Services (DSS)
    12.4. Director for Spiritual Affairs (DSA)
    12.5. Principal/Dean/Technical Education Department Head (TED Head)
    12.6. Vice Principal/Vice Dean
    12.7. Guidance Counselor/Facilitator
    12.8. Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer
    12.9. Health Services Coordinator/Nurse

As a Catholic School, with Rogationist Formation as the core of the curriculum, RC requires all students to attend and pass all the requirements of Christian Living Education or Religious Education subjects. Students also need to attend masses and/or similar activities and complete spiritual recollections and retreats scheduled by the institution. In addition, parents of High School students are required to follow the agreement (to attend/participate in parents' formation programs, PTA assemblies, etc.) signed during the enrolment of their children.

## SUSPENSION OF CLASSES

Classes are suspended in the following situations:

1. Typhoon warning signal is raised by PAGASA in the area of Cavite. Signal no. 2 automatically suspends classes in the secondary level while signal no. 3 automatically suspends classes in the tertiary level. Depending on signal numbers declared at 10:00 pm and 4:30 am of the following day, classes in appropriate levels for the whole day are deemed automatically cancelled/suspended. (DepEd Order series 2012).
2. Natural calamities or disturbances compromising the safety of the students;
3. Rector's announcement;
4. Cancellation of classes by DepEd, TESDA or CHED;
5. National, Provincial, and Municipal proclamations.

**N.B.** Afternoon classes in the appropriate levels in areas with signal numbers declared at 11:00 am that day will be cancelled or suspended at 2:00 pm to ensure the safety of the students and to provide ample time for the school authorities to contact school service providers and/or the parents. In addition to this, during heavy rains affecting the health or safety of their children, parents have the discretion not to send their children to school.

(DepEd Order No. 88 series 2010: Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, Section 119, Rules for Registration.) Thus, a student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month, may be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 20% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.
4. Examination permits are issued by the Registrar's Office for the JHS & SHS and by the Cashier for the College/TED students. This should be signed by the Cashier two weeks before each major examination. It is recommended that accounts be settled at least two weeks before the scheduled examination period to avoid inconvenience and delay.
5. Tuition fees and other charges for transferring or withdrawing students shall be governed by the following provisions:

   For College students (Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education, Section 100, Tuition Charges): A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged 25% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 50% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes.

## GUIDELINES AND CONDITIONS ON MONITORING/PROBATION STATUS

RC education is for all students who can maintain its standards of academic excellence and exemplary conduct. It requires a high degree of achievement from its students. Thus, a student who does not meet the standards set by the school is put on a probationary status.

Probation is a period of time designed to ascertain the ability of a student to abide by the academic and conduct standards of the school.

### I. Academic Probation Status

Academic Probation status is a formal warning or reminder that carries with it a condition that improvement in terms of academic achievement is necessary for readmission and/or promotion. This is given to a student so that he will show improvement during the school year.

1.1. Academic Probation is given to a student who has shown below average academic performance in the previous school year — a status imposed on a student whose final general average is below 82%.
1.2. The student on Academic Probation is required to maintain good grades (no failing marks in any subject) and to have a final general average of at least 82% in order for his/her status to change to that of a regular student the following school year.
1.3. Failure on the part of the student to show definite improvement or abide by the academic standards of the school within the school year would mean non-readmission for the following school year.
1.4. A student placed on Academic Probation for an incoming school year shall not be eligible for any higher elective and/or appointive position in any varsity team, club or organization, subject to the discretion of the Rector.

### II. Conduct Monitoring Status

1. A student is placed on Conduct Monitoring after incurring a series of minor offenses during a school year.
2. If a student commits other minor offenses or a major offense during the monitoring period, his/her status will be elevated to conduct probation for the current school year.
3. The Conduct Monitoring status may be lifted the following school year if the student shows satisfactory improvement in his/her behavior.

### III. Conduct Probation Status

1. A student is placed on Conduct Probation after committing a major offense.
2. A student placed on Conduct Probation for an incoming school year shall not be eligible for any elective and/or appointive position in any varsity team, club or organization, subject to the discretion of the Rector.
3. If a student placed on Conduct Probation commits a series of minor offenses or a major offense, he/she may be dismissed in the following school year or he/she may be dismissed immediately.
4. The Conduct Probation status may be lifted after a school year if the student shows remarkable improvement in his/her behavior.

### Enhanced Student Intervention Program

In relation to the above policy, RC adopts part of the outcome-based education approach, which focuses on giving "complete assistance for learners when they face challenges and giving them adequate time to [master a competency]" (Suvin, 2018). This means constant accompaniment through follow-ups, remediation, conferences and communication with parents is conducted throughout the quarter. If these efforts are still not effective and the student gets a failing mark at the end of the quarter, he/she will be given 1 more month to recuperate and do extra activities/remediation to reach the "Fairly Satisfactory" level, equivalent to a 75 mark. If these efforts are still futile, the student will be recommended for the Enhanced Student Intervention Program, wherein subject teachers will propose tutorial, enhancement, home visitation or home education program to further accompany the student towards better academic performance and better character formation. If at the end of the school year, all of these efforts are still not effective and the student still gets a failing mark in a subject, he/she will not be allowed to enrol in RC the next school year. This policy is stipulated in the Student Handbook and is presented during the Orientation with students and parents.

After one quarter of teaching, assessment, remediation, and parent conferences (Ref: DepEd Order #8, s. 2015), the subject teachers will propose tutorial, enhancement, home visitation or home education programs for selected students due to very low output (below average/failing marks/problematic study attitude, or even behavior in class or special concerns) and to further accompany them towards better academic performance and better character formation.

1. **Student Follow-Up and Parent Conference** — Students who need accompaniment either in academics or behavior are expected to be guided by both parents and the school. Close collaboration between the parents, the teachers, guidance office, discipline office and the office of the principal/vice principal is observed.
2. **Remedial Classes** — To assist students who still have difficulty understanding the lessons, Rogationist College will conduct online remedial classes/consultation with teachers on a specific schedule per week.
3. **Tutorial Program** — This is given to students who lack units in a specific subject due to transferring in or the subject not being offered in the previous school (e.g., students from abroad). This may also be availed of by students who transferred to another TLE/Elective course after a quarter due to change in future career choice or realignment of track in senior high school. There should only be 1-3 students per teacher per session. Topics to be covered will depend on the nature of the need, based on assessment by the tutor and the subject teacher's feedback; the program may extend from 1 quarter to 1 year depending on the case or need.
4. **Academic Enhancement Program** — Given to students who received an incomplete mark in the quarterly or final rating, either due to non-submission of requirements or a very low grade that did not reach the passing mark (75) despite educational interventions given. This serves as remedial and assistance to comply and get a passing mark within the given time frame. Coverage includes lessons taken and competencies lacked or had difficulty with during the quarter or school year.
5. **Home Visitation Program** — Employed after exhausting all the interventions mentioned above, especially if the parents are not as cooperative as expected, if there is no progress on the student's performance/behavior, or simply to strengthen school-family relations for the betterment of the child.
6. **Home Education Program**

   *Legal Basis:* Home schooling is legal in the Philippines. The 1987 Philippine Constitution, Article XIV, Section 1(2), states that the country will "Establish and maintain a system of free public education in the elementary and high school levels. Without limiting the natural right of parents to rear their children..." Under this phrase, religious groups, mission boards, and families can branch off from public education to create their own private education. However, the Constitution also provides under Section 4(1) that "the State recognizes the complementary roles of public and private institutions in the education system and shall exercise reasonable supervision and regulation of all education institutions."

   Further, DECS Memo No. 216 s. 1997, "Home Education Program," states that "home education is designed to provide an alternative delivery system of education from which children acquire the necessary skills to develop their full potential to become self-propelling, fulfilled, and contributing members of the community" and "if a home-schooled student wants to transfer into a conventional school, he or she must first be accredited by the DepEd."

   *Rationale:* Home Education Program is offered in Rogationist College on the bases of the following: (1) The child is a bonafide student of Rogationist College in the current year; (2) The child has experienced/suffered from: (a) sickness upon recommendation of an attending physician, with attached medical certificate/abstract or other related evidence; (b) displacement by calamity if the family was affected/heavily stricken by typhoon, flood, fire, earthquake and others that hinder the child's regular attendance at school; (c) financial incapability due to sudden unemployment or financial crisis.

### II. Academic Policies

**1. Types of Assessment** (DepEd Order #8, s. 2015: Policy Guidelines on Classroom Assessment of the Basic Education Program)

Teachers give the following guides to students: Title of the Assessment Activity; Date of Submission/Presentation; Guidelines/Mechanics of the Activity; Criteria/Rubric; Documentation/Evaluation & Sample evidences of the Performance Task/Assessment Activity.

**9. Guidelines in Technology Integration and Use of Gadgets**

*Rationale:* The use of Technology in classroom instruction has proved to be very helpful in the teaching-learning process. Several studies have proven that integration of technology has made student performance higher. However, other studies show that integration of technology reaches its optimal effect only if teachers are trained to utilize it and maximize its use in the classroom. Likewise, too much use of technology may hamper student learning and be detrimental to health, especially if not regulated. On this premise, the Rogationist College Junior High School Department has established guidelines in Technology Integration and Use of Gadgets, not only in the school but also in the classroom.

*Nature:* Several technological instructional aids (projectors and televisions) and even internet (WiFi connection) are provided in the school and installed in classrooms/hallways; hence maximum utilization is encouraged. However, other gadgets/devices may also be utilized inside and outside the classroom, both by teachers and students, provided they are used solely for documentation and the teaching-learning process. Generally, students are not allowed to bring and use gadgets in school except if there is a valid reason or when activity organizers, upon approval of the administration, allow their use. However, a sense of responsibility on both parties is encouraged. The school will not be liable for any damage or loss of a gadget not reported/surrendered to proper authorities. Proper sanction/administrative action will also be served to a student/teacher who does not subscribe to this rule. The use of Facebook, Messenger, games and other applications is blocked in school premises so that students and personnel may not be distracted from their work and study.

*Guidelines for Students:* Generally, students are not allowed to bring and use gadgets in school; however, this will not apply if the gadget will be used in activities related to academics. The following guidelines should be followed:
1. Students may be allowed to bring and use the said gadget provided that parents are informed of its use and the student presents a written permission from the parents (with specified purposes/subjects) to be signed by the subject teacher.
2. Students who brought the required gadgets should surrender the gadgets to their subject teacher concerned before the morning assembly or after the 1st period if the class involved will be in the first period.
3. He/She should log-in/out in the Gadget Depository Log and secure the Permitted Gadget Form from the teacher. The Gadget Depository Log should be placed in a visible/accessible place in case the teacher is not available when the student deposits or retrieves his/her gadget.
4. Should the gadget be used in multiple subjects in different schedules, the student should let each subject teacher sign before and after each use of the gadget during their class. He/She should deposit it with the last teacher from which the student used the gadget.

**N.B.** In the new normal, the school will determine amendments for the usage of gadgets.

### Guidelines in Giving Student Awards

Rogationist College gives quarterly and annual awards to outstanding students who have distinguished themselves in Academics, Diligence, Conduct, and Leadership. Special Awards related to co-curricular activities are also given. These awards promote the educational development and personal growth of students in the fields of intellectual, behavioral and affective formation. The highest award given by the institution is the **Rogate Award**.

**1. ACADEMICS**

1.1. **Quarterly** — All students who obtained a general average of 90% & above, have a satisfactory mark in conduct and no grade lower than 85% in any of the learning areas will be given a certificate of academic excellence at the end of the quarter: 98-100 With Highest Honors; 95-97 With High Honors; 90-94 With Honors.

1.2. **Annual** — All students who obtained a general average of 90% & above, have a satisfactory mark in conduct and no grade lower than 85% in any learning area in any quarter will be given medals at the end of the school year: 98-100 With Highest Honors (Gold Medal); 95-97 With High Honors (Silver Medal); 90-94 With Honors (Bronze Medal).

**2. DILIGENCE**

This award shall be given to students who manifest remarkable and positive attitude towards education and the learning process. They must possess a natural desire and willingness to learn and be educated and have the personal initiative to improve their understanding and comprehension of the lessons.

2.1. **Quarterly** — Awardees are determined by the following procedure: (2.1.1) In the first quarter, advisers and teachers nominate and rank candidates per class for the award; nominees should have satisfactory performance with Approaching Proficiency (AP) as the lowest rating in any learning area. Of the nominees per class, only the top three student awardees will receive certificates. (2.1.2) In the succeeding quarters (second and third), nominees' quarterly averages should have improved and must not have a rating lower than Approaching Proficiency (AP). Standard deviation is applied to the difference between quarterly marks. The top three ranking students per class per quarter will receive the certificates.

2.2. **Annual** — Quarterly awardees automatically become candidates for the annual awards. Other candidates can be nominated as long as their averages have improved remarkably and consistently until the finals. The top three ranking student awardees per year level will receive the medals. However, the number of awardees may vary depending on how many candidates have met the set criteria.

**3. CONDUCT**

This award shall be given to students who manifest exemplary human behavior and meet the highest standards of good character and discipline set by the school. They must have conducted themselves in conformity with the school rules and regulations. The rating for conduct shall be based on the four core values (Integrity – 25%, Excellence – 25%, Love of God and Neighbor – 25%, Patriotism – 25%). Awardees shall be determined from the computed averages of conduct marks given by subject teachers.

The core values should be manifested through:

- **LOVE OF GOD AND NEIGHBOR** — He/She influences fellow students to visit and pray in the chapel, leads fellow students in prayers, shows positive attitude in dealings with fellow students, exercises charity throughout the department, and refrains from anger when faced with difficult circumstances.
- **PATRIOTISM** — He/She shows pride and honor in being a true Filipino, involves himself/herself in the school's flag ceremony and other school programs which promote nationalism. He/She also abides by the rules and regulations set in his/her community and upholds Filipino virtues such as respect for the elderly, righteousness and *pakikisama*.
- **INTEGRITY** — He/She gives healthy opinions and sound views during class or group discussions, exercises responsibility over tasks performed in and outside the classroom, demonstrates honesty at all times, acts as a good role model to fellow students, and holds on to his/her moral beliefs.
- **EXCELLENCE** — He/She uses free time to help other students understand difficult lessons, complies with all school requirements on time, shows outstanding performance in academics and behavioral concerns, and is respected by fellow students and highly regarded by teachers and school personnel.

**4. Leadership Award**

This annual award is given to any graduating student who has exemplified outstanding leadership qualities during his/her stay in Rogationist College. The awardee shall be chosen from nominees presented by the teachers. Criteria for a nominee: (4.1) an elected school, club or class officer; (4.2) has manifested honesty, decisiveness, responsibility, obedience, promptness, affability/friendliness, and community-orientedness during his/her term of office; (4.3) has no failing grades in any learning area; (4.4) has not committed any major offense.

**5. Special Award**

Given to students who have represented and/or won in competition at the district, division, regional, national, or international levels — to publicly affirm learners who have brought honor to the school, exhibiting exemplary skills and achievement in leadership, academic, sports, arts, or religion.

**6. Rogate Award**

This is the highest honor Rogationist College bestows on any of its graduating students, given to whoever best exemplifies the spirit and ideals of Saint Hannibal Mary Di Francia. Requirements: (5.1) belongs to the graduating class; (5.2) must have stayed for four years in Rogationist College; (5.3) prays for and promotes vocation; (5.4) cares for the poor; (5.5) must have an academic average of Very Satisfactory or better in every grade level (no grade lower than 85% in all quarters, all grade levels, as reflected in the report card); (5.6) must have conducted himself/herself in conformity with the school rules and regulations (no conduct grade below 85% in any quarter, in all grade levels). Thus, those with Satisfactory (S) or lower conduct grades in any quarter of their stay are automatically disqualified.

**N.B.** Without a qualified candidate, no award will be given.

In selecting the Rogate Awardee: (1) there shall be nomination from students; (2) there shall be nomination and screening from teachers; (3) the short list of candidates is passed by the Honor's Committee to the School Board; (4) candidates shall submit an essay to the School Board (specific topic determined by the School Board); (5) the School Board shall conduct an individual or panel interview with candidates before determining the awardee.

**Guidelines on the Selection of Honor Students**

1. Any member of the non-graduating/graduating classes shall be a candidate for honors if he/she meets the criteria specified in the Annual Award/s.
2. An honor selection committee shall be created and chaired by the Principal to screen and select honor students, composed of: a teacher-representative from each year level, the Vice-Principal, the Prefect of Discipline and Guidance Facilitator. Final deliberations shall be approved by the Director for Academic Affairs.
3. The Principal shall make the final announcement of honor students after approval of the Director for Academic Affairs and information to the Rector.
4. Any clarifications are entertained only during or right after the deliberation and before the final announcement of results.

## DISCIPLINARY MEASURES

Disciplinary actions are corrective and formative measures given to students who fail to comply with the policies and rules of the school. The primary objective of these disciplinary actions is to guide and accompany erring students to the right behavior, which also takes into consideration the guidelines stated above (proper student decorum).

Disciplinary actions range from verbal warning to exclusion depending on the gravity of the offense and its circumstances.

The Discipline Officer directly monitors students' conduct. All offenses incurred by students shall be immediately reported to the Discipline Officers, who will do the necessary verification and processing of cases.

Prompt reporting of incidents of misbehavior of an RC student is encouraged, covering incidents from the first day of classes of the current year until the final report card distribution of the following year, in order to maximize intervention, reformation and/or imposition of appropriate disciplinary action(s) by the school.

### 1. Disciplinary Actions for Minor Offenses

| Frequency of Minor Offenses | Corresponding Disciplinary Action |
|---|---|
| 1st Minor offense | Verbal warning |
| 2nd Minor offense | Written warning |
| 3rd Minor offense | Community Service (a 2-hour service/work specified by the Discipline Officer in agreement with the student and/or parent or guardian) |
| 4th Minor offense & Succeeding minor offenses | Working Suspension (a 4th minor offense constitutes a major offense; done in agreement with the parent or guardian of the erring student) |

**N.B.** In some cases, erring students may not be allowed to attend/participate in a major school activity such as Acquaintance Party, Celestial Night, Christmas Party, Sports Festival, etc.

### 2. For Major Offenses

2.1. **Working Suspension** — A suspended student reports to work in school offices or canteen for one day or more, depending on the gravity/nature of the offense. Corresponding conduct grade applies (70%-79%).

2.2. **Preventive Suspension** — A student under investigation for a serious or grave case may be preventively suspended from entering the school premises, especially if the evidence of guilt is strong and the Office for Student Discipline and the Principal are morally convinced that continued stay during the investigation constitutes a distraction to normal school operations or poses a risk or danger to persons and property. This may also be imposed to protect the student with a pending case from acts of vindictiveness and to maintain an atmosphere conducive to learning.

2.3. **Exclusion** — The school reserves the right to exclude/dismiss, or drop from school during the year, any student who fails to give positive evidence of earnestness of purpose and active cooperation in all requirements of conduct, or whose conduct makes the student a liability and nuisance to the school.

**N.B.** In some major disciplinary offenses, erring students may not be allowed to attend/participate in a major school activity such as Acquaintance Party, Celestial Night, Christmas Party, Educational Tour, Sports Festival, Completion Rite, etc.

### II. Monitoring and Probation

1. **Conduct Monitoring** — A period during which a student who has incurred a series of minor offenses during a school year will be closely monitored by the Guidance Counselors/Facilitators and Discipline Officers for one school year. The monitoring may be lifted the following school year if the student shows satisfactory improvement of behavior. However, if the student commits other minor offenses or a major offense during the monitoring period, his/her status will be elevated to Conduct Probation for the current school year. Further, an erring student shall be placed under Conduct Monitoring if involved in a special case (neither a minor nor major offense).
2. **Conduct Probation** — A student who has committed a major offense falls under this status, closely followed up by the Adviser, Guidance Counselor and Discipline Officers for one school year. If he/she commits a series of minor offenses or a major offense, he/she may be excluded the following school year or immediately. The Conduct Probation status may be lifted the following school year if no offense has been made and the student has manifested the expected behavior.

### III. Conference with Subject Teachers

Conference with Subject Teacher is done if a student commits an academic-related offense such as no assignment, no book, or transferring seats. After a series of conferences and the student still commits an academic-related offense for the third time, inside or outside the classroom, the case may be referred to the Office for Student Discipline.

### IV. Due Process

The school recognizes students' right to due process through conference and dialogue before implementing a disciplinary action. The school conducts due inquiry or verification of students' cases, wherein parties involved are given the chance to present their sides.

The Discipline Officer is authorized by the school to call students involved, who will be asked to write a narrative account of the incident.

Considering that high school students are of minor age, parents or legal guardians of students involved are invited to the Office for Student Discipline to be informed of the case and to promptly answer charges against them, if they so desire. The basic principles of due process shall be in place.

In some major offenses, the discipline committee may be convened by the Director for Student Services to carefully assess the case and reach a decision. This committee is composed of the DSS, Director for Spiritual Affairs, Principal, Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer, and Guidance Counselor.

After the discipline committee has reached a decision, a final conference facilitated by the Director for Student Services, Principal and Prefect of Discipline will be made with the student and his/her parents.

A student who has committed an offense may also be referred to the Guidance Office if, after due verification and consideration, counseling is deemed more appropriate than disciplinary action.

### V. Attendance and Absences

Every student is expected to be regular and punctual in attending classes. As perfect attendance is the desired goal, students shall not absent themselves without a valid reason.

For every absence in class, there must be a justification duly signed by parents or legal guardian using the student's diary. The Discipline Officer must countersign this before the student is re-admitted to class.

Absences caused by prolonged illness (3 days or more) should be supported by an official medical certificate duly signed by a registered physician.

Students who report to school after recess and after lunch are considered absent for one half day and one whole day, respectively.

Habitual tardiness, especially during the first period in the morning and in the afternoon, shall not be allowed. Teachers concerned shall call for the parent or guardian of the student or visit them at home. Students who have been absent shall not be admitted to their classes unless they present an admission slip from the Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer.

Parents of students who wish to pull out their child from class must bring a letter noted by the Homeroom Adviser and approved by the Principal, and secure a gate pass from the Office for Student Discipline.

Excused absences do not exempt any student from accomplishing the work covered in class during his absence, as well as homework and other assignments given to other students.

**Reasons for excused and unexcused absences:**

*1. Excused Absences:* (1.1) serious illness; (1.2) crisis in the student's family (death or accident) or observance of a family event which requires the student's presence; (1.3) late enrollment; (1.4) performing official function; (1.5) entrance examination.

*2. Unexcused Absences:* (2.1) absences due to medical/dental appointment; (2.2) early departure for vacation or prolonged holiday without permission from the Principal; (2.3) cutting classes, leaving the campus without permission; (2.4) regularly occurring absence caused by headache or toothache; (2.5) serving of disciplinary action; (2.6) research, completion of written work or assignments; (2.7) inquiring for school admission/application.

**Note:** Other cases or reasons of absence not enumerated above are subject to verification by the Principal and the Prefect of Discipline.

**3. Monitoring and Probation**

3.1. **Attendance Monitoring** — A period during which a student who has accumulated a total of 10-15 absences/tardiness (excused or unexcused) at the end of a school year will be closely monitored the following school year. The monitoring shall be lifted only if the student has shown improvement in attendance.

3.2. **Attendance Probation** — A status in which a student is placed if he incurs a total of 16 or more absences/tardiness (excused or unexcused) at the end of a school year. During probation, 5 unexcused absences/tardiness will be counted as a major offense with a corresponding disciplinary action of 1 day working suspension. The status shall only be lifted for the next school year if the student has incurred less than 10 days of absence.

**4. Admission (Re-admission after Absence)**

Any student who, for unavoidable circumstances, absents himself/herself from class must submit an excuse letter, accompanied by pertinent documents (e.g. Medical Certificate or letter of consent from the parents or guardian), to the Discipline Officer, who will issue the admission slip. The admission slip must be presented to the teacher concerned not later than the second session following the return of the student. Excuses are for the time missed only. All work (quizzes, exams) covered by the class during the absence shall be made up within a reasonable time from the date of absence. Teachers will not allow any student to attend classes if he is not in proper uniform, is late, or was previously absent without an admission slip.

# APPENDIX A — MANUAL OF REGULATIONS FOR PRIVATE SCHOOLS

**Section 66. Tuition Charges**

A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged ten (10%) percent of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the first week of classes, or twenty percent if within the second week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the second week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to a justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.

The foregoing tuition fee schedules are based on the premise that a student who enrolls in a school stays enrolled for the entire school year or semester, regardless of subsequent transfer or withdrawal.

Section 66, MRPS and Section 104, TVET Manual, protect schools which accept tuition fee payments on installment — i.e., where a down payment is given upon enrollment and the balance is paid before examinations — by requiring students who transfer or withdraw from the course to first settle their accounts before the school issues transfer credentials. Per Section 62(e) of the MRPS and Section 124(e), TVET Manual, a school can legally collect outstanding tuition fees from a student who has transferred or withdrawn only if he was officially enrolled — meaning the student must have made at least an initial payment of school fees which the school accepted, authorizing him to participate in classes. Without these attending circumstances, there is no legal basis for the collection of tuition fee charges, since no contractual relationship between student and school was established.

A student who has paid in full or for any length longer than one month and transfers or withdraws in the first week after classes begin shall be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term (one semester for the collegiate level and TVET courses, one year for elementary and secondary levels); if he withdraws in the second week he will be charged 20%; if he withdraws any time after the second week, he may be fully charged for all fees applicable for the entire term. These charges hold whether or not he actually participated in his classes, because attendance is not the issue — what matters is that the school actually authorized him to attend.

For purposes of determination, the day of the student's submission of his written notice of withdrawal shall be considered the date of withdrawal. Verbal notice is not enough.

If the tuition fees, registration, library and laboratory fees already paid equal the amount still to be paid for the first month (or less) in a monthly installment scheme, no refund shall be given to the student when he withdraws after the registration period. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to justifiable cause, the student shall only be charged the pertinent fees up to the last month of attendance.

An existing DepEd ruling states that any promissory note signed in favor of a school by a student below 18 years old is null and void. It does not constitute the "payment of some kind" needed to establish enrollment unless a responsible relative or legal guardian acts as surety, since a person below 18 years old is a minor and not legally competent to contract such a financial obligation. Even a student of majority age but still dependent on parents or a guardian for support would not be considered competent by the DepEd to sign a promissory note; such a note would likewise be null and void unless a responsible relative or guardian appears as surety. Although the MRPS and the TVET Manual no longer provide for this, it is submitted that students are entitled to a full refund of fees for any course or subject that has been discontinued by the school, or which was not credited by the DepEd, CHED or TESDA.

# APPENDIX G — CONSTITUTION AND BY-LAWS OF THE STUDENT BODY ORGANIZATION OF ROGATIONIST COLLEGE — JUNIOR HIGH SCHOOL DEPARTMENT

**Preamble**

We, the students of Rogationist College Junior High School, with the help of God, and believing in the need for a better organized student organization, and in the development of the youth as future leaders of the nation, hereby form a unified student organization that shall embody the ideals and principles of democracy, in collective efforts to promote the welfare of all students and the academic standards of our Alma Mater, do hereby promulgate and adopt this constitution and by-laws that will promote, implement, and maintain our goals and aspirations.

### Article I — General Provisions

Sec. 1. This Constitution and By-Laws shall be known as the Constitution of the Student Body Organization of Rogationist College — Junior High School Department.

Sec. 2. For purposes of this Constitution and By-Laws, SBO refers to the Student Body Organization of Rogationist College Junior High School Department.

### Article II — Name and Domicile

Sec. 1. The name of the organization shall be known as the Student Body Organization.

Sec. 2. The office of the SBO shall be located inside the perimeter of the school.

### Article III — Declaration of Principles and Objectives

Sec. 1. The SBO of Rogationist College — Junior High School Department shall promote mutual understanding through social, civic, intellectual, spiritual, recreational and scientific programs and activities.

Sec. 2. The SBO shall have the following objectives:
a. Develop love of God and country, moral character, personal discipline, leadership and efficiency among its members
b. Train members for effective and efficient leadership
c. Help the students develop self-confidence
d. Promote unity among leaders and the student body
e. Maintain school aspirations to promote quality education and academic excellence
f. Serve and protect students' rights and welfare
g. Represent the studentry in the policy-making body of the school concerning students' welfare

### Article IV — Membership

Sec. 1. All bonafide students of the school are members of the studentry to whom the SBO shall be accountable at all times.

### Article V — Rights of Students

Sec. 1. Every student has the right to enjoy responsible freedom of speech and expression.
Sec. 2. Every student has the right to avail of all the services offered by the SBO.
Sec. 3. Every student has the right to conduct and participate in all school activities.
Sec. 4. Every student has the right to be informed about all the issues and matters concerning them.
Sec. 5. Every student has the right to vote, to be nominated and be elected into office.
Sec. 6. Every student has the right to be represented in the different meetings and assemblies of the school on issues affecting the welfare of the studentry.
Sec. 7. Every student has the right to give comments and suggestions about the activities and projects offered by the SBO.
Sec. 8. Every student has the right to due process.

### Article VI — Duties and Obligations of Students

Sec. 1. Every student has the responsibility to observe the laws of the Republic of the Philippines, and the rules and regulations of the school at all times.
Sec. 2. Every student must pay on time all SBO fees as approved by the School Principal or by the Parents-Teachers Association in an appropriate resolution.
Sec. 3. Every student must support and promote the thrusts and objectives of the SBO and the School.
Sec. 4. Every student must abide by the SBO constitution and by-laws.
Sec. 5. Every student must participate actively in all school activities and strive for academic excellence.
Sec. 6. Every student must exercise his/her rights and do his/her responsibilities as an SBO member.
Sec. 7. Every student must illustrate and perform social equity.

### Article VII — Powers and Duties of the Student Body Organization

Sec. 1. The Student Body Organization shall be the highest governing body of the entire studentry.

Sec. 2. The SBO shall have the following functions and responsibilities:
a. Formulate and recommend programs that will address relevant issues/concerns of the studentry;
b. Create committees necessary to address the needs of the students;
c. Monitor and evaluate the students' activities in the school and in the community;
d. Plan and implement policies and programs approved by school authorities designed to protect and promote students' rights and welfare;
e. Make recommendations to school authorities regarding student matters, affairs and activities;
f. Serve as a representative of the studentry in voicing their opinions, suggestions and grievances;
g. Participate in the crafting/formulation of the School Improvement Plans (SIPs);
h. Act as Coordinating Council of all campus co-curricular and extra-curricular organizations;
i. Have such other powers and duties as the school authorities and the studentry may, from time to time, grant or delegate, consistent with stated principles, objectives and school policies;
j. Spearhead academic, community and environmental programs in the school;
k. Monitor and supervise the election of SBO officers for the succeeding school year;
l. Turn over financial assets, papers, documents, properties and other responsibilities to the incoming SBO officers; and
m. Submit financial and accomplishment reports to the school authorities on a monthly basis. A terminal report must be submitted to the school authorities and the incoming SBO officers during the turn-over of responsibilities.

### Article VIII — Composition, Election and Term of Office of the SBO

Sec. 1. The officers of the SBO are the duly elected President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Auditor, Public Information Officer, Business Manager, Peace Officer, Grade Level Chairperson (if applicable) and the Grade Level Representatives.

Sec. 2. The Grade Level Representative who gets the highest number of votes among the elected Grade Level Representatives in their respective year level shall serve as the Grade Level Chairperson of the year level concerned.

Sec. 3. All SBO elections shall be conducted school-wide every third and/or fourth week of February annually.

Sec. 4. Elections for the Grade 7 Level Representative/s shall be conducted every 1st week of July annually.

Sec. 5. Campaign period shall not be more than five (5) school days prior to the SBO elections.

Sec. 6. Candidates for all SBO elective positions must be:
a. Bonafide students;
b. Of good academic standing with a general average of 85 and above without any failing grade during the 3rd grading period of the current school year;
c. Of good moral character and have not been subject to any disciplinary sanction;
d. Resident of the school for at least one (1) academic year prior to the filing of Certificate of Candidacy, except candidates for Grade 7 Level Representative/s;
e. Considered resigned and ineligible from all elective or designate positions from clubs and organizations once elected.

Sec. 7. The officers of the SBO shall hold office for one academic year.

### Article IX — Duties and Functions of Officers

Sec. 1. The **President** shall be the chief executive officer of the SBO. Duties: (a) preside over all meetings and/or designate another officer to preside for a specific meeting; (b) enforce this Constitution, By-Laws and other regulations that may be promulgated; (c) sign all official minutes, resolutions, and other official papers of the SBO; (d) represent the SBO or designate a representative for any external or internal affairs/functions; (e) head the Coordinating Council of Campus Co-Curricular Organizations; (f) implement SBO programs and projects; (g) create Ad Hoc committees, as the need arises, the Chairperson of which shall be appointed by the SBO President; and (h) perform such other functions inherent and incidental to the office.

Sec. 2. The **Vice President** duties: (a) assist the President in all matters where assistance is necessary; (b) assume the Office of the President should the position become vacant; (c) supervise members in planning and arranging meetings or programs of activities; (d) head the Internal Affairs Committee and conceptualize programs and projects and plan activities with the Year Level Representative; and (e) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 3. The **Secretary** duties: (a) keep accurate records of the minutes and document proceedings of every meeting; (b) keep a file of all pertinent documents and papers of the SBO and make them accessible to the studentry; (c) call and prepare all notices of SBO meetings; (d) head the SBO Secretariat; and (e) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 4. The **Treasurer** duties: (a) keep all financial records of the SBO; (b) serve as the disbursing officer of all the SBO's funds; (c) prepare the annual budget of the SBO; (d) prepare financial reports every month, after an activity, and at the end of term; (e) head the Finance Committee; (f) formulate pertinent financial guidelines for the organization; and (g) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 5. The **Auditor** duties: (a) certify the legitimacy and correctness of the disbursement of funds; (b) audit all expenditures of the SBO funds; (c) assist the treasurer in formulating guidelines and reports; (d) keep and update inventory of all the SBO's property; (e) act as the assistant head of the Finance Committee; and (f) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 6. The **Public Information Officer** duties: (a) popularize the thrusts and objectives of the SBO; (b) build and maintain a credible image of the SBO; (c) take charge of promotion of SBO projects and activities; (d) head the Publicity Committee; and (e) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 7. The **Business Manager** duties: (a) transact approved business deals inside and/or outside establishments and report the same to the SBO; (b) take charge of all purchases of the SBO; (c) submit project proposals and screen proposals submitted by other officers before presentation to the body; and (d) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 8. The **Peace Officer** duties: (a) help the presiding officer maintain peace and order during meetings; (b) act as disciplinary officer, in deed; (c) maintain peace and order within the premises of the school; (d) act as chief peace officer and overall sergeant-at-arms of subordinate organizations and classes; (e) chair the Students' Welfare Committee and appoint its members; and (f) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

Sec. 9. The **Grade Level Chairperson/s** shall act as the grade level head coordinator of all SBO programs and projects in his/her grade level, apart from other duties.

Sec. 10. The **Grade Level Representative/s** duties: (a) represent his/her grade level in all meetings of the SBO; (b) serve as the grievance desk for their respective grade level; (c) conceptualize and implement programs and projects for their respective grade level; (d) assist in the effective implementation of the SBO's programs and projects; and (e) perform such other duties assigned by the SBO.

### Article X — Permanent Committees in the Student Body Organization

Sec. 1. There shall be seven (7) permanent committees in the SBO: the Executive Committee, Internal Affairs Committee, Secretariat, Finance Committee, Publicity Committee, Students' Welfare Committee and the Special Projects Committee. Members of each committee shall be appointed by the President from among the officers of the homeroom organizations upon the recommendation of the chairperson of the committee.

Sec. 2. The Executive Committee shall be headed by the President and shall act as the administrative arm of the SBO.

Sec. 3. The Internal Affairs Committee shall be headed by the Vice President and shall take charge of all matters regarding the members of the SBO and their functions.

Sec. 4. The Secretariat shall be headed by the Secretary and shall be the official SBO work group.

Sec. 5. The Finance Committee shall be headed by the Treasurer and assisted by the Auditor. This committee shall take charge of all monetary matters and properties of the SBO.

Sec. 6. The Publicity Committee shall be headed by the Public Information Officer and shall be responsible for disseminating information on matters concerning the SBO.

Sec. 7. The Students' Welfare Committee shall be headed by the Peace Officer and shall look after the rights of the students and act upon their complaints.

Sec. 8. The Special Projects Committee shall be headed by a person appointed by the President and shall implement programs and projects of the SBO.

Sec. 9. Other committees, permanent or ad hoc in nature, may be created by the SBO as the need arises.

### Article XI — Homeroom Class Organizations

Sec. 1. There shall be a homeroom class organization in every section composed of officers parallel to the organizational structure of the SBO, including sergeant-at-arms, muse and escort.

Sec. 2. The homeroom class organization shall be responsible for implementing programs and projects of each class.

Sec. 3. The homeroom class organization shall assist the SBO in implementing its programs and projects.

Sec. 4. The homeroom class organization shall be guided by the designated class adviser who must be part of the teaching staff of the school.

### Article XII — Coordinating Council of Campus Co-Curricular Organizations

Sec. 1. The Coordinating Council shall be headed by the President of the Student Body Organization as the Chairperson.

Sec. 2. The Coordinating Council shall be composed of the different Presidents of all recognized legitimate campus co-curricular organizations.

Sec. 3. Functions: (a) coordinate school-wide activities requiring the participation and involvement of the whole studentry; (b) provide a consultative mechanism and support for implementation of the various programs and projects of all co-curricular organizations; (c) plan programs and synchronize student activities in the campus.

Sec. 4. The Coordinating Council shall meet once a month or upon notice of the Chairperson or the request of any of its members, or as often as deemed necessary.

Sec. 5. The School Principal shall serve as the Adviser of the Coordinating Council. All resolutions and agreements arrived at by the Coordinating Council shall be noted by the School Principal.

### Article XIII — Student Body Organization Adviser

Sec. 1. The SBO Adviser shall preferably be the teacher in Araling Panlipunan or any competent, able and willing teacher designated by the School Principal.

Sec. 2. The SBO Adviser shall monitor all programs, projects, activities and meetings of the SBO at all times.

Sec. 3. The Advisorship in the SBO shall be equivalent to one teaching load (DepEd Order No. 43, 2002).

### Article XIV — Commission on Elections

Sec. 1. The Commission on Elections, herein referred to as the SBO COMELEC, shall be established within forty-five (45) days before the day of election.

Sec. 2. The SBO COMELEC shall be the only agency that will manage the electoral process, including the campaign.

Sec. 3. The SBO COMELEC shall be composed of eleven (11) members represented by each grade level who do not have vested interest in the election, or in any way related to any of the candidates, and who shall select a chairperson among themselves.

Sec. 4. The composition of the members shall be three (3) from the Grade 7 Level (incoming Grade 8), four (4) from the Grade 8 Level (incoming Grade 9) and four (4) from the Grade 9 Level (incoming Grade 10).

Sec. 5. The members of the SBO COMELEC shall be appointed by the adviser of the SBO.

Sec. 6. Duties and responsibilities: (a) conduct fair, honest, and systematic elections; (b) validate the electoral proceedings and results; (c) accept or revoke candidacy; (d) proclaim the new set of officers; (e) decide upon protest relative to the conduct and results of elections; (f) keep all pertinent election papers/documents; (g) prepare and distribute the necessary election paraphernalia, certificate of candidacy forms and other election-related materials before, during and after the election; and (h) disqualify candidates who have violated any of the promulgated guidelines.

### Article XV — The General Assembly

Sec. 1. The General Assembly of the SBO shall be composed of all elected Homeroom Class Organization Presidents of the school.

Sec. 2. The General Assembly shall be a forum for information and consultations, whose decisions shall be significantly considered.

Sec. 3. The General Assembly shall be convened by the President once every two months starting in the month of July and as the need arises.

### Article XVI — Meetings and Quorum

Sec. 1. The SBO shall conduct regular meetings every first week of the month or as agreed upon by the officers of the SBO.

Sec. 2. Special meetings of the SBO may be called upon by the President or by a majority of the SBO officers.

Sec. 3. Majority of the officers of the SBO (50%+1) shall constitute a quorum.

Sec. 4. Each officer of the SBO is entitled to only one vote. No proxy shall be allowed when voting.

### Article XVII — Finance

Sec. 1. The SBO's funds shall be classified into Trust funds and Special funds. Trust funds shall consist of the SBO Membership Fee, the amount of which shall be recommended by the SBO in consultation with and approval of the PTA but in no case shall exceed the limit set by school administrators in an appropriate official issuance. Special funds are funds that come from donations, sponsorship, fund-raising projects or any amount derived from legitimate sources.

Sec. 2. The SBO shall enjoy the freedom to determine its priorities in the disbursement of its share in the SBO Membership Fee.

Sec. 3. The SBO may undertake fund-raising activities to subsidize its projects or activities.

Sec. 4. The organization can accept cash donations from any member or any public or private person or institution, as long as it will not affect the integrity of the SBO.

Sec. 5. The SBO President and the Treasurer shall be the signatories of all fund disbursement of the SBO. All fund disbursement must be accompanied by an SBO resolution duly noted by the SBO Adviser and all expenses supported by official receipts. The School Principal shall be provided a copy of all resolutions relative to the disbursements made.

Sec. 6. The SBO, through the Treasurer and the Auditor, shall promulgate financial guidelines for proper financial management.

Sec. 7. Funds of the SBO shall be audited at the end of the term, or at the end of every activity.

Sec. 8. Financial statements shall be posted on the SBO bulletin board every month and printed in every issue of the school paper.

Sec. 9. The balance of the Student Body Organization Fund for a specific term shall be added to the fund for the next term.

Sec. 10. For all purposes of this fund, the incumbent officers and their successors must use this fund to benefit the students in general.

### Article XVIII — Impeachment, Resignation and Vacancies

Sec. 1. The officers of the SBO may be impeached on the following grounds: (a) culpable violations of the Constitution and By-laws; (b) gross misconduct, violence to a person in authority, negligence, and disloyalty to the cause of the SBO and the school; (c) non-attendance in regular meetings for six (6) consecutive times; and (d) abuse or misuse of power and authority.

Sec. 2. Two-thirds (2/3) vote of the General Assembly shall be necessary to decide after hearing a case of impeachment.

Sec. 3. The decision of the General Assembly shall be final. However, the officer charged shall be informed twenty (20) days prior to his impeachment case proceedings of the charge/charges against him/her, to afford the opportunity to be heard with or without counsel in his/her defense.

Sec. 4. Resignation shall be in writing and subject to the approval of the SBO officers.

Sec. 5. Any vacancy in any of the positions due to death, resignation, or impeachment shall be filled, subject to the recommendation of the SBO.

Sec. 6. Any vacancy in the SBO, except the position of the President, shall be filled within thirty (30) days from the day the position is rendered or considered vacant, by appointment of the President from among the officers of the SBO. Immediately upon appointment, the officer appointed shall serve the remaining period.

Sec. 7. The SBO COMELEC shall certify as to the vacancy occurring in the SBO, and the SBO Adviser shall certify the fact of appointment of the officer to the vacant position.

### Article XIX — Amendments

Sec. 1. This Constitution and By-Laws may be amended or modified in full or in part once every three (3) years from its official issuance through a resolution.

Sec. 2. The SBO may propose amendments. Such proposal shall be carried through a resolution duly signed and concurred in by majority vote of the SBO officers and the General Assembly concerned, voting separately.

Sec. 3. Proposed Amendments shall be transmitted to the Student Affairs Coordinator (SAC) at least one (1) month before the holding of the Leadership Training Seminar for student organization officers, or any venue where the same shall be submitted for ratification.

Sec. 4. Amendments to the Constitution and By-Laws shall take effect immediately upon ratification and approval of the school administrators.

# APPENDIX H — RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING SEXUAL HARASSMENT

**KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:**

Whereas, Rogationist College is aware of the mandate of the 1987 Philippine Constitution as to the role of educational institutions to inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, respect for human rights, appreciation of the roles of national heroes in the historical development of the country, teach the rights and duties of citizenship, strengthen ethical and spiritual values, develop moral character and personal discipline, encourage critical and creative thinking, broaden scientific and technical knowledge, and promote vocational efficiency (Article XIV, Section 3(2));

Whereas, the State, to protect the rights of individuals more particularly against sexual harassment especially in educational institutions, promulgated Republic Act 7877, otherwise known as "the Sexual Harassment Act of 1995," which states it is the duty of the employer to provide proper decorum in the workplace and educational institution, and to provide the procedure for the settlement, resolution or prosecution of any act involving sexual harassment;

Whereas, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012 on May 14, 2012, otherwise known as "DepEd Child Protection Policy," with respect to Policy and Guidelines on Protecting Children in School from Abuse, Violence, Exploitation, Discrimination, Bullying and other Forms of Abuse;

Whereas, Rogationist College, in order to comply with the different laws, rules and regulations in relation to Sexual Harassment, formulated its own rules of procedure in addressing complaints on sexual harassment;

Thus, the foregoing rule is hereby adopted.

**Section 1. Short Title** — This shall be known as "RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING SEXUAL HARASSMENT."

**Section 2. State Policy** — It is the mandate of the 1987 Philippine Constitution for educational institutions to inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, respect for human rights, appreciation of the roles of national heroes in the historical development of the country, teach the rights and duties of citizenship, strengthen ethical and spiritual values, develop moral character and personal discipline, encourage critical and creative thinking, broaden scientific and technical knowledge, and promote vocational efficiency (Article XIV, Section 3(2)). It is the duty of the State to protect the rights of individuals against sexual harassment especially in educational institutions, per Republic Act 7877. In order to protect the rights of individuals in educational institutions, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, which Rogationist College hereby adopts in toto.

**Section 3. School Policy** — It is the objective of Rogationist College to provide not only good and quality education but also to inculcate the values and teachings of its founder, Fr. Hannibal de Francia, in relation to morals, proper decorum and good behavior.

It is the policy of Rogationist College to free its environment from all acts of sexual harassment and condemn any act pertinent or related thereto, for it is highly unacceptable behavior, contrary to law and morals. However, considering the effect on the welfare of the child or employment of individuals complained of, Rogationist College will undertake to consider all aspects of the complaint and all proceedings shall be treated with strict confidentiality.

Rogationist College shall consider due process in handling cases involving sexual harassment to protect not only the rights of the complainant but also of the person being charged, without prejudice to complying with the reportorial requirement mandated by Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, and separate criminal cases that may be instituted.

**Section 4. Definition of Terms**

a. **Child** — refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age or those over but unable to fully take care of themselves from abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation or discrimination because of physical or mental disability or condition, including all students who are above 18 years of age but are enrolled in Rogationist College.

b. **School personnel** — means any person who works for Rogationist College, whether as: (b.1) school head — the chief executive officer or administrator of a school or learning center; (b.2) other school officials — those occupying supervisory positions or positions of responsibility, involved in policy formulation or implementation; (b.3) academic personnel — all school personnel formally engaged in actual teaching service or research assignments, full or part-time, as well as those possessing prescribed academic functions directly supportive of teaching, such as registrars, librarians, guidance counselors, researchers and similar persons; (b.4) other personnel — all other non-academic personnel in the school, regardless of the nature of their appointment and status of employment (per DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012).

c. **Sexual Harassment** (per R.A. 7877) — is committed by an employer, employee, manager, supervisor, agent of the employer, teacher, coach, trainor, instructor, professor, or any other person who, having authority, influence or moral ascendancy over another in a work, training or education environment, demands, requests or otherwise requires any sexual favor from the other, regardless of whether the demand, request or requirement for submission is accepted by the object of the Act.

   c.1. In a work-related or employment environment, sexual harassment is committed when: (1) the sexual favor is made a condition in the hiring or employment, re-employment or continued employment of said individual, or in granting said individual favorable compensation, terms of employment, promotions, or privileges, or the refusal to grant the sexual favor results in limiting, segregating or classifying the employee in a way that would discriminate, deprive or diminish employment opportunities or otherwise adversely affect said employee; (2) the above acts would impair the employee's rights or privileges under existing labor laws; or (3) the above acts would result in an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment for the employee.

   c.2. In an education or training environment, sexual harassment is committed: (1) against one who is under the care, custody or supervision of the offender; (2) against one whose education, training, apprenticeship or tutorship is entrusted to the offender; (3) when the sexual favor is made a condition to the giving of a passing grade, the granting of honors and scholarships, or the payment of a stipend, allowance or other benefits, privileges, or consideration; or (4) when the sexual advances result in an intimidating, hostile or offensive environment for the student, trainee or apprentice.

   Any person who directs or induces another to commit any act of sexual harassment as herein defined, or who cooperates in its commission by another without which it would not have been committed, shall also be held liable under this Act.

d. **Sexual Violence** — refers to acts which are sexual in nature, including but not limited to: (d.1) rape, sexual harassment, lasciviousness, demeaning and sexually suggestive remarks, physically attacking the sexual parts of the victim's body; (d.2) forcing the child to watch obscene publications and indecent shows, or to do indecent sexual acts and/or engage in the creation or distribution of such films or materials; (d.3) acts causing or attempting to cause the child to engage in any sexual activity by force, threat of force, physical or other harm or threat thereof, or coercion, or through inducements, gifts or favor; (d.4) other acts of sexual nature that are prejudicial to the interest of students and personnel.

**Section 5. Procedure — Procedures for Filing a Complaint with the Discipline and Grievance Committee**

1. A written complaint shall be filed as follows: (a) by the complainant himself to the Office of the Rector, if the complainant is a personnel of the school or is above 18 years of age; (b) in the event the complainant or aggrieved party is a child, the written incident report submitted by any student, personnel, parent, guardian or guidance counselor who has knowledge of the incident shall be treated as a complaint, immediately submitted to the Office of the Rector; (c) by the complainant, though a minor, but with the conformity of a parent, guardian, teacher or guidance counselor.
2. After receipt of the complaint, the Office of the Rector has the authority to call the parties to the case for possible amicable settlement.
3. In the event there was failure of settlement, the Office of the Rector shall call for the convocation of the grievance committee, composed of: Chairman — Director of Student Services; Members — Director of Spiritual Affairs, Dean/TED Head/Principal, Coordinator for Guidance & Counseling Office, HRD (when a school employee is involved), Prefect of Discipline (when a student is involved). The complaint/incident report shall be endorsed to the chairman of the grievance committee.
4. After convocation of the Grievance Committee, the chairman shall send a copy of the complaint/incident report to the respondent/s, who shall be given five (5) working days to answer in writing, informed of their right to the services of counsel in preparing the Answer.
5. If the respondent/s fail to answer within five (5) days from receipt, this shall be treated as a waiver of the right to file an Answer, and the Committee shall proceed with the case ex-parte. However, should the respondent fail to reply within the prescribed period due to justifiable reasons, a non-extendable period of three (3) days shall be granted.
6. The Chair shall act as the presiding officer during the hearing, commencing proceedings by reading the complaint/s and reply/ies filed before the Committee.
7. In the presentation of evidence, the parties shall be given the right to present their respective evidence and witnesses before the body. Witnesses are subject to cross-examination by the Committee but not by the other party.
8. In the event a new witness files a statement in support of the complaint during the investigation, the respondent shall be given five (5) days to file an answer to rebut or refute the claim, which shall be forwarded to the complainant.
9. In the presentation of evidence, the complainant shall first present his/her side, followed by the respondent/s.
10. The Committee shall submit its findings and recommendations to the Rector, who in turn will submit the same to the House Council for final decision.
11. In all cases, the decisions of the House Council shall be final and executed by the Rector.

**Section 6. Penalty** — The penalty of reprimand, suspension or termination may be imposed based on the gravity of the charge complained of.

**Section 7. Confidentiality clause** — All proceedings and records of the case shall be treated with utmost confidentiality, especially the identity of the victim if he/she is a minor.

**Section 8. Reportorial Requirement of Department of Education** — A report concerning the case must be prepared by the Human Resource Development and Management Office if it involves school personnel. For cases involving student/s, it is the duty of the Director for Student and Personnel Services to prepare the report to the Department of Education.

# APPENDIX I — RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES OF BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE

**KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:**

Whereas, Rogationist College, as a Catholic School, acknowledges its obligations to exercise reasonable supervision over the conduct of the student and to cultivate the best potentialities of the heart and mind of students by committing to the formation of its members towards love of God and neighbor and promotion of proper decorum, good behavior and positive values;

Whereas, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, on May 14, 2012, otherwise known as "DepEd Child Protection Policy," with respect to Policy and Guidelines on Protecting Children in School from Abuse, Violence, Exploitation, Discrimination, Bullying and other Forms of Abuse;

Whereas, in line with the mandate of curtailing bullying inside and outside the school, REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10627, or the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013," was passed to address growing concerns of bullying, and Rogationist College is one in promoting zero-tolerance on bullying;

Whereas, Rogationist College, in order to comply with the different laws, rules and regulations in relation to bullying, formulated its own rules of procedure in addressing complaints on bullying;

Thus the foregoing rule is hereby adopted.

**Section 1. Short Title** — This shall be known as "RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE."

**Section 2. State Policy** — It is the mandate of the 1987 Philippine Constitution for educational institutions to inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, respect for human rights, appreciation of the roles of national heroes in the historical development of the country, teach the rights and duties of citizenship, strengthen ethical and spiritual values, develop moral character and personal discipline, encourage critical and creative thinking, broaden scientific and technical knowledge, and promote vocational efficiency (Article XIV, Section 3(2)). In order to protect the rights of individuals in educational institutions, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, as well as Republic Act No. 10627, the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013," which Rogationist College hereby adopts in toto.

**Section 3. School Policy** — It is the objective of Rogationist College to provide not only good and quality education but also to inculcate the values and teachings of its founder, St. Hannibal Mary di Francia, in relation to morals, proper decorum and good behavior.

It is the policy of Rogationist College to free its environment from all acts of bullying and condemn any act pertinent or related thereto, for it is highly unacceptable behavior, contrary to law and morals. However, considering the effects on the welfare of the child and employment of individuals, Rogationist College undertakes to consider all aspects of the complaint and all proceedings shall be treated with strict confidentiality.

Rogationist College shall consider due process in handling cases involving bullying to protect not only the rights of the complainant but also of the person being charged, without prejudice to complying with the reportorial requirement mandated by Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, and separate criminal cases that may be instituted.

**Section 4. Definition of Terms**

"**Bullying**" refers to any severe or repeated use by one or more students of a written, verbal or electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at another student, that has the effect of actually causing or placing the latter in reasonable fear of physical or emotional harm or damage to his property; creating a hostile environment at school for the other student; infringing on the rights of another student at school; or materially and substantially disrupting the education process or the orderly operation of a school.

"**Bullied**" or "**Victim**" — any student who experiences the acts of bullying or retaliation as defined by the Act or this IRR.

"**Bystander**" — any person who witnesses or has personal knowledge of any actual or perceived acts or incidents of bullying or retaliation as defined by this IRR.

"**Learning center**" — refers to learning resources and facilities of a learning program for out-of-school youth and adults as defined in DepEd Order No. 43, s. 2013.

"**Service provider**" — any person who is not a teacher or school personnel but who works in the school, such as, but not limited to, security guards, canteen personnel, utility workers, and transportation service personnel.

"**Student**" — a person who attends classes in any level of basic education, including a pupil or learner as defined in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012.

"**Prompt Report**" — refers to prompt reporting of an incident of bullying from the first day of classes of the school year until the final report card distribution of the same school year, in order to maximize intervention, reformation and/or imposition of appropriate sanction/s of the school.

**Section 5. Forms of Bullying**, such as, but not limited to:

1. Any unwanted physical contact between the bully and the victim like punching, pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, inflicting school pranks, teasing, fighting and the use of available objects as weapons;
2. Any act that causes damage to a victim's psyche and/or emotional well-being;
3. Any slanderous statement or accusation that causes the victim undue emotional distress like directing foul language or profanity at the target, name-calling, tormenting and commenting negatively on the victim's looks, clothes and body;
4. Cyberbullying or any bullying done through the use of technology or any electronic means, including through texting, email, instant messaging, chatting, internet, social media, online games, or other platforms or formats as defined in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012; bullying at a location, activity, function or program that is not school-related and through the use of technology or an electronic device or other forms of media that is not owned, leased or used by a school;
5. Social bullying — any deliberate, repetitive and aggressive social behavior intended to hurt others or belittle another individual or group;
6. Gender-based bullying — any act that humiliates or excludes a person on the basis of perceived or actual sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI);
7. Retaliation against a person who reports bullying, who provides information during an investigation of bullying, or who is a witness to or has reliable information about bullying.

**Section 6. Place of Commission of Bullying** — Bullying can be committed on the following places: (a) school grounds; (b) property immediately adjacent to school grounds; (c) school-sponsored or school-related activities, functions or programs whether on or off school grounds; (d) school bus stops; (e) school buses or other vehicles owned, leased or used by a school; or (f) school buses or school services privately-owned but accredited by the school.

**Section 7. Procedure**

**A. Immediate Responses**

1. A victim, bystander, or school personnel who receives information of a bullying incident or retaliation, or any person who witnesses or has personal knowledge of any incident, shall report the same to the Prefect of Discipline or Guidance Counselor.
2. The school personnel notified of a bullying incident shall intervene by: (i) stopping the bullying or retaliation immediately; (ii) separating the students involved; (iii) removing the victim or, in appropriate cases, the bully or offending student, from the site; (iv) ensuring the victim's safety by determining and addressing immediate safety needs and ensuring medical attention if needed (securing a medical certificate in cases of physical injury); (v) bringing the bully to the Guidance Office or Office for Student Discipline.

**B. Reporting the Bullying Incident or Retaliation** — A written complaint shall be filed by any of the following to the Office for Student Discipline: (1) by the complainant/victim himself if above 18 years of age or a personnel of the school; (2) in the event the complainant or aggrieved party is a child, the written incident report submitted by any student, personnel, parent, guardian, guidance counselor or anyone with knowledge of the incident shall be treated as a complaint, immediately submitted to the Office for Student Discipline; or (3) by the complainant, though minor, with the consent of a teacher, parent, legal guardian or guidance counselor.

The Prefect of Discipline who receives the report shall ask the victim, bystander, or school personnel who witnessed or has personal knowledge of the incident to fill up the Intake Sheet as provided in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012. The Prefect of Discipline shall inform the Principal and Rector of all reports of bullying received and of the actions taken in response.

**C. Fact-Finding and Documentation** — The Prefect of Discipline shall:
1. Separately interview in private the offending student/s, victim/s and all other individuals with vital information, in the presence of their respective homeroom adviser or guidance facilitator/counselor, with students asked to write down their account of the incident.
2. Determine the levels of threat and develop intervention strategies; if the incident requires immediate attention or high level of threat, appropriate action shall be taken within twenty-four (24) hours.
3. Inform the parents/legal guardian of the offending student and the victim, and personally confer with them to resolve any issue and restore, as far as practicable, the relationships of the parties involved.
4. Carefully and thoroughly study the case to determine appropriate interventions for the victim and offending student, or disciplinary measure to be imposed.
5. Submit to the Director of Student Services a comprehensive report on the results of the interview/verification, conference with parents/guardians, and appropriate interventions/disciplinary measure.
6. Make appropriate referral to the Child Protection Committee if the issue is not resolved among the parties or for further fact-finding.

**D. Due Process**

1. The Director for Student Services shall convene the Child Protection Committee, composed of: Director for Student Services (Chairperson), Guidance Counselor/Teacher (Vice Chairperson), Representative of the Teachers as designated by the Faculty Members, President of the Parents Teacher Association, President of the Student Body Organization.
2. The offending student/s and parent/s or legal guardian/s shall be informed of the complaint in writing.
3. The students shall be given the opportunity to answer the complaint in writing within the school ground, in the presence of the Child Protection Committee members, with the assistance of parents or legal guardian.
4. As a rule, the period to reply shall not be extendable except for justifiable reasons; said extension, if granted, shall not exceed three (3) calendar days.
5. In case of contradicting statements of material value, parties may be called by the Child Protection Committee and subjected to clarificatory questioning by the Committee members only, not by any of the parties.
6. The Committee shall submit its findings and recommendations to the Office of the Rector for a decision and/or imposition of appropriate sanction, if any.
7. Parents can appeal to the School Board within 10 calendar days from receipt of the decision. Pending appeal, an intervention program, but not disciplinary action, shall commence.
8. The decision of the School Board shall be final.

**E. Referral** — The Rector or the Child Protection Committee may refer the victim/s and bully/ies to trained professionals outside the school (social workers, psychologists, or child protection specialists) for further assessment and appropriate intervention, as necessary. The School Head or designated school personnel shall notify the Women and Children's Protection Desk (WCPD) of the local PNP if criminal charges may be pursued against the bully.

**Section 8. False Accusation of Bullying** — If a student, after investigation, is found to have knowingly made a false accusation of bullying, the student shall be subjected to disciplinary actions or appropriate interventions in accordance with existing rules and regulations.

**Section 9. Incident of Bullying or Retaliation Involving Students from More than One School** — If an incident involves students from more than one school, the school first informed shall promptly notify the appropriate administrator or school head of the other school so both schools may take appropriate action.

**Section 10. Anonymous Reporting** — Reports of incidents initiated by persons who prefer anonymity shall be entertained, and the reporter shall be afforded protection from possible retaliation; provided that no disciplinary administrative action shall be taken against an alleged bully solely on the basis of an anonymous report without any other evidence.

**Section 11. Role/Duty of Child Protection Committee** — In addition to duties under DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012: (a) conduct awareness-raising programs with school stakeholders in preventing and addressing bullying; (b) ensure the anti-bullying policy adopted by the school is implemented; (c) monitor all cases or incidents related to bullying reported or referred; (d) make necessary referrals to appropriate agencies, offices or persons.

**Section 12. Penalty** — Written reprimand, community service, suspension, exclusion or expulsion, in accordance with existing rules and regulations of the school or of the Department for public schools, may be imposed provided due process requirements are complied with. In addition, the bully shall be required to undergo an intervention program administered or supervised by the school's Child Protection Committee. Parents of the bully shall be encouraged to join the intervention program.

**Section 13. Jurisdiction** — Complaints of bullying and other acts of abuse under DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012 shall be within the exclusive jurisdiction of Rogationist College and shall not be brought for amicable settlement before the Barangay, subject to existing laws. Complaints for acts committed by persons not under the jurisdiction of the Disciplinary Authority of Rogationist College shall be referred to the appropriate authorities.

**Section 14. Confidentiality** — In bullying or peer abuse and other acts of abuse by a student, whether victim or offender, the student's identity or other identifying information shall be withheld from the public to protect his or her privacy.

**Section 15. Criminal and Civil Liability** — Criminal and civil liabilities arising from bullying and other acts of abuse are separate and distinct, and shall not bar the filing of an administrative case under these guidelines. If the incident resulted in serious physical injuries or death, the case shall be dealt with in accordance with Republic Act 9344 (the "Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act," as amended) and other applicable laws.

**Section 16. Repealing Clause** — All rules and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of the foregoing act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

**Section 17. Effectivity** — These rules and regulations shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication by posting copies in the school bulletin board or in any school-authorized publication, whichever comes first.

# APPENDIX J — ANTI-HAZING LAW OF THE REPUBLIC OF THE PHILIPPINES (Republic Act No. 8049)

*An Act Regulating Hazing and Other Forms of Initiation Rites in Fraternities, Sororities and Organizations and Providing Penalties Therefor.*

**Section 1.** Hazing, as used in this Act, is an initiation rite or practice as a prerequisite for admission into membership in a fraternity, sorority or organization by placing the recruit, neophyte or applicant in embarrassing or humiliating situations, such as forcing him to do menial, silly, foolish and other similar tasks or activities, or otherwise subjecting him to physical or psychological suffering or injury. The term "organization" shall include any club or the Armed Forces of the Philippines, Philippine National Police, Philippine Military Academy, or officer and cadet corps of the Citizen's Military Training and Citizen's Army Training. Physical, mental and psychological testing and training procedures to determine and enhance the fitness of prospective regular members of the AFP and PNP, as approved by the Secretary of National Defense and the National Police Commission, shall not be considered hazing under this Act.

**Section 2.** No hazing or initiation rites in any form or manner by a fraternity, sorority or organization shall be allowed without prior written notice to the school authorities or head of organization seven (7) days before the conduct of such initiation. The written notice shall indicate the period of the initiation activities, which shall not exceed three (3) days, include the names of those to be subjected to such activities, and contain an undertaking that no physical violence be employed during the initiation rites.

**Section 3.** The head of the school or organization or their representatives must assign at least two (2) representatives to be present during the initiation, whose duty is to see that no physical harm of any kind is inflicted upon a recruit, neophyte or applicant.

**Section 4.** If the person subjected to hazing suffers physical injury or dies as a result thereof, the officers and members of the fraternity, sorority or organization who actually participated in the infliction of physical harm shall be liable as principals. The penalties escalate depending on the consequence of the hazing:

1. *Reclusion perpetua* (life imprisonment) if death, rape, sodomy or mutilation results;
2. *Reclusion temporal* maximum period (17 years, 4 months and 1 day to 20 years) if the victim becomes insane, imbecile, impotent or blind;
3. *Reclusion temporal* medium period (14 years, 8 months and 1 day to 17 years and 4 months) if the victim loses the use of speech, hearing, smell, an eye, hand, foot, arm or leg, or becomes incapacitated for habitual work;
4. *Reclusion temporal* minimum period (12 years and 1 day to 14 years and 8 months) if the victim becomes deformed or loses any other body part or its use, or is incapacitated for more than ninety (90) days;
5. *Prision mayor* maximum period (10 years and 1 day to 12 years) if incapacitated for more than thirty (30) days;
6. *Prision mayor* medium period (8 years and 1 day to 10 years) if incapacitated for ten (10) days or more, or requiring medical assistance for the same period;
7. *Prision mayor* minimum period (6 years and 1 day to 8 years) if incapacitated from one (1) to nine (9) days, or requiring medical assistance for the same period;
8. *Prision correccional* maximum period (4 years, 2 months and 1 day to 6 years) if the victim sustains physical injuries which do not prevent him from engaging in habitual activity or work nor require medical attendance.

The responsible officials of the school or of the police, military or citizen's army training organization may impose appropriate administrative sanctions on the person(s) charged even before conviction. The maximum penalty is imposed in instances such as: (a) recruitment accompanied by force, violence, threat, intimidation or deceit on a recruit who refuses to join; (b) the recruit initially consents but is prevented from quitting upon learning hazing will occur; (c) the recruit is prevented from reporting the unlawful act to parents, school or police authorities through force, violence, threat or intimidation; (d) the hazing is committed outside the school; or (e) the victim is below twelve (12) years of age at the time of hazing.

The owner of the place where hazing is conducted shall be liable as an accomplice if he had actual knowledge but failed to act to prevent it. If the hazing is held in the home of an officer or member, the parents shall be held liable as principals if they had actual knowledge but failed to act. School authorities, including faculty members, who consent to or have actual knowledge of the hazing but fail to act shall be punished as accomplices. Officers, former officers, or alumni who actually planned the hazing (though not present) shall be liable as principals. A fraternity/sorority adviser present during the hazing who fails to act shall be liable as principal. Presence during hazing is *prima facie* evidence of participation as principal unless the person prevented the commission of the acts.

Any person charged under this provision shall not be entitled to the mitigating circumstance of no intention to commit so grave a wrong. This section shall apply to the president, manager, director or other responsible officer of a corporation engaged in hazing as a requirement for employment.

**Section 5.** If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions shall remain valid and effective.

**Section 6.** All laws, orders, rules or regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby amended or repealed accordingly.

**Section 7.** This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) calendar days after its publication in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.

*Approved: June 07, 1995 — (Signed) Fidel V. Ramos, President of the Philippines*

# APPENDIX K — DANGEROUS DRUGS BOARD REGULATION NO. 6, SERIES OF 2003

## General Guidelines for the Conduct of Random Drug Testing for Secondary and Tertiary Students

Pursuant to Section 36(c), Article III of Republic Act No. 9165, the following guidelines are hereby promulgated, applicable to the random drug testing of students in public and private secondary, tertiary/higher education institutions and post-secondary technical vocational schools. These guidelines outline the purposes of the random drug-testing program, as well as procedures and necessary consequences of a positive drug test result after confirmation.

All procedures shall take into account the ideals of fairness and rehabilitation, not isolation of the drug dependent. The school must not violate the constitutional rights to due process, equal protection and self-incrimination.

### A. Guiding Principles

1. Government recognizes the primary responsibility of the family, particularly parents, for the education and awareness of ill effects of dangerous drugs.
2. Parental involvement shall be maximized in the implementation of drug education, random drug testing, treatment and rehabilitation.
3. The school, with the assistance of LGUs and other agencies, has the obligation to employ every reasonable means to provide a healthy and drug-free environment.
4. Cognizant of the right of students to continue and complete their studies, government and schools shall emphasize guidance, counseling, treatment and rehabilitation of any student found to have used or be dependent on dangerous drugs.
5. Academic freedom of institutions of higher learning shall be respected in the implementation of random drug testing and all other pertinent provisions of RA 9165.
6. Drug abuse prevention and education programs in schools shall be intensified as part of overall demand reduction efforts.
7. Random drug testing shall be a collaborative undertaking of government, schools, students and their parents, and shall not be used to harass students.
8. Random drug testing shall be implemented primarily for prevention and rehabilitation.
9. The drug-testing program shall guarantee and respect the personal privacy and dignity of the student.
10. Drug test results shall be treated with utmost confidentiality.
11. Test results shall not be used in any criminal proceedings.

### B. Purposes of Random Drug Testing

1. To determine the prevalence of drug users among students
2. To assess the effectivity of school-based and community-based prevention programs
3. To deter the use of illegal drugs
4. To facilitate the rehabilitation of drug users and dependents
5. To strengthen the collaboration efforts of identified agencies against illegal drug use and rehabilitation of users and dependents

### C. Definition of Terms

- **Drug Counselor** — a person trained in guidance counseling techniques dealing with drug dependency cases, designated by the Drug Testing Coordinator.
- **Drug Testing Coordinator** — the point person in the school tasked with handling random drug testing (the principal of a secondary school, the administrator of a TVET institution, or the administrator appointed by the president/CEO in tertiary institutions).
- **Parents** — includes court-appointed guardians.
- **Random selection** — the unbiased process of selecting students to undergo drug testing.
- **Rehabilitation** — the dynamic process, including after-care and follow-up treatment, directed towards physical, emotional/psychological, vocational, social and spiritual change/enhancement of a drug dependent to enable him/her to live without dangerous drugs, become compatible with his/her capabilities and potentials, and become a law-abiding and productive community member.
- **Schools** — institutions whose primary purpose is the education of students, including secondary, tertiary and technical vocational education and training institutions.
- **Selection Board** — the board constituted at the school level, composed of the Drug Testing Coordinator as chairperson, and one representative each from students, faculty and parents. In the absence of a parents' association, the School Head may appoint any parent as a member.
- **Supervising Agency** — the government agency exercising supervision over the school (DepEd, CHED, or TESDA).

### D. Procedures in the Conduct of Random Drug Testing

**1. Notification**
   a. The Supervising Agency shall inform all schools under its supervision about the government's actions against illegal drugs, including these guidelines. School administration shall explain these provisions to the school community and include them in the school's handbook or listing of procedures.
   b. All students and their parents shall be notified in writing of the process and manner by which random drug testing shall be conducted, sent at any time during the school term. Failure to return the acknowledgment receipt shall not bar the conduct of drug testing.

**2. Samples**
   a. The Supervising Agency shall inform randomly selected schools of their inclusion in the program.
   b. The whole student population of the selected school shall be included in random sampling.
   c. The number of samples should yield a statistical 95% confidence level for the whole student population.

**3. Selection of Samples**
   a. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall convene the Selection Board within five days of receipt of notice of inclusion.
   b. On the day of testing, the Selection Board shall conduct the random selection of those to be tested.
   c. The Selection Board shall ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the random selection process.
   d. The selection process shall be random through a lottery, which may be computerized or agreed upon by the Board.
   e. Random selection and the drug testing shall be done on the same day.
   f. Prior to testing, selected students shall be asked to reveal prescription medicines, vitamins, food supplements ingested within the past five (5) days; the Drug Testing Coordinator shall keep this listing for use in evaluating the confirmatory drug test.
   g. The laboratory shall follow DOH-prescribed guidelines in collecting urine specimens, observing universal precautions at all times, posted in strategic/visible areas of the school.
   h. The monitor assigned to ensure integrity of the collection process should be of the same sex as the student.
   i. Drug testing shall be done in the school by a duly accredited drug-testing laboratory, assisted by the school's health personnel.
   j. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall ensure confidentiality and integrity of testing for students, teachers, administration and personnel; it is strongly recommended that testing for all groups be done simultaneously.

**4. Treatment of Random Drug Test Results**

   a. The results of the test shall be strictly confidential. No school shall publish or post results, positive or negative.

   [Sections on counseling, rehabilitation, and consequences of repeat positive results:] Counseling shall be done in coordination with the Drug Counselor of the school. At the end of three months, if the student shows no improvement or fails the drug test a second time, the DOH-accredited facility or physician may recommend that the student be referred to a DOH-accredited facility suited to the student's level of dependency. If found positive a second time on subsequent testing, the school shall proceed in accordance with Section 61, R.A. 9165. The parent and student may choose to enroll in a private rehabilitation center/program or avail of government rehabilitation services through a DOH-accredited facility. If parents refuse to act, the school shall proceed per Section 61 of RA 9165, without prejudice to Section 73, RA 9165.

### E. Reportorial Requirements of Results of the Random Drug Testing

1. The Drug Testing Coordinator, Drug Counselor and employees of DOH-accredited facilities/testing laboratories shall not reveal the names of students or test results to any other persons except the student concerned or his/her parents.
2. Aggregate test results from each school (excluding student identities) shall be submitted by the School Head to the Division Superintendent of DepEd (secondary schools), the Regional Director of CHED (tertiary schools), or the Training Institution Administrator for TESDA, for consolidation and evaluation of the efficacy of drug abuse prevention programs.

### F. Training of Guidance Counselors

DepEd, CHED, TESDA, the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency and the Dangerous Drugs Board, in coordination with each other, shall formulate and conduct training programs for guidance counselors to enhance their skills in handling drug abuse prevention programs and drug dependency cases.

### G. Expenses of the Program

The Department of Health, in coordination with the Supervising Agencies, shall designate the drug testing laboratories to be utilized. Payment of testing fees shall be done by the government through the Department of Health to the Drug Testing Laboratories.

### H. Enforcement of Compliance

Students who refuse to undergo random drug testing shall be dealt with in accordance with the rules and regulations of the schools; provided that at no time shall refusal to undergo testing give rise to a presumption of drug use or dependency, provided further that the school may impose sanctions on such refusal other than the offense of drug use or dependency. Schools that refuse to implement the random drug testing program shall be liable under Section 32 of RA 9165, without prejudice to other administrative sanctions imposed by the Supervising Agencies. The Supervising Agency shall report the same to the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA) and the Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB).

### I. Miscellaneous Provisions

**Separability clause.** If any provision of these Guidelines or their application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the other provisions and applications shall not be affected.

**Effectivity.** These Guidelines shall take effect immediately after approval by the Dangerous Drugs Board.

*ADOPTED and APPROVED this 1st day of August, 2003 at Camp Crame, Quezon City.*

# APPENDIX L — REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11313 ("SAFE SPACES ACT")

*An Act Defining Gender-Based Sexual Harassment in Streets, Public Spaces, Online, Workplaces, and Educational or Training Institutions, Providing Protective Measures and Prescribing Penalties Therefor.*

**Section 1. Short Title.** This Act shall be known as the "Safe Spaces Act."

**Section 2. Declaration of Policies.** It is the policy of the State to value the dignity of every human person and guarantee full respect for human rights, to recognize the role of women in nation-building and ensure fundamental equality before the law of women and men, and to ensure that both men and women have equality, security and safety not only in private but also on the streets, public spaces, online, workplaces and educational and training institutions.

**Section 3. Definition of Terms.**

- **Catcalling** — unwanted remarks directed towards a person, commonly in the form of wolf-whistling and misogynistic, transphobic, homophobic, and sexist slurs.
- **Employee** — a person who, in exchange for remuneration, agrees to perform specified services for another person (natural or juridical, private or public) who exercises fundamental control over the work, regardless of term or duration.
- **Employer** — a person who exercises control over an employee.
- **Gender** — a set of socially ascribed characteristics, norms, roles, attitudes, values and expectations identifying the social behavior of men and women and the relations between them.
- **Gender-based online sexual harassment** — an online conduct targeted at a particular person causing or likely to cause mental, emotional or psychological distress and fear for personal safety, including unwanted sexual remarks/comments, threats, uploading/sharing photos without consent, video/audio recordings, cyberstalking and online identity theft.
- **Gender identity and/or expression** — the personal sense of identity as characterized by manner of clothing, inclinations, and behavior in relation to masculine or feminine conventions.
- **Public spaces** — streets and alleys, public parks, schools, buildings, malls, bars, restaurants, transportation terminals, public markets, evacuation centers, government offices, public utility vehicles, private vehicles covered by app-based transport network services, and other recreational spaces.
- **Stalking** — conduct involving non-consensual visual or physical proximity, repeated communication, or a combination thereof, that causes or will likely cause fear for one's own safety or the safety of others, or emotional distress.

## Article I — Gender-Based Streets and Public Spaces Sexual Harassment

**Section 4.** The crimes of gender-based streets and public spaces sexual harassment are committed through any unwanted and uninvited sexual actions or remarks against any person, regardless of motive, including catcalling, wolf-whistling, unwanted invitations, misogynistic/transphobic/homophobic/sexist slurs, persistent uninvited comments or gestures, relentless requests for personal details, sexual comments and suggestions, public masturbation or flashing, groping, or any advance, verbal or physical, that is unwanted and threatens one's sense of personal space and safety, committed in public spaces such as alleys, roads, sidewalks, buildings, schools, churches, restaurants, malls, public washrooms, bars, internet shops, public markets, parks, transportation terminals or public utility vehicles.

**Section 5.** Restaurants, bars, cinemas, malls, buildings and other privately-owned places open to the public shall adopt a zero-tolerance policy against gender-based sexual harassment, providing assistance to victims by coordinating with local police, making CCTV footage available when ordered by the court, and providing a safe, gender-sensitive environment to encourage reporting. These establishments shall install clearly visible warning signs, including the anti-sexual harassment hotline number, and designate at least one anti-sexual harassment officer. Security guards may be deputized to apprehend perpetrators caught in flagrante delicto.

**Section 6.** The LTO may cancel the license of perpetrators who commit sexual harassment in public utility vehicles, and the LTFRB may suspend or revoke the franchise of transportation operators who commit such acts. Sexual harassment in PUVs by the driver also constitutes a breach of contract of carriage.

**Section 7.** In case the offense is committed by a minor, the DSWD shall take necessary disciplinary measures under RA 9344.

**Section 8. Duties of LGUs** — pass a localizing ordinance within 60 days; disseminate copies of the Act and ordinance; provide prevention measures in educational institutions; discourage and fine acts of harassment; create an anti-sexual harassment hotline; coordinate with the DILG.

**Section 9. Role of the DILG** — inspect LGU compliance; disseminate best practices; provide capacity-building and training in coordination with PCW, LGA and DAP.

**Section 10. Implementing Bodies** — the MMDA, local PNP units, and the WCPD of the PNP shall apprehend perpetrators and enforce the law (after Gender Sensitivity Training). PCW, DILG and DICT are the national bodies overseeing implementation. The MMDA and local PNP shall deputize Anti-Sexual Harassment Enforcers (ASHE) to receive street complaints and apprehend perpetrators caught in flagrante delicto, keeping a ledger of repeat offenders.

**Section 11. Specific Acts and Penalties** —
(a) Cursing, wolf-whistling, catcalling, leering, intrusive gazing, slurs, persistent taunting/pursuing, unwanted invitations, unwanted comments, relentless requests for personal details, ridicule based on sex/gender/orientation, sexual jokes/names/comments/demands, invasion of personal space: 1st offense — fine of P1,000 and 12 hours community service plus Gender Sensitivity Seminar; 2nd offense — arresto menor (6-10 days) or fine of P3,000; 3rd offense — arresto menor (11-30 days) and fine of P10,000.
(b) Offensive body gestures, exposing private parts, flashing, public masturbation, groping and similar lewd actions: 1st offense — fine of P10,000 and 12 hours community service plus Gender Sensitivity Seminar; 2nd offense — arresto menor (11-30 days) or fine of P15,000; 3rd offense — arresto mayor (1 month and 1 day to 6 months) and fine of P20,000.
(c) Stalking, and acts under (a)/(b) accompanied by touching, pinching, or brushing against the body, genitalia, face, arms, anus, groin, breasts, inner thighs, buttocks or any part of the victim's body: 1st offense — arresto menor (11-30 days) or fine of P30,000 plus Gender Sensitivity Seminar; 2nd offense — arresto mayor (1 month and 1 day to 6 months) or fine of P50,000; 3rd offense — arresto mayor maximum or fine of P100,000.

## Article II — Gender-Based Online Sexual Harassment

**Section 12.** Gender-based online sexual harassment includes acts using information and communications technology to terrorize and intimidate victims through physical, psychological, and emotional threats; unwanted sexual remarks and comments online (publicly or via private messages); invasion of privacy through cyberstalking and incessant messaging; unauthorized uploading/sharing of photos, voice, or video with sexual content; impersonating victims online or posting lies to harm their reputation; or filing false abuse reports to silence victims.

**Section 13.** The PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group (PNPACG) is primarily responsible for implementing pertinent laws on cybercrime, receiving complaints and developing an online reporting mechanism. The Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) of the DICT shall coordinate with PNPACG to monitor and penalize such harassment.

**Section 14. Penalties** — prision correccional in its medium period or a fine of not less than P100,000 but not more than P500,000, or both, at the court's discretion. If the perpetrator is a juridical person, its license/franchise shall be automatically revoked, with liability falling on its officers. An alien who commits this offense shall be subject to deportation after serving sentence.

## Article III — Qualified Gender-Based Streets, Public Spaces and Online Sexual Harassment

**Section 15.** The penalty next higher in degree applies if: (a) the act takes place in a common carrier or PUV where the perpetrator is the driver and the offended party a passenger; (b) the offended party is a minor, senior citizen, PWD, or breastfeeding mother; (c) the offended party has a diagnosed mental problem impairing consent; (d) the perpetrator is a uniformed service member (PNP/AFP) acting in uniform; (e) the act occurs on government agency premises and the perpetrator is a government employee.

## Article IV — Gender-Based Sexual Harassment in the Workplace

**Section 16.** Includes: (a) unwelcome sexual advances, requests or demands for sexual favors or acts of sexual nature (verbal, physical, or via technology) with a detrimental effect on employment/education conditions or opportunities; (b) conduct of sexual nature affecting the dignity of a person that is unwelcome, unreasonable, and offensive; (c) conduct that is unwelcome, pervasive and creates an intimidating, hostile or humiliating environment — may be committed between peers, by a subordinate to a superior, or by a student to a teacher/trainee to a trainer; (d) "information and communication system" is also defined.

**Section 17. Duties of Employers** — disseminate the Act; provide prevention measures; create an independent internal Committee on Decorum and Investigation (adequately representative, headed by a woman, impartial, deciding within 10 days, observing due process, protecting from retaliation, guaranteeing confidentiality); provide and disseminate a code of conduct/workplace policy.

**Section 18. Duties of Employees and Co-Workers** — refrain from committing such acts; discourage such conduct; support victims; report witnessed acts.

**Section 19. Liability of Employers** — for non-implementation of duties (fine of P5,000-P10,000) or failure to act on reported acts (fine of P10,000-P15,000).

**Section 20. Routine Inspection** — DOLE (private sector) and CSC (public sector) shall conduct yearly spontaneous inspections.

## Article V — Gender-Based Sexual Harassment in Educational and Training Institutions

**Section 21.** All schools shall designate an officer-in-charge to receive complaints and ensure a gender-sensitive, respectful environment. Schools must adopt and publish grievance procedures. Even without a formal complaint, if school authorities know or reasonably should know of a possible or impending act, the school must promptly investigate, take immediate action to eliminate the acts, prevent recurrence, and address effects. Once a perpetrator is found guilty, the institution may strip a diploma or issue an expulsion order. The Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) of all educational institutions shall address gender-based and online sexual harassment per their CODI manual.

**Section 22. Duties of School Heads** — disseminate the Act; provide prevention measures; create an independent CODI (adequately representative, headed by a woman, ensuring representation of diverse SOGI, impartial, deciding within 10 days, observing due process, protecting from retaliation, guaranteeing confidentiality); provide and disseminate a code of conduct/school policy.

**Section 23. Liability of School Heads** — for non-implementation of duties (fine of P5,000-P10,000) or failure to act (fine of P10,000-P15,000).

**Section 24. Liability of Students** — Minor students found to have committed gender-based sexual harassment shall only be held liable for administrative sanctions by the school as stated in the school handbook.

**Section 25. Routine Inspection** — DepEd, CHED, and TESDA shall conduct regular spontaneous inspections.

## Article VI — Common Provisions

**Section 26. Confidentiality** — the rights of the victim and a minor accused shall be recognized at all stages.

**Section 27. Restraining Order** — the court may issue an order directing the perpetrator to stay away from the offended person or specified places.

**Section 28. Remedies and Psychological Counselling** — victims may avail of psychological counselling with the aid of the LGU and DSWD, in coordination with DOH and PCW; fees shall be borne by the perpetrator.

**Section 29. Administrative Sanctions** — without prejudice to administrative sanctions if the perpetrator is a government employee.

**Section 30. Imposition of Heavier Penalties** — LGUs may impose heavier penalties via ordinance.

**Section 31. Exemptions** — legitimate expressions of indigenous culture/tradition and breastfeeding in public shall not be penalized.

## Article VII — Final Provisions

**Section 32. PNP Women and Children's Desks** shall act on complaints, coordinating with ASHE officers, security guards, and anti-sexual harassment officers.

**Section 33. Educational Modules and Awareness Campaigns** — PCW leads a national awareness campaign with DILG, DICT and women's groups; schools shall educate students from elementary to tertiary level about the law.

**Section 34. Safety Audits** — LGUs shall conduct multisectoral, participatory safety audits.

(AC5573 Tumbaga v. Teoxon, 11/21/2017)

**N.B.** One that reflects the child's malicious and uncontrollable nature that endangers him/her or everyone around him/her, or crimes where the penalty is 6 years and above.

### DISCIPLINARY MEASURES (Blended Learning Context)

Disciplinary actions are corrective and formative measures given to students who fail to comply with school policies and rules. The primary objective is to guide and accompany erring students to the right behavior, taking into account proper student decorum. Disciplinary actions range from verbal warning to dismissal/exclusion depending on the gravity of the offense.

The teacher-in-charge directly monitors students' conduct. All offenses shall be immediately reported to the Discipline Officers for verification and processing. Prompt reporting is encouraged, covering incidents from the first day of classes until the final report card distribution of the following year.

**Conference with Subject Teachers** — done if a student commits an academic-related offense such as non-submission of mini-task, making unnecessary remarks during online class, etc. After a series of conferences and a third offense, the case may be referred to the Office for Student Discipline.

**Due Process** — The school recognizes students' right to due process through conference and dialogue before implementing disciplinary action. The Discipline Officer is authorized to call students involved. Considering high school students are of minor age, parents or legal guardians are invited to be informed of the case and to answer charges, if desired.

In some major offenses, the discipline committee may be convened by the Prefect of Discipline to assess the case, composed of the DSS, Principal, Discipline Officer, and Guidance Counselor. After a decision is reached, a final conference facilitated by the Prefect of Discipline is held with the student and parents. A student may also be referred to the Guidance Office if counseling is deemed more appropriate than disciplinary action.

### PARENT(S)/GUARDIAN(S) AS COLLABORATORS IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF RC DIGIPLINE

Parent(s)/guardian(s) of RC students are key collaborators in ensuring continuity of learning and discipline at home. They are encouraged to:

1. Promote and implement proper netiquette to be observed under their supervision.
2. Conscientiously guide their children/wards in online activities, especially during online classes.
3. Monitor them during online classes to help prevent dishonesty in accomplishing schoolwork/requirements, promoting academic integrity as part of the core values of EXCELLENCE AND INTEGRITY.
4. Encourage children/wards to practice time management in day-to-day activities.
5. Let children know by heart what digital literacy and digital citizenship mean: "Digital literacy" is the ability to use ICT to find, evaluate, create and communicate information, requiring both cognitive and technical skills; "Digital citizenship" refers to the norms of appropriate, responsible behavior with regard to technology use. (p.3 of DOJ Office of Cybercrime's Public Advisory, September 22, 2020)
6. Instill in their children/wards the exercise of due diligence in online dealings and activities.
7. Teach them how to discern information online and provide guidance on implementing safety measures to prevent becoming a victim of cybercrimes.
8. Not take any drastic action or in any way harass the other party in case of misunderstanding between or among students.
9. Not join or engage in the students' group chat; instead, address any apprehension to the concerned teacher or discipline officer, giving students the opportunity to settle misunderstandings, miscommunication and misinterpretation among themselves educatively and maturely. RESPECT and COURTESY should be observed and set a good example for students and children.
