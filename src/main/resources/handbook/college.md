## THE FOUNDER — SAINT HANNIBAL MARY DI FRANCIA

St. Hannibal was born to a knight and a noblewoman in Messina, Italy on July 5, 1851. He was orphaned at a tender age. As a young man, he had been given the intuition of the necessity of Prayer for Vocations. When he became a priest (March 16, 1878), he committed himself to the moral and spiritual redemption of the poorest area in his city, giving up all his inherited wealth to the service of God through his manifold works of charity among the poor children. He is known as the "Apostle of Prayer for Vocations" because he focused all his works on the promotion of the ROGATE (Latin for "pray"), following the evangelical call of our Lord as told by two evangelists: Matthew (9:37-38) and Luke (10:2) — "THE HARVEST IS GREAT, BUT THE WORKERS ARE FEW, PRAY THEREFORE THE LORD OF THE HARVEST TO SEND GOOD WORKERS INTO HIS HARVEST." Pondering upon the meaning and message of the sacred words, he saw in them the ineffable solution for the salvation of the many poor children whom he learned to love with tender and divine affection.

Fr. Hannibal founded two religious congregations, the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and the Daughters of Divine Zeal, to help him carry out and propagate the prayer for vocations.

Fr. Hannibal passed to eternity on June 1, 1927, but his spirit remains alive in the hearts and minds of all his spiritual sons and daughters. He was beatified on October 7, 1990 during the Synod of Bishops and was canonized on May 16, 2004 in Rome, Italy by His Holiness, Pope John Paul II.

## FOREWORD

A wholesome educational formation is possible only through cooperative efforts extended by both the home and the school. Consistency of purpose and manner in realizing this formation is best achieved through policies and regulations.

This handbook is intended for the purpose of this realization. It aims to summarize in an orderly and readable manner all the salient information and the policies and regulations of Rogationist College. Through this, it is expected that:

1. Students will acquaint themselves with the objectives, policies, procedures and regulations of Rogationist College;
2. Administrators and teachers will acquire working knowledge in guiding the education community to realize the vision-mission and goals of the school as they promote the observance of its policies and regulations; and
3. Parents will understand their role in promoting and realizing the educational and institutional objectives of the school.

The policies, rules and regulations in this handbook are intended to promote an atmosphere that is conducive to the students' personal growth founded on Christ and His Gospel to pursue God's call, in obedience to the Rogate, and in accordance with the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

# Part I — HISTORY

Like any family or community, Rogationist College (RC) has a rich historical heritage which every RC student should be proud of. To appreciate more deeply the roots of an RC community, an overview of the history of the congregation of the Rogationists, and more particularly that of RC, is presented here.

## THE ROGATIONIST CONGREGATION

The Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus (RCJ) is a religious congregation of priests and brothers founded by St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia in 1887 in Messina, Italy. Both the Rogationists of the Heart of Jesus and its sister congregation, the Daughters of Divine Zeal, are devoted to prayer and promotion of vocations in the Church, and to the education of young people, particularly the poor and disadvantaged. The legacy of St. Hannibal is kept alive by these congregations present today in Europe (Italy, Spain, Albania and Poland); in South America (Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay); in North America (USA and Mexico); in Africa (Rwanda and Cameroun); in Oceania (Papua New Guinea); and in Asia (the Philippines, India, Korea, Vietnam, and Indonesia).

## THE ROGATIONIST PHILIPPINE DELEGATION

The Rogationists established their first missionary activities in the Philippines in November 1976 to promote a culture of vocation and to assist the poor especially the children. Their long term strategy was to prepare young Filipinos to be agents of social growth and transformation driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

Milestones in the development of the Rogationist Philippine Delegation:

- **October 1977** — opening of the first Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park (Parañaque);
- **November 1979** — construction of a parish church in Multinational Village (Parañaque);
- **June 1980** — profession of the religious vows of the first three Filipino Rogationist novices;
- **December 1981** — opening of a second and larger Rogationist Formation Center in Merville Park;
- **June 1987** — opening of St. Anthony's Boys Village and Rogationist Academy (High School) in Silang, Cavite;
- **April 1990** — opening of the Rogationist Formation Center for the Visayan Region in Cebu City;
- **January 1999** — start of the new mission in Toril, Davao City with Fr. Hannibal Children's Home, a charitable institution for children;
- **December 1999** — inauguration of the Fr. Di Francia Center of Studies for Religious Formation in Theology in Parañaque City;
- **January 2002** — opening of the Oasis of Prayer and Retreat House in Silang, Cavite;

## THE OFFICIAL RC LOGO

**DEUS ET PROXIMUS**

The Official RC logo represents man's total human and Christian formation.

The three Latin terms *Fides*, *Sapientia* and *Virtus* stand for Faith, Wisdom and Virtue, which are indicative of the three pillars (Religion, Reason, and Love) upon which the Rogationist College System of Education rests.

The cross represents Christ, the living Foundation of the whole Rogationist educational community.

The four vertical columns sustaining the cross signify the four core values (Love of God and neighbor, Patriotism, Integrity and Excellence) anchored on the Rogationist educational philosophy.

The curved lines leaning on one of the pillars of the cross symbolize the secondary education. The gear of the industrial wheel stands for the formation offered by the College and Technical Education Departments.

The open book represents the Word of God — The HOLY BIBLE — the source of inspiration, knowledge and strength of all those who are part of the Institution.

The Latin words *Deus et Proximus* (God and neighbor) stand for the direction of Rogationist College education.

# Part II — LIFE PURPOSE, GUIDING PHILOSOPHY, VISION AND MISSION

## THE LIFE PURPOSE

Rogationist College exists primarily to enable the youth, especially the poor and underprivileged assisted by the St. Anthony's Boys Village Foundation, to discover in their life the project of God by becoming good Christians and responsible members of society through Secondary, Technical and Tertiary education anchored on the formative philosophy of the Rogationists.

## GUIDING PHILOSOPHY

Rogationist College (RC) in St. Anthony's Boys Village in Silang, Cavite is a Catholic co-educational community committed to the formation of the whole human person.

RC, in its mission to help the youth, bases its educative system on a philosophy of education which is attentive to the instances of human life. It is both personalistic and vocational in nature. RC forms its students to the proper understanding and dynamics of self. The process brings them to a serene enjoyment of life according to the dictum of responsible acting and sharing.

The vocational nature of the RC system facilitates its students in the acquisition of a realistic life perspective. It is a view which is sensitive to life's origin and destiny. RC believes and professes that every person is called not only to live but also to fulfill life as God's gift.

RC is convinced that real education is not just academic education. It espouses a style of forming that opens avenues to the proper exercise of love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence.

The educative formation of RC is always considered an encounter between the teachers and their students. In doing the personalistic and vocational approach to education, RC individuates three interrelated moments of the meeting, which ends in the mature choices for the life orientation of its students.

The first moment is **ORGANIZATION**. Here, the organizing student is met by a teacher who is a guide. In this moment, the students are initiated to find and form the most important instances of their life and then cherish them.

The second moment is **IDENTIFICATION**. Here teachers are models. St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia, founder of the Rogationists, wants all institutions of the Rogationists to educate through good examples. RC believes that for students to grow in the right direction, the edifying life of teachers is very important.

The third moment is **DECISION**. Self-fulfillment is also self-determination. At the end of the formative process, students of RC should have already acquired a certain degree of independence. Knowing better to distinguish right from wrong, they should be capable of pursuing not just terrestrial but also personal and possibly celestial good.

RC's philosophy of education, following the teachings of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia and inspired by the Gospel of the Christ of the Rogate, goes therefore to the roots of the human person.

## VISION

Rogationist College is a prime Catholic educational community dedicated to the formation of culturally competent and socially responsible persons driven by the ideals of St. Hannibal Mary Di Francia.

## MISSION

Rogationist College commits itself to:

1. Form its members toward Love of God and neighbor, patriotism, integrity, and excellence;
2. Offer a balanced and technically-oriented curriculum, as well as excellent instruction, learning environment and facilities;
3. Prepare well-rounded graduates who are agents of social growth and transformation; and
4. Promote a culture of vocation and good workers who, like St. Hannibal, will be espousing the cause of the poor especially the children.

# Part III — GENERAL POLICIES AND GUIDELINES

> "The home shall fully support the school in the implementation of the total school program — curricular and co-curricular — toward the proper physical, social, intellectual and moral development of the child."
> — Article 76, The Child and Youth Welfare Code (Presidential Decree No. 603, s. 1974)

## GENERAL POLICIES

1. The rules and regulations of RC as stated in this Handbook are intended to enhance the life in school of an RC student as he relates and interacts with the members of the school community. As such, it is understood that they are mutual good role models of each other.
2. It is the duty and responsibility of the parents and/or guardians to keep track of the progress of their children/wards in school. The parents and/or guardians are encouraged to confer with the school authorities at their own volition or whenever calls for conference are issued to them. This is to enhance the harmonious working relationship between the school and the home for the welfare of the student.
3. The enrollment of a student in RC is taken as an expressed willingness on his/her part, and consequently of his/her parents and/or guardians, to abide by all rules and regulations of the school. Thus, the student and his/her parents and/or guardians are committed to the strict observance of the same.
4. RC reserves the right to admit, readmit, refuse admission or exclude a student or drop the name of an erring student from the school rolls on the basis of his/her academic performance, conduct or attendance.
5. RC acknowledges its obligations to exercise reasonable supervision over the conduct of the student and to cultivate the best potentialities of the heart and mind of the student. Its role in "loco parentis" is the exercise of substitute parental authority over students in the school and in school-related activities (Civil Code, Art. 349).
6. RC, as a Catholic school, strongly respects and adheres to the practice of the holy sacrament (matrimony) and does not tolerate sexual indiscretions. Violation of this policy would merit the student appropriate disciplinary action, including but not limited to, requiring the student to go on leave or will be excluded or dropped from the school rolls.
7. RC expects its students to uphold the standards of responsibility and dignity. When a student of RC does not measure up to these standards, he/she will be excluded or dropped from the school rolls after due process.
8. Students, either as individuals or as a group, must not use the name and seal of the school without official authorization.
9. Students who are using social media in their respective homes are not to make inappropriate comments (including in private messages) about the school personnel or other students. Concerns and/or complaints must be written and directly addressed to the proper school authority rather than posting them on social media — whether on their own pages, in closed groups (e.g. groups set up for school parents to communicate with each other) or on the school's pages. Complaints posted on social media will not be entertained by the school.
10. Parents or guardians may consult or confer with teachers or instructors provided they secure appointments with the Department Head (Principal, Dean or TED Head) two days before the actual date of appointment.
11. Parents, guardians or visitors are not allowed to go to the classrooms. Messages and allowance for the students must be coursed through the Guidance Office.
12. Students may be called out of the classrooms provided an approved written permission/call slip is issued by either of the following:
    12.1. Rector/Vice-Rector
    12.2. Director for Academic Affairs (DAA)
    12.3. Director for Student Services (DSS)
    12.4. Director for Spiritual Affairs (DSA)
    12.5. Principal/Dean/Technical Education Department Head (TED Head)
    12.6. Vice Principal/Vice Dean
    12.7. Guidance Counselor/Facilitator
    12.8. Prefect of Discipline/Discipline Officer
    12.9. Health Services Coordinator/Nurse

As a Catholic School, with Rogationist Formation as the core of the curriculum, RC requires all students to attend and pass all the requirements of Christian Living Education or Religious Education subjects. Students also need to attend masses and/or similar activities and complete spiritual recollections and retreats scheduled by the institution. In addition, parents of High School students are required to follow the agreement (to attend/participate in parents' formation programs, PTA assemblies, etc.) signed during the enrolment of their children.

For parents or other visitors, a visitor's ID is issued by the guard in exchange for a valid identification card, which is returned after a legitimate business transaction or visit inside the campus. ## SUSPENSION OF CLASSES

Classes are suspended in the following situations:

1. Typhoon warning signal is raised by PAGASA in the area of Cavite. Signal no. 2 automatically suspends classes in the secondary level while signal no. 3 automatically suspends classes in the tertiary level. Depending on signal numbers declared at 10:00 pm and 4:30 am of the following day, classes in appropriate levels for the whole day are deemed automatically cancelled/suspended. (DepEd Order No. 43 series 2012).
2. Natural calamities or disturbances compromising the safety of the students;
3. Rector's announcement;
4. Cancellation of classes by DepEd, TESDA or CHED;
5. National, Provincial, and Municipal proclamations.

**N.B.** Afternoon classes in the appropriate levels in areas with signal numbers declared at 11:00 am that day will be cancelled or suspended at 2:00 pm to ensure the safety of the students and to provide ample time for the school authorities to contact school service providers and/or the parents. In addition to this, during heavy rains affecting the health or safety of their children, parents have the discretion not to send their children to school.

5. Tuition fees and other charges for transferring or withdrawing students shall be governed by the following provisions:

   When a student registers in a school, it is understood that he/she is enrolling for the entire school year for secondary courses. (DepEd Order No. 88 series 2010: Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, Section 119, Rules for Registration.) Thus, a student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 20% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.

   For College students: Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education, Section 100, Tuition Charges — A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged 25% of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the 1st week of classes, or 50% if within the 2nd week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the 2nd week of classes.

## ACADEMIC PROGRAMS AND POLICIES

### I. Academic Year

The academic year for semester-based subjects is divided into two (2) terms of 18 weeks each, inclusive of examination periods and class days lost due to natural or man-made calamities. A summer session of six (6) weeks follows the second semester.

For non-semester-based programs, the academic year is based on the number of modules or blocks offered per program.

The school calendar shall serve as the guide for the schedule of all academic and non-academic activities, unless a change is made by the school officials.

### II. Study Load

Students' academic loads are prescribed for all regular and irregular students.

1. **Term Load**
   1.1. For regular students, the term load is as prescribed in the curriculum.
   1.2. For irregular students, the term load must not exceed the corresponding number of units prescribed for the year level based on the curriculum.
2. **Summer Load**

   The summer load should not exceed three (3) subjects.
3. **Underload**
   3.1. Underload is any load below the number of units prescribed in curriculum.
   3.2. Students may be allowed to have underload on the following conditions:
   3.2.1. Employed either in or out of school
   3.2.2. Probationary or warning status
   3.2.3. Health reasons
   3.2.4. Unavailability of subjects needed in the curriculum to complete the full load
4. **Overload**

   Overload is any load beyond the number of units prescribed in the curriculum. A student may be allowed to have overload on the following conditions:
   4.1. Graduating student in his/her last term of attendance
   4.2. Irregular Student/Transferee Student that needs to cope-up with missed subjects from his/her existing program.

   A maximum overload of 6 units may be allowed, subject to the endorsement of the Program Chair and the approval of the Dean/TED Head.

### III. Change of Matriculation

Each type of change requires a different set of documentation and authorization.

1. **Change of Subject and Load**

   Students are allowed to change subjects or schedules within two (2) weeks after the start of classes. Subjects cancelled during this period will not appear in the student's transcript of records. Steps necessary to effect the change are as follows:
   1.1. Accomplishment of the Change of Matriculation Form
   1.2. Program Chair/TED Head's endorsement
   1.3. Re-assessment of fees and approval from the Registrar's Office
   1.4. Payment to the Cashier's Office
   1.5. Submission of completed form to the Office of the Registrar
   1.6. Official "stamping" of the student's copy by the Registrar's Office
2. **Dropping of Subjects**

   Students are allowed to drop a subject without being given a failing grade in the subject before the midterm examination. The transcript will contain a grade of Dropped or "DRP" for the subject, earning for the students zero credits. Steps for dropping of subject/s are as follows:
   2.1. Accomplishment of the Student's Dropping Form
   2.2. Instructor's approval of the subject to be dropped
   2.3. Endorsement of the Program Chair
   2.4. Approval of the Dean/TED Head
   2.5. Notation of the Registrar
   2.6. Payment of processing fee at the Cashier's Office
   2.7. Submission of completed form to the Office of the Registrar
   2.8. Official "stamping" of the student's Dropping Form.
3. **Shifting of Academic Programs**

   Students are allowed to shift their academic program as long as they satisfy the admission requirements of the particular program. Program "shifted" takes effect on the semester indicated in the form. Steps necessary to effect the change are as follows:
   3.1. Accomplishment of the application for program change
   3.2. Consent from the parent or guardian
   3.3. Program Chair's endorsement
   3.4. Validation and approval from the Dean's/TED's Office
   3.5. Official "stamping" of the student's copy by the Registrar's Office
4. **Refund of Fees**

   A refund of fees shall be made only in accordance with the following rules:
   4.1. A duly enrolled student who wishes to drop one or more subjects and obtain a refund, must give an accomplished Dropping Form to the DFA, copy furnished the Dean/TED Head and the Registrar. This must be done within three weeks from the first day of classes during the regular semester and within the first week of classes during summer term. Beyond this period, no refund will be allowed.
   4.2. If the student files the required dropping notice within the first week after the opening of classes during the regular semester and within the first three days of the summer term, the student shall be entitled to a refund of 90% of the tuition fee paid for the subject/s dropped.
   4.3. If the student files the required dropping notice within the second and third week after the opening of classes during the regular semester or after the third day up to the sixth day of summer classes, the student shall be entitled to a refund of 80% of the tuition fee paid for the subject/s dropped.
5. **Refund without deduction**

   Full refund or credit adjustments shall be made when the class is dissolved or discontinued.
6. **Official Dropping Without Refund**

   Official dropping forms shall be accomplished and filed with the Registrar any time before the start of the midterm examinations, provided the student has not incurred more than the allowable absences.
7. **Consequences of Load Withdrawal/Dropping without Notice**

   A student who drops one or more subjects without filing the required dropping form shall receive a grade of 5.0 (failed) and shall be charged the total assessment or the corresponding assessment for the subject/s dropped.

### IV. Attendance

1. **General Guidelines**
   1.1. All students are required to attend classes regularly and punctually. If and when the student is tardy or absent, he/she is expected to assume full responsibility for the subject matter taught, discussed and assigned.
   1.2. A student who ceases attending classes until the end of the term such that he/she exceeds the maximum allowable unexcused absences will be given a grade of 5.0 with "FAILED" remarks.
2. **Unexcused Absences**
   2.1. The maximum allowable unexcused absence of a student per subject per term should not be more than 20% of the total class hours, unless otherwise authorized by the Dean/TED Head for meritorious reasons.
   2.2. The maximum allowable unexcused absence depends on the total number of units/class hours required per class. Refer to the table below on the maximum unexcused absence a student may incur for each of the subjects with certain number of units/class hours:

   | Units | Total Lecture Hrs. per Semester | 20% Allowable Absences (hrs.) | Total Laboratory Hrs. per Semester | 20% Allowable Absences (hrs.) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 6 units | 108 | 22 | 324 | 65 |
   | 5 units | 90 | 18 | 270 | 54 |
   | 4 units | 72 | 15 | 216 | 43 |
   | 3 units | 54 | 11 | 162 | 33 |
   | 2 units | 36 | 7 | 108 | 22 |
   | 1 unit | 18 | 4 | 54 | 11 |

   To illustrate: A student is not allowed to exceed 7 class hours of unexcused absences in a 2-unit lecture subject or 22 laboratory hours in a 2-unit laboratory subject. For a 4-unit class with 3 units of lecture and 1 unit of laboratory, a student must not incur unexcused absences of more than 11 lecture hours nor more than 11 laboratory hours.
   2.3. Three (3) instances of tardiness are equivalent to one (1) absence.
   2.4. Time lost due to late enrolment is considered time lost by absence unless these steps are accomplished:
   2.4.1. The student applies for his/her absence to be excused
   2.4.2. The faculty considers the absence excused
3. **Excused Absences**
   3.1. Excused absences are limited to the following cases:
   3.1.1. Indisposed due to sickness, certified by an attending physician
   3.1.2. Death of next of kin (grandparent, parent, brother, sister, spouse, or guardian) subject to verification by the Discipline Officer.
   3.1.3. Other special cases deemed meritorious by the Dean/TED Head and approved by the DAA
   3.2. Claims for excused absences must be filed within the term.
4. **Waiting Period**
   4.1. An instructor is considered absent only after the fifteen-minute grace period has elapsed.
   4.2. A student who leaves the classroom before the waiting period has elapsed will be considered absent if the instructor arrives within the stipulated waiting period.

### V. Leave of Absence (LOA)

A student is expected to enroll each semester until he/she completes the course or is dropped from the rolls. When a student cannot enroll in the succeeding semester, he/she must file in writing for an official leave of absence with the Registrar's Office before the end of the currently enrolled term wherein the leave starts.

The maximum leave period that may be applied for in a single application is one (1) academic year. However, the total duration of all leaves taken must not exceed the length of the program.

1. **Conditions for LOA**

   Leaves are officially granted for the following reasons:
   1.1.1. Death of an immediate member of the family (spouse, children, parents, brothers, sisters or guardians) as certified by a copy of the death certificate
   1.1.2. Attendance in approved seminars sponsored or recognized by CHED or TESDA
   1.1.3. Poor health as certified by the school physician or a duly registered physician.
   1.1.4. Any other reason deemed acceptable by the Dean/TED Head.
   1.2. Students applying for LOA must accomplish the Leave of Absence Form from the Registrar's Office. Necessary supporting documents (e.g. medical certificate) must be attached to this form.
   1.3. **Extension of Leave**

   Any extension of an official leave is considered as another leave application. As such, the leave extension must be formally applied for in writing. For the purpose of computing the maximum residency status, the duration of official LOA is not counted as residency.
   1.4. **Return to School**

   A student shall be classified as a Returnee upon his/her return from an approved leave and he/she shall enroll with this status.
2. **AWOL Status**

   A student who takes an unofficial or disapproved leave is placed on AWOL (Absence without Official Leave) status. Any AWOL period is included in the reckoning of a student's maximum residency.

   The school reserves the right to refuse automatic admission of a student upon his/her return.

   A student who is refused automatic admission is required to officially apply for readmission.

   The following are the requirements for readmission:
   a. Letter from the student (noted by the parent/guardian)
   b. Interview by the Program Chair
   c. Approval of the Dean/TED Head

### VI. Residency

There are prescribed minimum and maximum periods of residency in order to acquire a degree at Rogationist College.

1. **Minimum Residency**
   1.1. A student may graduate from any CHED academic program if he/she has completed at Rogationist College the major and core subjects of the last two (2) years of the program prior to graduation.
   1.2. For TESDA-governed courses, the student must have completed at Rogationist College at least a year of the academic units excluding PE and NSTP.
2. **Maximum Residency**
   2.1. A student must finish the requirements of a course within a period of actual residence equivalent to the length of the course plus an additional half (50%) of the total number of years of the course.
   2.1.1. one-year course - not to exceed 1 & 1/2 years
   2.1.2. two-year course - not to exceed 3 years
   2.1.3. four-year course - not to exceed 6 years
   2.2. In meritorious cases, as in the case of graduating students, the Dean/TED Head may extend the allowable period of maximum residency.

### VII. On-the-Job Training (OJT) Program

A student who has completed all units prerequisite to the program will be allowed to enroll in the OJT. The Program Chair shall recommend the enrollment of the student for approval of the Registrar and the Dean.

1. **Purposes of the OJT:**
   1.1. provide the OJT student the opportunity to gain practical/hands-on experience in the field;
   1.2. enhance the personality, confidence, human relations/communication and critical thinking skills of the OJT student;
   1.3. inculcate in the OJT student the attitude of a responsible professional; and,
   1.4. provide opportunities for the OJT student to integrate and synthesize the various theories of his/her course.
2. **General Guidelines**
   2.1. **Duration and Time:** The student must complete the required minimum number of OJT hours as stated in the student's curriculum.
   2.2. **Leave of Absence or Deferment of OJT:** As a general rule, no deferment shall be allowed once a student has been deployed for OJT. However, for justifiable reasons and after due consultation with the Program Chair and the Partner Institution, the External Relations Officer may allow a student a maximum period of six (6) months deferment. The student should file the request for such deferment in written form. The student trainees are not allowed to transfer or move to another company without notifying the OJT Supervisor, External Relations Officer, and OJT Adviser. Violation of this provision will mean non-credit of the enrolled practicum subject.
   2.3. **Uniform:** It is the prerogative of the Rogationist College to prescribe the necessary uniform for the OJT students provided that it is not identical with the uniform of the personnel of the Partner Institution. Such uniform shall bear a name patch on the upper left side bearing clearly the student's surname and initials of the first and middle names together with the name of the related course.
   2.4. **Safety and Protection:** Rogationist College (according to the provisions of the insurance coverage and other appropriate safety nets) and the Partner Institution shall ensure the safety and protection to life of the OJT students at all times during the OJT.
   2.5. **OJT tuition fee:** Rogationist College collects tuition fee from the students who shall undertake the OJT, in conformity with the existing CHED/TESDA rules and regulations.
   2.6. **Venue:** The venue for the OJT shall be near or within the vicinity of the School. However, other venues may be recommended by the OJT Coordinator with the advice of the Program Chair and approval of the Dean/TED Head.
3. **School's Responsibility:** As a general rule, Rogationist College is responsible for:
   3.1. making OJT arrangements with the Partner Institution through the External Relations Officer;
   3.2. providing and ensuring the safety and protection of the OJT students;
   3.3. setting and prescribing of institutional requirements and necessary forms to be accomplished by the OJT students; and
   3.4. conducting periodic visits and dialogue with OJT students and regularly coordinating with the Partner Institution regarding the implementation of the OJT.
4. **Grading System**

   The OJT grade is derived from 60% of the grades earned from the periodic evaluation of the student's on-the-job training by the OJT Supervisor and 40% of the grade given by the OJT Adviser.

   Grade = 0.60 (Grade from OJT Supervisor) + 0.40 (Grade from OJT Adviser)

   The evaluation shall adopt the following grading system:

   | Grade | Equivalent |
   |---|---|
   | 98-100 | 1.00 |
   | 95-97 | 1.25 |
   | 92-94 | 1.50 |
   | 89-91 | 1.75 |
   | 86-88 | 2.00 |
   | 83-85 | 2.25 |
   | 80-82 | 2.50 |
   | 77-79 | 2.75 |
   | 75-76 | 3.00 |
   | below 75 | 5.00 |

   A failing grade (5) shall mean the repetition of the OJT in another host company. The following conditions shall result to a failing grade in OJT:
   4.1. Failure to submit requirements for OJT placement within the term as scheduled by the External Relations Officer.
   4.2. Failure to report on time on three separate occasions for interview or examination without justifiable reasons.
   4.3. Failure to submit time sheets and evaluations for two consecutive evaluation periods.
   4.4. Failing grades in two (2) consecutive evaluations from the partner institution.
   4.5. Dropping of the student by the partner institution due to any of the following: dishonest/illegal acts, misconduct, tardiness/absences. These offenses will also constitute sanctions for major offense as provided in this Handbook.

### IX. Grading System and Earned Credits

Grades are determined by averaging a student's performance over the term for both lecture and laboratory. A failing grade may be given to a student who does not meet attendance requirements.

1. **Grading System**

   | Description | Grade | Equivalent |
   |---|---|---|
   | Excellent | 98-100% | 1.00 |
   | | 95-97% | 1.25 |
   | Very Good | 92-94% | 1.50 |
   | Very Good | 89-91% | 1.75 |
   | Good | 86-88% | 2.00 |
   | Good | 83-85% | 2.25 |
   | | 80-82% | 2.5 |
   | | 77-79% | 2.75 |
   | Fair | 75-76% | 3.00 |
   | Fair | Below 75% | 5.00 (Failed) |
   | DRP | | Officially Dropped |
   | INC | | Incomplete |
   | Satisfactory | | Satisfactory |
2. **Subject Grade**

   Grade = Prelim (0.3) + Midterm (0.3) + Final (0.3) + Final Output (0.1)

   In subjects with a combination of lecture and laboratory, the subject grade is based on the proportion of lecture to laboratory units. Hence, the subject grade for a three (3) unit subject with two (2) units lecture and one (1) unit of laboratory shall be a ratio of 2:1, lecture: laboratory, and rounded off to the nearest numeric grade.
3. **Periodical Grade**

   Recommended periodical grade components:

   Major Examinations: 30%
   Class Standing: 70%
   Total: 100%
4. **General Weighted Average (GWA)**

   Grades for all subjects prescribed in the curriculum are included in the computation of the GWA. Religious Education and National Service Training Programs subjects are assigned 1.5 units only for purposes of computing the GWA.

   The following are not included in the GWA computation:
   4.1. non-numeric grade such as "INC" (Incomplete) and "DRP" (Dropped)
   4.2. grades earned outside Rogationist College
5. **Computation of the GWA**

   Multiply the Final Grade (FG) by the Number of Units (Un) of each subject prescribed in the curriculum to get the Credit Points per Subject (CPS). Add all of the CPS and divide the sum by the Total Number of Units (TNU).
   5.1. Add the credit points of all the subjects to get the total credit points (TCP)
   5.2. Divide the total credit points by the total number of units of all the subjects, and round off to two (2) decimal places.

   CPS = Un x FG
   TCP = CPS1 + CPS2 + CPS3 + ... + CPSn
   GWA = TCP / TNU

   The subject grade is based on the percentage distribution for each periodical grade to be expressed in percentage form rounded to the nearest unit.
6. **Release of Subject Grades**

   Report of subject grades will be released on the dates specified in the school calendar to students and parents without any pending accountability.
7. **Completion of Incomplete Ratings**

   Incomplete grades incurred in a subject must be completed within one (1) semester from the time the grade was given or within the set period, by the office of the dean, for grade completion.

   Failure to remove an incomplete mark within the allowed period or before the student transfers to another school will automatically result to a failing grade of 5.0 which shall be indicated in the Transcript of Records.

   Students forfeit the right to complete an incomplete grade even within the stipulated period once a Certificate of Transfer is issued to them.

   7.1. **Steps for Completion of Grades**

   The steps to be followed for the completion of grades are as follows:
   7.1.1. The student gets the application for completion from the Registrar's Office.
   7.1.2. The student fills out the form and confers with the faculty member concerned regarding the means of grade completion. In the absence of the faculty member, the student may confer with the Program Chair.

Students of RC are expected to behave as worthy members of the Christian society and of the Rogationist family. **Objectives of the Student Discipline Program**

1. To develop in the students the freedom, social conscience and the commitment to be well-mannered persons.
2. To consolidate the Filipino values of respect and cooperation for the purpose of fostering family-oriented behavior and self-discipline.
3. To foster in the students a deep respect for persons, in obedience to authority, and appreciation of rules.
4. To provide for the development of young people who are confident, disciplined, and self-reliant.
5. To develop in the students the value of productive work, cooperation, and responsible leadership.

## DISCIPLINARY MEASURES

All offenses are subject to imposition of penalties ranging from warning to separation from the school, depending on the gravity and attendant circumstances, whether mitigating or aggravating, as each case may warrant.

Every bona fide student of the Rogationist College is obliged to observe all disciplinary policies of the school. Any violation of such policies shall compel the school to take the necessary disciplinary measures.

All offenses shall be attended to by Office for Student Discipline. Offenses shall be counted on a semestral basis.

Prompt reporting of incident of misbehavior of an RC student is encouraged. This covers the period when said student is officially enrolled in the College/Technical Education department. This is done in order to maximize the intervention, reformation and/or imposition of appropriate disciplinary action/s of the school.

### I. Disciplinary Actions

1. **For Minor Offenses**

   | Minor Offense (Number of Occurrence) | Disciplinary Action |
   |---|---|
   | First | Dialogue with the Program Chair |
   | Second | Dialogue with the Dean or Assistant Dean |
   | Third | Dialogue with the Dean |
   | Fourth | Dialogue with the Discipline Officer and 1 hour of community service (Community Service is a specified hour of service/work to be determined by the Discipline Officer in agreement with the student) |
   | Fifth | Dialogue with the Discipline Officer and 2 hours of community service |
   | Sixth | Dialogue with the Discipline Officer and 4 hours of community service |
   | Seventh | Considered as habitual disregard or willful violation of established policies and regulations (Equivalent to Major offense). Disciplinary Probation for 1 Semester and 8 hours community service |

2. **For Major Offenses (Procedural Due Process Standard)**
   2.1. All complaints shall be in writing, duly signed and filed in the Office for Student Discipline by the aggrieved or by any person having direct knowledge of the commission of act complained. The form of complaint signed by the complainant must be submitted to OSD not later than 3 days after the date of the reported incident. The duly accomplished form must contain the following information:
   2.1.1. The name of the student against whom the complaint is filed; and
   2.1.2. A narration of the pertinent facts and circumstances of the act/s complained of.
   2.2. The Office for Student Discipline through the Discipline Officer conducts its own preliminary inquiry through other concerned persons.
   2.3. A written report shall be forwarded by the Discipline Officer to the Prefect of Discipline, attaching pertinent documents related to the case. Cases falling under Category A of Major Offenses will be decided by the Prefect of Discipline.
   2.4. Cases falling under Categories B & C are to be deliberated by the Discipline Committee headed by the DSS and composed of the Prefect of Discipline, Guidance Counselor, and Program Chair. The DSS shall initiate the hearing/deliberation the cases for Non-admission/Dismissal.

   The following items are to be observed in student disciplinary hearings (Sarmiento, "Students' Rights and Wrongs", 2006, pp. 65-66):
   2.4.1. The student shall be informed in writing of the nature and cause of the accusation against him/her;
   2.4.2. He/She shall have the right to answer the charges against him/her within 3 school days.
   2.4.3. The basic principles of due process shall be in place. He/She shall be informed of the evidence against him/her;
   2.4.4. He/She shall have the right to adduce evidence in his/her own behalf, and
   2.4.5. The evidence must be considered by the investigating committee or official designated by the school authorities to hear and decide the case.
   2.5. The result of the deliberation shall be submitted by the DSS to the Rector for his final decision.
   2.6. The DSS shall conduct conference with the erring student and his/her parents/guardian concerning the final decision.

### II. Probation, Preventive Suspension, Non-admission and Exclusion

In accordance with the provision in the Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, 9th edition, the categories of disciplinary administrative sanctions for serious offenses or violation of school rules and regulations, which may be applied upon an erring student, are:

1. **Disciplinary Probation**

   This means that the student has forfeited the confidence and high esteem of the school authorities and he is required to restore these by definite and manifest acts of attention to duty. Habitual misbehavior and unsatisfactory conduct may lead the Discipline Committee to recommend a student to be placed under disciplinary probation for a specific period of time (i.e. for one semester). If the student is placed on probation for 1 semester, his/her compliance with the school rules will be evaluated at the end of the semester. His/Her failure to show remarkable improvement forfeits his chance to re-enroll the following semester.

   Students with offenses committed in the months of June & July, the Disciplinary Probation shall be automatically given in the current semester while those students with offenses in the months of August - October, the Disciplinary Probation shall be implemented in the next semester.
2. **Preventive Suspension**

   A student under investigation may be preventively suspended from entering the school premises and from attending classes, when the evidence of guilt is strong, and the responsible school official is morally convinced that the continued stay of the student pending investigation would cause sufficient distraction to the normal operations of the institution, or would pose real or imminent threat or danger to persons and property inside the institution's premises.
3. **Non-admission**

   The school reserves the right to deny admission or enrolment of an erring student for the semester/school year following the term when the decision finding the student guilty of the offense charged and imposing the penalty was promulgated.
4. **Exclusion**

   The school reserves the right to exclude or drop the name of the erring student from the roll of students immediately upon resolution for exclusion was promulgated or at any time during the year/semester if he/she fails to give positive evidence of earnestness of purpose and active cooperation in all the requirements of conduct and academic work, or his/her conduct makes the student a liability and nuisance to the school authority, and a bad influence to his/her fellow students. His/Her credentials are readily given to facilitate transfer to another school.

   Some specific cases for exclusion:
   a. organizing, recruiting for, or membership in, a fraternity or sorority which does not promote the vision-mission of the college
   b. hazing
   c. carrying a deadly weapon
   d. sex-related offenses such as sexual violence
   e. selling and/or possession of prohibited drugs
   f. drug dependency
   g. drunkenness
   h. hooliganism
   i. assaulting a student or school personnel
   j. instigating or leading illegal strikes or similar concerted activities resulting in the stoppage of classes
   k. preventing or threatening any student or school personnel from entering the school premises or attending classes or discharging their duties
   l. forging or tampering with school forms or records,
   m. securing or using forged school records, forms and documents
   n. other gross misbehavior judged as such by the Discipline Committee.

# APPENDIX A — MANUAL OF REGULATIONS FOR PRIVATE SCHOOLS

**Section 66. Tuition Charges**

A student who transfers or otherwise withdraws, in writing, within two weeks after the beginning of classes and who has already paid the pertinent tuition and other fees in full or for any length of time longer than one month may be charged ten (10%) percent of the total amount due for the term if he withdraws within the first week of classes, or twenty percent if within the second week of classes, regardless of whether or not he has actually attended classes. The student may be charged all the school fees in full if he withdraws any time after the second week of classes. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to a justifiable reason, the student shall be charged the pertinent fees only up to and including the last month of attendance.

The foregoing tuition fee schedules are based on the premise that a student who enrolls in a school stays enrolled for the entire school year or semester, regardless of his subsequent transfer or withdrawal.

Section 66, MRPS and Section 104, TVET Manual protect schools which accept tuition fee payments on installment — i.e., where a down payment is given upon enrollment and the balance is paid before examinations — by requiring students who transfer or withdraw from the course to first settle their accounts before the school issues them transfer credentials.

It must be stressed that according to the stipulations of Section 62(e) of the MRPS and section 124(e), TVET Manual, a school can legally collect outstanding tuition fees from a student who has transferred or withdrawn only if he was officially enrolled. This means that the student must have at least made an initial payment of his school fees and that the school must have accepted that payment and authorized him to participate in his classes. Without these attending circumstances, there is no legal basis for collection of tuition fee charges, since no contractual relationship between the student and school was established.

A student who has paid in full or for any length longer than one month and transfers or withdraws in the first week after classes begin shall be charged 10% of the total amount due for the term (i.e. one semester for the collegiate level and TVET courses, one year for elementary and secondary levels); if he withdraws in the second week he will be charged 20%; if he withdraws any time after the second week, he may be fully charged for all fees applicable for the entire term. These charges hold whether or not he actually participated in his classes, because attendance is not the issue; what matters is that the school actually authorized him to attend them.

For purposes of determination, the day of the student's submission of his written notice of withdrawal shall be considered the date of withdrawal. Verbal notice is not enough.

If the tuition fees, registration, library and laboratory fees already paid equal the amount still to be paid for the first month (or less) in a monthly installment scheme, no refund shall be given to the student when he withdraws after the registration period. However, if the transfer or withdrawal is due to justifiable cause, the student shall only be charged the pertinent fees up to the last month of attendance.

An existing DepEd ruling states that any promissory note signed in favor of a school by a student below 18 years old is null and void. It does not constitute the "payment of some kind" needed to establish enrollment unless a responsible relative or legal guardian acts as surety. The reason for this is that in law, a person below 18 years old is still a minor and is not legally competent to contract such a financial obligation. Yet even if the student were of majority age but still dependent on the parents or a guardian for support, the DepEd would not consider him competent to sign a promissory note. Therefore, such a note would likewise be considered null and void unless a responsible relative or guardian appears as surety.

Although the MRPS and the TVET Manual no longer provide for this, it is submitted that students are entitled to a full refund of fees for any course or subject that has been discontinued by the school, or which was not credited by the DepEd, CHED or TESDA.

In order to protect the rights of individuals in education institution, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, on May 14, 2012 otherwise known as "DepEd Child Protection Policy" with respect to Policy and Guidelines on Protecting Children in School from Abuse, Violence, Exploitation, Discrimination, Bullying and other Forms of Abuse, which Rogationist College hereby adopts in toto.

It is the objective of Rogationist College to provide not only good and quality education but also to inculcate the values and teachings of its founder, Fr. However, considering the effect on the welfare of the child or employment of individuals complaint, Rogationist College will undertake to consider all aspects of the complaint and all proceedings shall be treated with strict confidentially.

**Section 4.** Rogationist College shall consider due process in handling cases involving sexual harassment to protect not only the rights of the complainant but also of the person being charged, without prejudice to complying with the reportorial requirement mandated by Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, issued by the Department of Education and separate criminal cases that may be instituted.

**Definition of Terms**

a. **Child** - refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age or those over but are unable to fully take care of themselves from abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation or discrimination because of physical or mental disability or condition, including all students who are above 18 years of age but are enrolled in Rogationist College

   b.1. school head which refers to the chief executive officer or administrator of a public or private school or learning center

   c.1. In a work-related or employment environment, sexual harassment is committed when:
   (1) The sexual favor is made as a condition in the hiring or in the employment, re-employment or continued employment of said individual, or in granting said individual favorable compensation, terms of conditions, promotions, or privileges; or the refusal to grant the sexual favor results in limiting, segregating or classifying the employee which in any way would discriminate, deprive or diminish employment opportunities or otherwise adversely affect said employee;
   (2) The above acts would impair the employee's rights or privileges under existing labor laws; or
   (3) The above acts would result in an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment for the employee.
   c.2. In an education or training environment, sexual harassment is committed:
   (1) Against one who is under the care, custody or supervision of the offender;
   (2) Against one whose education, training, apprenticeship or tutorship is entrusted to the offender;
   (3) When the sexual favor is made a condition to the giving of a passing grade, or the granting of honors and scholarships, or the payment of a stipend, allowance or other benefits, privileges, or consideration; or
   (4) When the sexual advances result in an intimidating, hostile or offensive environment for the student, trainee or apprentice.

   Any person who directs or induces another to commit any act of sexual harassment as herein defined, or who cooperates in the commission thereof by another without which it would not have been committed, shall also be held liable under this Act.
d. **Sexual Violence** - refers to acts which are sexual in nature. It includes but not limited to:
   d.1. rape, sexual harassment, acts of lasciviousness, making demeaning and sexually suggestive remarks, physically attacking the sexual parts of the victims' body
   d.2. forcing the child to watch obscene publications and indecent shows or forcing the child to do indecent sexual acts and/or to engage or be involved in, the creation or distribution of such films, indecent publication or material
   d.3. acts causing or attempting to cause the child to engage in any sexual activity by force, threat of force, physical or other harm or threat of physical or other harm or coercion, or through inducements, gifts or favour.
   d.4. other acts of sexual nature that are prejudicial to the interest of the students and personnel.

**Section 5. Procedures for Filing a Complaint with the Discipline and Grievance Committee**

1. A written complaint shall be filed as follows:
   a. by the complainant himself to the Office of the Rector, if the complainant is a personnel of the school or is above 18 years of age.
   b. In the event that the complainant or aggrieved party is a child, the written incident report submitted by any student, personnel, parent or guardian or guidance counselor who has knowledge of the incident shall be treated as a complaint which incident report shall be immediately submitted to the Office of the Rector.
   c. By the complainant, though minor, but with the conformity by parent, guardian, teacher or guidance counselor.
2. After receipt of the complaint, the Office of the Rector has the authority to call the parties to the case for possible amicable settlement.

   In the event that there was failure of settlement, the Office of the Rector shall call for the convocation of the grievance committee, composed of the following:

   Chairman: Director of Student & Personnel Services
   Members: Director of Spiritual Affairs, Dean/TED Head/Principal, Coordinator for Guidance & Counseling Office, HRD (when a school employee is involved), Prefect of Discipline (when a student is involved)

   The Complaint/incident report shall be endorsed to the chairman of the grievance.
3. After the convocation of the Grievance Committee, the chairman shall send a copy of the complaint/incident report to the respondent/s who shall be given five (5) working days to answer in writing the complaint filed against him/them, informing of his/her right to the services of counsel in the preparation of the Answer.
4. If after five (5) days from receipt of the complaint/incident report and the respondent/s failed to answer, the same shall be treated as a waiver of his right to file an Answer and the Committee shall proceed with the case ex-parte.
5. However, should the respondent fail to reply within the prescribed period due to justifiable reasons, a non-extendable period of three (3) days shall be granted.
6. The Chair shall act as the presiding officer during the hearing. He shall commence the proceedings by reading the complaint/s and reply/ies filed before the Committee.
7. In the presentation of the evidence, the parties shall be given the rights to present their respective evidence and witness before the body. Witnesses are subject to cross-examination by the Committee but not by the other party.
8. In the event that in the course of the investigation, a new witness filed a statement in support of the complaint, the respondent shall be given a period of five (5) days within which to file any answer to rebut or refute the claim, which answer shall be forwarded to the complainant.
9. In the presentation of evidence, the complainant shall first present his/her side followed by the respondent/s.
10. The Committee shall submit its findings and recommendations to the Rector, who in turn will submit the same to the House Council for final decision.
11. In all cases, the decisions of the House Council shall be final and the same shall executed by the Rector.

**Section 6. Penalty.** The penalty of reprimand, suspension or termination may be imposed based on the gravity of the charge complained of.

**Section 7. Confidentiality clause.** All proceedings and records of the case shall be treated with outmost confidentiality especially the identity of the victim, if he/she a minor.

**Section 8. Reportorial Requirement of Department of Education.** Report concerning the case must be prepared by the Human Resource Development and Management Office if it involves school personnel. For cases involving student/s, it is the duty of the Director for Student and Personnel Services to prepare the report to Department of Education.

# APPENDIX H — RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES OF BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE

**KNOW ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS:**

Whereas, Rogationist College as a Catholic School acknowledges its obligations to exercise reasonable supervision over the conduct of the student and to cultivate the best potentialities of the heart and mind of the students by committing to the formation of its members towards love of God and neighbor and promotion of proper decorum, good behavior and positive values;

Whereas, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, on May 14, 2012 otherwise known as "DepEd Child Protection Policy" with respect to Policy and Guidelines on Protecting Children in School from Abuse, Violence, Exploitation, Discrimination, Bullying and other Forms of Abuse;

Whereas, in line with the mandate of curtailing bullying, inside and outside of the school, REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10627, or the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013", was passed to address growing concerns of bullying, and Rogationist College is one in promoting zero-tolerance on bullying;

Whereas, Rogationist College in order to comply with the different laws, rules and regulation in relation to bullying formulated its own rules of procedure in addressing the complaint on bullying;

Thus the foregoing rule is hereby adopted.

**Section 1. Short Title**

This shall be known as "RULES AND REGULATIONS IN RELATION TO CASES INVOLVING BULLYING IN ROGATIONIST COLLEGE"

**Section 2. State Policy**

It is the mandate of the 1987 the Philippine Constitution for educational institutions to inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, respect for human rights, appreciation of the roles of national heroes in the historical development of the country, teach the rights and duties of citizenship, strengthen ethical and spiritual values, develop moral character and personal discipline, encourage critical and creative thinking, broaden scientific and technical knowledge, and promote vocational efficiency (Article XIV, Section 3 (2));

In order to protect the rights of individuals in education institution, the Department of Education issued Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, on May 14, 2012 otherwise known as "DepEd Child Protection Policy" with respect to Policy and Guidelines on Protecting Children in School from Abuse, Violence, Exploitation, Discrimination, Bullying and other Forms of Abuse, as well as Republic Act No. 10627, or the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013", which Rogationist College hereby adopts in toto.

**Section 3. School Policy**

It is the objective of Rogationist College to provide not only good and quality education but also to inculcate the values and teachings of its founder, St. Hannibal Mary di Francia, in relation to morals, proper decorum and good behaviour.

It is the policy of Rogationist College to free its environment from all acts of bullying and condemns any act pertinent or related thereto for it is highly unacceptable behaviour, contrary to law and morals.

However, considering the effect/s on the welfare of the child and employment of individuals, Rogationist College undertakes to consider all aspects of the complaint and all proceedings shall be treated with strict confidentiality.

Rogationist College shall consider due process in handling cases involving bullying to protect not only the rights of the complainant but also of the person being charged, without prejudice to complying with the reportorial requirement mandated by Department Order No. 40, Series of 2012, issued by the Department of Education and separate criminal cases that may be instituted.

**Section 4. Definition of Terms**

"Bullying" refers to any severe, or repeated use by one or more students of a written, verbal or electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at another student that has the effect of actually causing or placing the latter in reasonable fear of physical or emotional harm or damage to his property; creating a hostile environment at school for the other student; infringing on the rights of another student at school; or materially and substantially disrupting the education process or the orderly operation of a school;

"Bullied" or "Victim" - refers to any student who experiences the acts of bullying or retaliation as defined by the Act or this IRR.

"Bystander" - refers to any person who witnesses or has personal knowledge of any actual or perceived acts or incidents of bullying or retaliation as defined by this IRR.

"Learning center" - refers to learning resources and facilities of a learning program for out-of-school youth and adults as defined in DepEd Order No. 43, s. 2013.

"Service provider" - refers to any person who is not a teacher or school personnel but who works in the school, such as, but not limited to, service personnel, security guards, canteen personnel, utility workers, and transportation

"Student" - refers to a person who attends classes in any level of basic education, and includes a pupil or learner as defined in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012.

"Prompt Report" - refers to prompt reporting of incident of bullying from June 1 of the current year until March 31 of the following year in order to maximize the intervention, reformation and/or imposition of appropriate sanction/s of the school.

**Section 5. Forms of Bullying**, such as, but not limited to, the following:

1. Any unwanted physical contact between the bully and the victim like punching, pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, inflicting school pranks, teasing, fighting and the use of available objects as weapons;
2. Any act that causes damage to a victim's psyche and/or emotional well-being;
3. Any slanderous statement or accusation that causes the victim undue emotional distress like directing foul language or profanity at the target, name-calling, tormenting and commenting negatively on victim's looks, clothes and body;
4. Cyber-bullying or any bullying done through the use of technology or any electronic means. The term shall also include any conduct resulting to harassment, intimidation, or humiliation, through the use of other forms of technology, such as, but not limited to texting, email, instant messaging, chatting, internet, social media, online games, or other platforms or formats as defined in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012; Bullying at a location, activity, function or program that is not school-related and through the use of technology or an electronic device or other forms of media that is not owned, leased or used by a school;
5. Social bullying or any deliberate, repetitive and aggressive social behavior intended to hurt others or to belittle another individual or group.
6. Gender-based bullying or any act that humiliates or excludes a person on the basis of perceived or actual sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI).
7. Retaliation against a person who reports bullying, who provides information during an investigation of bullying, or who is a witness to or has reliable information about bullying.

**Section 6. Place of Commission of bullying**

Bullying can be committed on the following places:

a. school grounds;
b. property immediately adjacent to school grounds;
c. school-sponsored or school-related activities, functions or programs whether on or off school grounds;
d. school bus stops;
e. school buses or other vehicles owned, leased or used by a school; or
f. school buses or school services privately-owned but accredited by the school.

**Section 7. Procedure**

**A. Immediate Responses**

1. A victim or a bystander, or a school personnel who receives information of a bullying incident or retaliation, or any person who witnesses or has personal knowledge of any incident of bullying or retaliation, shall report the same to the Prefect of Discipline or Guidance Counselor.
2. The school personnel who was notified of a bullying incident or retaliation shall intervene, by:
   i. Stopping the bullying or retaliation immediately;
   ii. Separating the students involved;
   iii. Removing the victim or, in appropriate cases, the bully or offending student, from the site;
   iv. Ensuring the victim's safety, by: Determining and addressing the victim's immediate safety needs; and Ensuring medical attention, if needed, and securing a medical certificate, in cases of physical injury. Bringing the bully to the Guidance Office or Office for Student Discipline.

**B. Reporting the Bullying Incident or Retaliation**

A written complaint shall be filed by any of the following to the Office for Student Discipline:

1. by the complainant/victim himself if the complainant is a personnel of the school or is above 18 years of age;
2. In the event that the complainant or aggrieved party is a child, the written incident report submitted by any student, personnel, parent or guardian or guidance counsellor or anyone who has knowledge of the incident shall be treated as a complaint which incident report shall be immediately submitted to the Office for Student Discipline; or
3. By the complainant, though minor, with the consent of the teacher, parent, legal guardian or guidance counselor

The Prefect of Discipline who receives the report or information regarding a bullying incident or retaliation shall ask the victim or the bystander, or the school personnel who witnessed or has personal knowledge of the incident of bullying or retaliation, shall be asked to fill up the Intake Sheet as provided in DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012. The Prefect of Discipline shall inform the Principal and Rector of all reports of incident of bullying received by the Office and of the actions taken in order to respond to the report on bullying.

**C. Fact-Finding and Documentation**

The Prefect of Discipline who receives the report or information regarding a bullying incident or retaliation shall perform the following:

1. Separately interviews in private the offending student/s, the victim/s and all other individuals who hold vital information regarding the parties or incidents. During the interview, students shall be asked to write down their account of the incident in the presence of their respective homeroom adviser or guidance facilitator/counselor.
2. Determines the levels of threats and develop intervention strategies. If the alleged bullying incident or retaliation or the situation requires immediate attention or intervention, or the level of threat is high, appropriate action shall be taken by the school within twenty-four hours (24) from the time of the incident.
3. Informs the parents/legal guardian of the offending student and the victim of the bullying incident and personally confers the parents/legal guardian of students involved to resolve any issue and to restore, as far as practicable, the relationships of the parties involved.
4. Carefully and thoroughly studies the case to determine the appropriate interventions to be provided to the victim and the offending student or disciplinary measure to be imposed to the offending student.
5. Submits to the Director of Student Support Services a comprehensive report on the result of interview or verification held with the students involved, conference made with the students' parents/legal guardian and the appropriate interventions to be provided to the victim and the offending student or disciplinary measure to be imposed to the offending student.
6. Makes appropriate referral to the Child Protection Committee if the issue on bullying is not resolved among the parties or for purpose of further fact-finding.

**D. Due Process**

1. The Director for Student Support Services shall call for the convocation of the Child Protection Committee, composed of the following:
   1. Director for Student Support Services - Chairperson
   2. Guidance Counselor/Teacher - Vice Chairperson
   3. Representative of the Teachers as designated by the Faculty Members
   4. President of the Parents Teacher Association
   5. President of the Student Body Organization
2. The offending student/s and the parent/s or legal guardian/s shall be informed of the complaint in writing.
3. The students shall be given the opportunity to answer the complaint in writing within the school ground in the presence of the Child Protection Committee members, with the assistance of the parents or legal guardian.
4. As a rule, the period to reply shall not be extendable except for justifiable reasons. Said extension, if granted, shall not exceed three (3) calendar days.
5. In case of contradicting statements of material value, parties may be called by the Child Protection Committee and be subjected to clarificatory questioning by the Child Protection Committee members. Questions can only be propounded by the Child Protection Committee and not by any of the parties.
6. The Committee shall submit its findings and recommendations to the Office of the Rector for the decision and/or imposition of appropriate sanction, if any.
7. Parents can make an appeal to the School Board within 10 calendar days from receipt of the decision. Pending the appeal, intervention program, but not disciplinary action, shall commence.
8. The decision of the School Board shall be final.

**E. Referral**

The Rector or the Child Protection Committee may refer the victim/s and the bully/ies to trained professionals outside the school, such as social workers, psychologists, or child protection specialists, for further assessment and appropriate intervention measures, as may be necessary. The School Head or the designated school personnel shall notify the Women and Children's Protection Desk (WPCD) of the local Philippine National Police, if he/she believes that appropriate criminal charges may be pursued against the bully or offending student.

**Section 8. False Accusation of Bullying**

If the student, after an investigation, is found to have knowingly made a false accusation of bullying, the said student shall be subjected to disciplinary actions or to appropriate interventions in accordance with the existing rules and regulations of the Department or the private school.

**Section 9. Incident of Bullying or Retaliation Involving Students from More than One School**

If an incident of bullying or retaliation involves students from more than one school, the school that was first informed of the bullying or retaliation shall promptly notify the appropriate administrator or school head of the other school so that both schools may take appropriate action.

**Section 10. Anonymous Reporting**

Reports of incidents of bullying or retaliation initiated by persons who prefer anonymity shall be entertained, and the person who reported the incident shall be afforded protection from possible retaliation; provided, however, that no disciplinary administrative action shall be taken against an alleged bully or offending student solely on the basis of an anonymous report and without any other evidence. Moreover, it should be made clear to the person who made the report that when they report incidents of bullying they are giving factual and truthful information.

**Section 11. Role/Duty of Child Protection Committee.**

In addition to their duties and responsibilities provided by DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012, the Child Protection Committee shall perform the following tasks:

a. Conducts awareness-raising programs with school stakeholders in preventing and addressing bullying;
b. Ensures that the anti-bullying policy adopted by the school is implemented;
c. Monitors all cases or incidents related to bullying reported or referred by the teacher, guidance counselor or coordinator or any person designated to handle prevention and intervention measures mentioned by the preceding sections of this IRR; and
d. Makes the necessary referrals to appropriate agencies, offices or persons, as may be required by the circumstances.

**Section 12. Penalty**

Written reprimand, community service, suspension, exclusion or expulsion, in accordance with existing rules and regulations of the school or of the Department for public schools, may be imposed, if the circumstances warrant the imposition of such penalty, provided that the requirements of due process are complied with.

In addition to the disciplinary sanction, the bully shall also be required to undergo an intervention program which shall be administered or supervised by the school's Child Protection Committee. The parents of the bully shall be encouraged to join the intervention program.

**Section 13. Jurisdiction**

Complaints of bullying and other acts of abuse under DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012 shall be within the exclusive jurisdiction of Rogationist College, and shall not be brought for amicable settlement before the Barangay, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations. Complaints for acts committed by persons not under the jurisdiction of the Disciplinary Authority of Rogationist College shall be referred to the appropriate authorities.

**Section 14. Confidentiality**

In bullying or peer abuse and other acts of abuse by a student, the identity or other information that may reasonably identify the student, whether victim or offender, shall be withheld from the public to protect his or her privacy.

**Section 15. Criminal and Civil Liability**

Criminal and civil liability arising from bullying and other acts of abuse are separate and distinct, and shall not be a bar to the filing of an administrative case under these guidelines.

If the bullying incident or retaliation resulted in serious physical injuries or death, the case shall be dealt with in accordance with the provisions of Republic Act 9344 or the "Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act", as amended, and its Implementing Rules and Regulations, in connection with other applicable laws, as may be warranted by the circumstances attendant to the bullying incident.

**Section 16. Repealing clause**

All rules and regulations inconsistent to the provision of the foregoing act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

**Section 17. Effectivity**

This rules and regulations shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication by posting copies thereof in the school bulletin board or its publication in any school authorized publications, whichever comes first.

# APPENDIX I — ANTI-HAZING LAW OF THE REPUBLIC OF THE PHILIPPINES

**Republic Act No. 8049**

*An Act Regulating Hazing and Other Forms of Initiation Rites in Fraternities, Sororities and Organizations and Providing Penalties Therefore.*

Be enacted by Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

**Section 1.** Hazing, as used in this Act, is an initiation rite or practice as a prerequisite for admission into membership in a fraternity, sorority or organization by placing the recruit, neophyte or applicant in some embarrassing or humiliating situations such as forcing him to do menial, silly, foolish and other similar tasks or activities or otherwise subjecting him to physical or psychological suffering or injury.

The term "organization" shall include any club or the Armed Forces of the Philippines, Philippine National Police, Philippine Military Academy, or officer and cadet corp of the Citizen's Military Training and Citizen's Army Training. The physical, mental and psychological testing and training procedure and practices to determine and enhance the physical, mental and psychological fitness of prospective regular members of the Armed Forces of the Philippines and the Philippine National Police as approved by the Secretary of National Defense and the National Police Commission duly recommended by the Chief of Staff, Armed Forces of the Philippines and the Director General of the Philippine National Police shall not be considered as hazing for the purposes of this Act.

**Section 2.** No hazing or initiation rites in any form or manner by a fraternity, sorority or organization shall be allowed without prior written notice to the school authorities or head of organization seven (7) days before the conduct of such initiation. The written notice shall indicate the period of the initiation activities which shall not exceed three (3) days, shall include the names of those to be subjected to such activities, and shall further contain an undertaking that no physical violence be employed by anybody during such initiation rites.

**Section 3.** The head of the school or organization or their representatives must assign at least two (2) representatives of the school or organization, as the case may be, to be present during the initiation. It is the duty of such representative to see to it that no physical harm of any kind shall be inflicted upon a recruit, neophyte or applicant.

**Section 4.** If the person subjected to hazing or other forms of initiation rites suffers any physical injury or dies as a result thereof, the officers and members of the fraternity, sorority or organization who actually participated in the infliction of physical harm shall be liable as principals. The person or persons who participated in the hazing shall suffer:

1. The penalty of reclusion perpetua (life imprisonment) if death, rape, sodomy or mutilation results there from.
2. The penalty of reclusion temporal in its maximum period (17 years, 4 months and 1 day to 20 years) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall become insane, imbecile, impotent or blind.
3. The penalty of reclusion temporal in its medium period (14 years, 8 months and one day to 17 years and 4 months) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall have lost the use of speech or the power to hear or to smell, or shall have lost an eye, a hand, a foot, an arm or a leg or shall have lost the use of any such member shall have become incapacitated for the activity or work in which he was habitually engaged.
4. The penalty of reclusion temporal in its minimum period (12 years and one day to 14 years and 8 months) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall become deformed or shall have lost any other part of his body, or shall have lost the use thereof, or shall have been ill or incapacitated for the performance on the activity or work in which he was habitually engaged for a period of more than ninety (90) days.
5. The penalty of prison mayor in its maximum period (10 years and one day to 12 years) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall have been ill or incapacitated for the performance on the activity or work in which he was habitually engaged for a period of more than thirty (30) days.
6. The penalty of prison mayor in its medium period (8 years and one day to 10 years) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall have been ill or incapacitated for the performance on the activity or work in which he was habitually engaged for a period of ten (10) days or more, or that the injury sustained shall require medical assistance for the same period.
7. The penalty of prison mayor in its minimum period (6 years and one day to 8 years) if in consequence of the hazing the victim shall have been ill or incapacitated for the performance on the activity or work in which he was habitually engaged from one (1) to nine (9) days, or that the injury sustained shall require medical assistance for the same period.
8. The penalty of prison correccional in its maximum period (4 years, 2 months and one day to 6 years) if in consequence of the hazing the victim sustained physical injuries which do not prevent him from engaging in his habitual activity or work nor require medical attendance.

The responsible officials of the school or of the police, military or citizen's army training organization, may impose the appropriate administrative sanctions on the person or the persons charged under this provision even before their conviction. The maximum penalty herein provided shall be imposed in any of the following instances:

(a) when the recruitment is accompanied by force, violence, threat, intimidation or deceit on the person of the recruit who refuses to join;
(b) when the recruit, neophyte or applicant initially consents to join but upon learning that hazing will be committed on his person, is prevented from quitting;
(c) when the recruit, neophyte or applicant having undergone hazing is prevented from reporting the unlawful act to his parents or guardians, to the proper school authorities, or to the police authorities, through force, violence, threat or intimidation;
(d) when the hazing is committed outside of the school or institution; or
(e) when the victim is below twelve (12) years of age at the time of the hazing.

The owner of the place where hazing is conducted shall be liable as an accomplice, when he has actual knowledge of the hazing conducted therein but failed to take any action to prevent the same from occurring. If the hazing is held in the home of one of the officers or members of the fraternity, group, or organization, the parents shall be held liable as principals when they have actual knowledge of the hazing conducted therein but failed to take any action to prevent the same from occurring.

The school authorities including faculty members who consent to the hazing or who have actual knowledge thereof, but failed to take any action to prevent the same from occurring shall be punished as accomplices for the acts of hazing committed by the perpetrators.

The officers, former officers, or alumni of the organization, group, fraternity or sorority who actually planned the hazing although not present when the acts constituting the hazing were committed shall be liable as principals. A fraternity or sorority's adviser who is present when the acts constituting the hazing were committed and failed to take action to prevent the same from occurring shall be liable as principal.

The presence of any person during the hazing is prima facie evidence of participation therein as principal unless he prevented the commission of the acts punishable herein.

Any person charged under this provision shall not be entitled to the mitigating circumstance that there was no intention to commit so grave a wrong.

This section shall apply to the president, manager, director or other responsible officer of a corporation engaged in hazing as a requirement for employment in the manner provided herein.

**Section 5.** If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other parts or provisions thereof shall remain valid and effective.

**Section 6.** All laws, orders, rules or regulations which are inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby amended or repealed accordingly.

**Section 7.** This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) calendar days after its publication in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.

Approved; June 07, 1995

(Signed) Fidel V. Ramos, President of the Philippines

# APPENDIX J — DANGEROUS DRUGS BOARD REGULATION NO. 6, Series of 2003

Republic of the Philippines - Office of the President

**DANGEROUS DRUGS BOARD**

**BOARD REGULATION NO. 6, Series of 2003**

**SUBJECT:** General Guidelines For The Conduct Of Random Drug Testing For Secondary And Tertiary Students

Pursuant to Section 36 (c), Article III of Republic Act No. 9165, the following guidelines are hereby promulgated.

The guidelines shall be applicable to the random drug testing of students in public and private secondary, tertiary/higher education institutions and post-secondary technical vocational schools. These guidelines outline the purposes of the random drug-testing program, as well as procedures and necessary consequences of a positive drug test result after confirmation.

All procedures undertaken shall take into account the ideals of fairness and rehabilitation and not isolation of the drug dependent. The school must not violate the constitutional rights to due process, equal protection and self-incrimination.

### A. Guiding Principles in the Implementation of Random Drug Testing in Schools and Management of Drug Test Results

1. Government recognizes the primary responsibility of the family, particularly the parents for the education and awareness of its members of the ill effects of dangerous drugs.
2. Parental involvement shall be maximized in the implementation of drug education, random drug testing, treatment and rehabilitation of drug users and dependents.
3. The school, with the assistance of Local Government Units (LGUs) and other agencies where the school is located, has the obligation to employ every reasonable means to provide a healthy and drug-free environment to its populace.
4. Cognizant of the right of the students to continue and complete their studies, the government and the schools shall give emphasis to the implementation of measures aimed at guidance and counseling together with the treatment and rehabilitation of any student found to have used or to be dependent on dangerous drugs.
5. Academic freedom of institutions of higher learning shall be respected in the implementation of random drug testing and all other pertinent provisions of RA 9165.
6. The implementation of drug abuse prevention and education programs in schools shall be intensified as an integral part of the over-all demand reduction efforts of the government.
7. The random drug testing shall be implemented as a collaborative undertaking of the government, the schools, the students and their parents. The whole process shall not in any manner be utilized to harass the students.
8. Random drug testing shall be implemented primarily for prevention and rehabilitation.
9. The drug-testing program shall guarantee and respect the personal privacy and dignity of the student.
10. The drug test results shall be treated with utmost confidentiality.
11. The test results shall not be used in any criminal proceedings.

### B. Purposes of Random Drug Testing

1. To determine the prevalence of drug users among the students
2. To assess the effectivity of school-based and community-based prevention programs
3. To deter the use of illegal drugs
4. To facilitate the rehabilitation of drug users and dependents
5. To strengthen the collaboration efforts of identified agencies against the use of illegal drugs and in the rehabilitation of drug users and dependents.

### C. Definition of Terms

**"Drug Counselor"** shall mean a person trained in the techniques of guidance counseling particularly dealing with cases of drug dependency. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall designate such person.

**"Drug Testing Coordinator"** shall be the point person in the school tasked with handling random drug testing which shall be the principal of a secondary school, the administrator of a technical vocational education and training institution or the administrator appointed by the president/chief executive officer in tertiary institutions.

**"Parents"** shall, for purposes of these guidelines, include court appointed guardians.

**"Random selection"** refers to the unbiased process of selecting students who are to undergo drug testing.

**"Rehabilitation"** is the dynamic process, including after-care and follow-up to treatment, directed towards the physical, emotional/psychological, vocational, social and spiritual change/enhancement of a drug dependent to enable him/her to live without dangerous drugs, enjoy the fullest life compatible with his/her capabilities and potentials and render him/her to become a law-abiding and productive member of the community.

**"Schools"** shall mean an institution that has as its primary purpose the education of students including secondary, tertiary and technical vocational education and training institutions.

**"Selection Board"** shall be the board constituted at the level of the school composed of the Drug Testing Coordinator as chairperson, one representative each from the students, faculty and parents as members. The authorized governing body duly recognized by their respective constituents shall choose the representatives from these stakeholders based on a set of selection criteria formulated for this purpose. In the absence of a parents' association, the School Head may appoint any parent who shall be a member of the Selection Board.

**"Supervising Agency"** shall refer to the government agency that exercises supervision over the school such as the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), or the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA).

### D. Procedures in the Conduct of Random Drug Testing

1. **Notification**
   a. The Supervising Agency through an appropriate order that includes these guidelines on random drug testing, shall inform all schools under its supervision about the government's actions against illegal drugs. The schools' administration shall be required to explain these provisions and their procedures to the school community and when applicable, include these in the schools' handbook or listing of procedures. (The Supervising Agencies' Memorandum Circulars should include a list of DOH-accredited Testing Centers/Laboratories.)
   c. All students and their parents shall be notified in writing on the process and manner by which the random drug testing shall be conducted. Such notification may be sent at any time during the school term. Failure to return the acknowledgment receipt shall not be a bar to the conduct of the drug testing.
2. **Samples**
   a. The Supervising Agency shall inform randomly selected schools on their inclusion in the random drug testing program.
   b. The whole student population of the school selected shall be included in the random sampling.
   c. The number of samples should yield a statistical 95% confidence level for the whole student population
3. **Selection of Samples**
   a. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall convene the Selection Board within five days from the receipt of notice from the Supervising Agency stating that the school is included in the program.
   b. On the day of the testing, the Selection Board shall conduct the random selection of those to be tested.
   c. The Selection Board shall ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the random selection process.
   d. The selection process shall be random through a lottery, which may be computerized, or in any other manner that shall be agreed upon by the Board
   e. The random selection of students and the drug testing shall be done on the same day.
   f. Prior to testing, the selected students shall be asked to reveal the prescription medicines, vitamins, food supplements that they had ingested within the past five (5) days. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall keep the listing and utilize this in the evaluation of the confirmatory drug test.
   g. The laboratory shall follow the DOH prescribed guidelines in the collection of urine specimens. Universal precautions shall be observed at all times. DOH Prescribed Guidelines shall be posted in strategic places/visible areas of the school.
   h. The monitor assigned to ensure the integrity of the collection process should be of the same sex as the student.
   i. The drug testing shall be done in the school and conducted by a duly accredited drug-testing laboratory. The school, through its respective health personnel, shall assist the Drug Testing Laboratory in the conduct of the drug testing.
   j. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the random drug testing for the students, teachers, administration and personnel of the school. It is strongly recommended that the drug testing for students, the teachers, administration and personnel be done simultaneously.
4. **Treatment of Random Drug Test Results**
   a. The results of the test shall be strictly confidential. No school shall publish or post results whether positive or negative.
   b. Any person who violates the rules of confidentiality of the results and selection shall be liable under Section 72 of RA 9165 and such other appropriate laws.
   c. The laboratory shall place the drug test result in a sealed envelope and deliver the same via personal service to the Drug Testing Coordinator. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall then inform all the students tested individually of the test results.
   d. In case the test results are positive, the Drug Testing Coordinator shall inform both the student and parent concerned that a confirmatory test shall be conducted. The student shall be told to inform his/her parents of the scheduled conference with the Drug Testing Coordinator. The student shall be advised to refrain from revealing the test results to other persons.
   e. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall give to the parents full information on the process that shall be undertaken for the confirmatory test. In the event that the parents do not appear on the scheduled conference, the student shall be informed of the schedule of the confirmatory test.
   f. The confirmatory drug test shall be conducted in the same manner as the initial drug test.
   g. The results of the confirmatory test shall be transmitted by the laboratory in a sealed envelope and handed directly to the Drug Testing Coordinator.
   h. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall inform both the parents and the student of the results of the test.
   i. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall not delegate such task of informing the student and parent to any other person, nor shall the Drug Testing Coordinator reveal the results of the test to any person other than the student and parent.
   j. First time positive confirmatory drug test result shall not be a ground for expulsion or any disciplinary action against the student.
   k. The Drug Testing Coordinator shall refer the student and his/her parent to government-owned DOH-accredited facility or DOH-accredited government physician to determine the student's dependency level.
   l. The student may opt for a private DOH-accredited facility or physician for this initial determination provided it is at his/her own expense.
   m. In the event that it is determined that the student is a drug dependent, the school may impose the appropriate sanctions against the student as provided for in the school's Student Handbook and the Manual of Regulations for Private Schools, provided that in the case of public secondary schools, if the student is later on found to have been rehabilitated, the student shall then be allowed to re-enroll.
   n. The student shall then undergo a three (3) month observation and counseling period under the supervision of the DOH-accredited facility or physician in consultation with the parent. Such process of observation and counseling shall be done in coordination with the Drug Counselor of the school.
   o. At the end of the three months, it is hoped that with the counseling done, the student will be properly rehabilitated. If student shows no signs of improvement, recovery or fails the drug test the second time, the DOH-accredited facility or physician, may make a recommendation to the student, parent, and Drug Testing Coordinator to have the student referred to a DOH-accredited facility suited to the student's level of dependency. If another drug testing is conducted for another period on the same student population, and the student is found positive the second time, the school shall proceed in accordance with Section 61, R.A. 9165. The parent and the student may choose to enroll the student in a private rehabilitation center or program or opt to avail of the rehabilitation services of the government through a DOH-accredited facility. If the parents refuse to act, the school shall proceed in accordance to Sec. 61 of RA 9165 without prejudice to the provision of Section 73, RA 9165.

### E. Reportorial Requirements of Results of the Random Drug Testing

1. The Drug Testing Coordinator, Drug Counselor and employees of DOH-accredited facilities, testing laboratories, shall not reveal the names of the students or test results to any other persons except to the student concerned or his/her parents.
2. The aggregate test results from each school which shall not include the identities of the students tested, shall be submitted by the School Head to the Division Superintendent of DepEd for secondary schools, the Regional Director of CHED for tertiary schools and Training Institution Administrator for TESDA for consolidation for the purpose of evaluating the efficacy and effectiveness of drug abuse prevention programs.

### F. Training of Guidance Counselors

The Department of Education, Commission on Higher Education, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency and Dangerous Drugs Board in coordination with each other, shall formulate and conduct the training program for guidance counselors for the purpose of enhancing their skills in handling drug abuse prevention programs and handling drug dependency cases.

### G. Expenses of the Program

The Department of Health, in coordination with the Supervising Agencies shall designate the drug testing laboratories that shall be utilized for purposes of the program.

Payment of testing fees shall be done by the government thru Department of Health to the Drug Testing Laboratories.

### H. Enforcement of Compliance

Students who refuse to undergo random drug testing shall be dealt with in accordance with the rules and regulations of the schools; provided that at no time shall refusal to undergo testing give rise to a presumption of drug use or dependency; provided further that the school may impose sanctions on such refusal other than the offense of drug use or dependency. Schools that refuse to implement the random drug testing program shall be liable under Section 32 of RA 9165 without prejudice to other administrative sanctions imposed by the Supervising Agencies. The Supervising Agency shall report the same to the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA) and the Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB).

### I. Miscellaneous Provisions

**Separability clause.** If any provision of these Guidelines or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held to be invalid, the other provisions of these Guidelines and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby.

**Effectivity.** These Guidelines shall take effect immediately after its approval by the Dangerous Drugs Board.
